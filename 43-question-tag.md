# Summary: Question Tags in English

## 1. What Is a Question Tag?

A **question tag** is a short question added to the end of a statement.

It is usually used to:

- **Check information**
- **Ask for confirmation**
- **Invite agreement**
- Make conversation sound more **natural and friendly**

### Example

> You are tired, **aren't you?**

The statement is positive, so the tag is negative.

---

## 2. Basic Rule

The most common rule is:

> **Positive statement → Negative tag**  
> **Negative statement → Positive tag**

| **Statement** | **Question Tag** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Negative | You are ready, **aren't you?** |
| **Negative** | Positive | You aren't ready, **are you?** |

### ⭐ Easy Pattern

> **Positive → Negative**

> She is happy, **isn't she?**

> **Negative → Positive**

> She isn't happy, **is she?**

---

# 3. Use the Auxiliary Verb

The question tag normally uses the **same auxiliary or modal verb** as the statement.

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| You **are** ready, | **aren't you?** |
| She **is** coming, | **isn't she?** |
| They **have** finished, | **haven't they?** |
| He **can** swim, | **can't he?** |
| We **should** leave, | **shouldn't we?** |
| You **will** come, | **won't you?** |

### Example

> She **can** drive, **can't she?**

The auxiliary **can** is repeated in the tag.

---

# 4. When There Is No Auxiliary Verb

With the **present simple** and **past simple**, use **do, does, or did**.

| **Tense** | **Statement** | **Question Tag** |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | You like coffee, | **don't you?** |
| Present simple | She likes coffee, | **doesn't she?** |
| Present simple | They work here, | **don't they?** |
| Past simple | He went home, | **didn't he?** |
| Past simple | They studied English, | **didn't they?** |

### Example

> She likes coffee, **doesn't she?**

The main verb is **likes**, so we use **does** in the tag.

---

# 5. Negative Statements

When the statement is negative, the tag is normally positive.

| **Negative Statement** | **Positive Tag** |
|---|---|
| You don't like coffee, | **do you?** |
| She isn't coming, | **is she?** |
| He hasn't finished, | **has he?** |
| They can't swim, | **can they?** |
| We won't be late, | **will we?** |
| She didn't call, | **did she?** |

### Example

> You don't know him, **do you?**

---

# 6. The Subject in the Tag

The subject in the question tag is usually a **pronoun**.

| **Statement Subject** | **Tag Subject** |
|---|---|
| John is here, | **isn't he?** |
| Mary is ready, | **isn't she?** |
| The children are sleeping, | **aren't they?** |
| Your brother can drive, | **can't he?** |
| The car is expensive, | **isn't it?** |

### 📌 Rule

> **Noun in the statement → Pronoun in the tag**

Example:

> Sarah is coming, **isn't she?**

Not:

> ❌ Sarah is coming, isn't Sarah?

---

# 7. Special Case: "I am"

The usual question tag for **I am** is:

> **aren't I?**

| **Statement** | **Correct Tag** |
|---|---|
| I am late, | **aren't I?** |
| I'm right, | **aren't I?** |
| I am invited, | **aren't I?** |

### Example

> I'm your friend, **aren't I?**

### 📌 Important

Although **amn't I?** might seem logical, standard English normally uses:

> **aren't I?**

---

# 8. Imperatives

Question tags can be used after commands or requests.

| **Imperative** | **Tag** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Positive command | **will you?** | Open the door, **will you?** |
| Request | **would you?** | Help me, **would you?** |
| Suggestion | **shall we?** | Let's go, **shall we?** |
| Negative command | **will you?** | Don't be late, **will you?** |

### Example

> Let's have some coffee, **shall we?**

---

# 9. "Let's" + Question Tag

After **Let's**, use:

> **shall we?**

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| Let's go, | **shall we?** |
| Let's start, | **shall we?** |
| Let's have dinner, | **shall we?** |
| Let's watch a movie, | **shall we?** |

### Example

> **Let's go home, shall we?**

---

# 10. "There Is / There Are"

When the statement begins with **there is/are**, use **there** in the tag.

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| There is a problem, | **isn't there?** |
| There isn't any milk, | **is there?** |
| There are many people here, | **aren't there?** |
| There aren't any seats, | **are there?** |

### Example

> There is a mistake, **isn't there?**

---

# 11. Negative Words

Some words have a **negative meaning**, so the tag is normally positive.

Common negative words include:

> never, nobody, no one, nothing, hardly, rarely, seldom, few, little

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| He never lies, | **does he?** |
| Nobody called, | **did they?** |
| Nothing happened, | **did it?** |
| She rarely complains, | **does she?** |
| He hardly knows her, | **does he?** |

### Example

> She never drinks coffee, **does she?**

The word **never** already makes the statement negative in meaning.

---

# 12. Indefinite Pronouns

With words such as **everyone, everybody, someone, nobody**, the tag commonly uses **they**.

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| Everyone is ready, | **aren't they?** |
| Somebody called, | **didn't they?** |
| Nobody knows, | **do they?** |
| Everybody enjoyed it, | **didn't they?** |

### Example

> Everyone is here, **aren't they?**

---

# 13. "This" and "That"

Use **it** in the question tag.

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| This is your book, | **isn't it?** |
| That is your car, | **isn't it?** |
| This isn't yours, | **is it?** |
| That wasn't John, | **was it?** |

### Example

> That's your phone, **isn't it?**

---

# 14. "These" and "Those"

Use **they** in the question tag.

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| These are yours, | **aren't they?** |
| Those are your shoes, | **aren't they?** |
| These aren't yours, | **are they?** |
| Those weren't expensive, | **were they?** |

### Example

> Those are your keys, **aren't they?**

---

# 15. Question Tags with Modal Verbs

Use the same modal verb in the tag.

| **Modal** | **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | She can swim, | **can't she?** |
| **could** | He could help, | **couldn't he?** |
| **will** | They will come, | **won't they?** |
| **would** | She would agree, | **wouldn't she?** |
| **should** | We should leave, | **shouldn't we?** |
| **must** | He must be tired, | **mustn't he?** |
| **may** | She may come, | **mayn't she?** |

---

# 16. Intonation and Meaning

The same question tag can have different meanings depending on **intonation**.

| **Intonation** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Rising ↗** | Genuine question / uncertainty | You're coming, **aren't you? ↗** |
| **Falling ↘** | Expecting agreement | It's a beautiful day, **isn't it? ↘** |

### Example

**Rising intonation:**

> You haven't met him, **have you? ↗**

The speaker genuinely wants to know.

**Falling intonation:**

> It's a great movie, **isn't it? ↘**

The speaker expects the listener to agree.

---

# 17. Quick Formation Rules

| **Statement** | **Tag Formation** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Positive + auxiliary | Negative auxiliary + pronoun | She is here, **isn't she?** |
| Negative + auxiliary | Positive auxiliary + pronoun | She isn't here, **is she?** |
| Positive present simple | don't/doesn't | He works here, **doesn't he?** |
| Positive past simple | didn't | He worked here, **didn't he?** |
| Negative present simple | do/does | He doesn't work here, **does he?** |
| Negative past simple | did | He didn't work here, **did he?** |
| **I am** | **aren't I?** | I'm late, **aren't I?** |
| **Let's** | **shall we?** | Let's go, **shall we?** |
| **Imperative** | usually will/would/can/can't | Open the door, **will you?** |
| **There is/are** | there | There is a problem, **isn't there?** |

---

# 18. Common Mistakes

| **Incorrect** | **Correct** | **Reason** |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ She is happy, **doesn't she?** | ✅ She is happy, **isn't she?** | Use the same auxiliary. |
| ❌ He likes coffee, **isn't he?** | ✅ He likes coffee, **doesn't he?** | Use **does** for present simple. |
| ❌ They went home, **weren't they?** | ✅ They went home, **didn't they?** | Use **did** for past simple. |
| ❌ I'm late, **amn't I?** | ✅ I'm late, **aren't I?** | Standard English uses **aren't I?** |
| ❌ Let's go, **will we?** | ✅ Let's go, **shall we?** | **Let's → shall we?** |

---

# 19. Quick Summary

| **Rule** | **Example** |
|---|---|
| **Positive → negative tag** | You are ready, **aren't you?** |
| **Negative → positive tag** | You aren't ready, **are you?** |
| **Use the same auxiliary** | She has left, **hasn't she?** |
| **No auxiliary → do/does/did** | He works here, **doesn't he?** |
| **Noun → pronoun** | John is here, **isn't he?** |
| **I am → aren't I?** | I'm right, **aren't I?** |
| **Let's → shall we?** | Let's start, **shall we?** |
| **There is/are → there** | There is a problem, **isn't there?** |
| **Negative meaning → positive tag** | She never lies, **does she?** |
| **Everyone/someone → they** | Everyone came, **didn't they?** |

---

## ⭐ Key Point

> **Question tag = short question added to the end of a statement to check or confirm information.**

### 💡 Easy Formula

**Positive statement → Negative tag**

> She is beautiful, **isn't she?**

**Negative statement → Positive tag**

> She isn't angry, **is she?**

### Remember:

> **Statement + opposite auxiliary + pronoun**

| **Statement** | **Tag** |
|---|---|
| You **are** ready, | **aren't you?** |
| He **doesn't** smoke, | **does he?** |
| They **can** swim, | **can't they?** |
| She **went** home, | **didn't she?** |
| Let's go, | **shall we?** |
| I'm right, | **aren't I?** |
