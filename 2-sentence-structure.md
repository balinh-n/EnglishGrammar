# Sentence Structure in English

## 1. What Is Sentence Structure?

**Sentence structure** describes how words, phrases, and clauses are arranged to form a complete sentence.

The basic English sentence pattern is:

**Subject + Verb + Object (S + V + O)**

> She **reads** books.

- **She** = Subject
- **reads** = Verb
- **books** = Object

---

## 2. Main Parts of a Sentence

| Part | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject (S)** | The person or thing that performs the action or is described. | **Tom** works here. |
| **Verb (V)** | Shows an action, state, or occurrence. | Tom **works** here. |
| **Object (O)** | Receives the action of the verb. | Tom reads **books**. |
| **Complement (C)** | Completes or describes the subject or object. | Tom is **happy**. |
| **Adverbial (A)** | Gives information about time, place, manner, reason, etc. | Tom works **at home**. |

---

# 3. Basic Sentence Patterns

| Pattern | Structure | Example | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SV** | Subject + Verb | Birds **fly**. | The verb does not need an object. |
| **SVC** | Subject + Verb + Complement | She **is happy**. | The complement describes the subject. |
| **SVA** | Subject + Verb + Adverbial | He **is at home**. | The adverbial gives information about location. |
| **SVO** | Subject + Verb + Object | She **reads books**. | The object receives the action. |
| **SVOO** | Subject + Verb + Object + Object | She **gave me a book**. | The verb has two objects. |
| **SVOC** | Subject + Verb + Object + Complement | They **made him angry**. | The complement describes the object. |
| **SVOA** | Subject + Verb + Object + Adverbial | She **put the book on the table**. | The adverbial gives information about the object. |

---

# 4. Subject + Verb (SV)

This is one of the simplest sentence patterns.

**Structure:**

**S + V**

| Example | Subject | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Birds **fly**. | Birds | fly |
| Children **laugh**. | Children | laugh |
| The baby **slept**. | The baby | slept |
| John **arrived**. | John | arrived |

These verbs do not require a direct object.

---

# 5. Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)

The object receives the action of the verb.

**Structure:**

**S + V + O**

| Example | Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| She **reads books**. | She | reads | books |
| I **like coffee**. | I | like | coffee |
| They **play football**. | They | play | football |
| He **opened the door**. | He | opened | the door |

---

# 6. Subject + Verb + Complement (SVC)

A complement gives more information about the subject.

This pattern commonly uses **linking verbs** such as:

- be
- become
- seem
- look
- feel
- appear
- remain

**Structure:**

**S + V + C**

| Example | Subject | Verb | Complement |
|---|---|---|---|
| She **is a teacher**. | She | is | a teacher |
| He **became angry**. | He | became | angry |
| The food **looks delicious**. | The food | looks | delicious |
| They **seem tired**. | They | seem | tired |

---

# 7. Subject + Verb + Adverbial (SVA)

An adverbial gives additional information, often about **place** or **time**.

**Structure:**

**S + V + A**

| Example | Subject | Verb | Adverbial |
|---|---|---|---|
| She **is at home**. | She | is | at home |
| They **live in London**. | They | live | in London |
| The meeting **is tomorrow**. | The meeting | is | tomorrow |
| He **stayed at the hotel**. | He | stayed | at the hotel |

---

# 8. Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object (SVOO)

Some verbs can take two objects.

- **Indirect object (IO)** = person who receives something
- **Direct object (DO)** = thing that is given, sent, bought, etc.

**Structure:**

**S + V + IO + DO**

| Example | Subject | Verb | Indirect Object | Direct Object |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| She **gave me a book**. | She | gave | me | a book |
| He **sent his mother a letter**. | He | sent | his mother | a letter |
| I **bought my sister a gift**. | I | bought | my sister | a gift |

The same idea can often be expressed with a preposition:

> She gave **a book to me**.

---

# 9. Subject + Verb + Object + Complement (SVOC)

The complement gives more information about the object.

**Structure:**

**S + V + O + C**

| Example | Object | Complement |
|---|---|---|
| They **made him angry**. | him | angry |
| We **elected her president**. | her | president |
| The news **made me happy**. | me | happy |
| They **painted the door red**. | the door | red |

The complement describes or identifies the object.

---

# 10. Subject + Verb + Object + Adverbial (SVOA)

Some verbs require additional information after the object.

**Structure:**

**S + V + O + A**

| Example | Subject | Verb | Object | Adverbial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| She **put the book on the table**. | She | put | the book | on the table |
| He **placed the keys on the desk**. | He | placed | the keys | on the desk |
| They **kept the food in the fridge**. | They | kept | the food | in the fridge |

---

# 11. Simple, Compound, Complex, and Compound-Complex Sentences

Sentence structure can also be classified by the number and type of clauses.

| Type | Structure | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | 1 independent clause | Contains one main clause. | She studies English. |
| **Compound** | 2+ independent clauses | Joins independent clauses with coordinating conjunctions. | She studies English, **and** her brother studies French. |
| **Complex** | 1 independent + 1+ dependent clause | Contains a main clause and at least one dependent clause. | She studies English **because she wants to travel**. |
| **Compound-Complex** | 2+ independent + 1+ dependent clause | Combines compound and complex structures. | She studies English **because she wants to travel**, and her brother studies French. |

---

# 12. Simple Sentence

A **simple sentence** has one independent clause.

It can have a compound subject or compound verb but still have only one independent clause.

| Example | Structure |
|---|---|
| She works. | S + V |
| She reads books. | S + V + O |
| Tom and Mary study English. | Compound S + V + O |
| She sings and dances. | S + Compound V |

A simple sentence does **not** necessarily mean a short sentence.

---

# 13. Compound Sentence

A **compound sentence** contains two or more independent clauses.

They are commonly connected with:

**FANBOYS**

- for
- and
- nor
- but
- or
- yet
- so

### Structure

**Independent Clause + , + Coordinating Conjunction + Independent Clause**

**Example:**

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

The two independent clauses are:

1. I was tired.
2. I continued working.

---

# 14. Complex Sentence

A **complex sentence** contains:

- One independent clause
- At least one dependent clause

### Common Subordinating Conjunctions

- because
- although
- if
- when
- while
- since
- unless
- before
- after

### Examples

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

> **Although I was tired**, I continued working.

The dependent clause cannot normally stand alone as a complete sentence.

---

# 15. Compound-Complex Sentence

A compound-complex sentence contains:

- At least **two independent clauses**
- At least **one dependent clause**

**Example:**

> I stayed home because it was raining, **and** my brother watched TV.

| Clause | Type |
|---|---|
| I stayed home | Independent |
| because it was raining | Dependent |
| my brother watched TV | Independent |

---

# 16. Questions

English questions have several common structures.

| Question Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes/No Question** | Auxiliary + S + V...? | **Do** you like coffee? |
| **Be Question** | Be + S + complement...? | **Are** you tired? |
| **Wh- Question** | Wh-word + auxiliary + S + V...? | **Where do** you live? |
| **Subject Question** | Wh-word + verb...? | **Who called** you? |
| **Modal Question** | Modal + S + V...? | **Can** you swim? |

---

# 17. Negative Sentences

Negative sentences commonly use **not** with an auxiliary verb.

| Tense / Verb | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | do/does + not + V1 | She **does not like** coffee. |
| Past Simple | did + not + V1 | They **did not go**. |
| Be | am/is/are + not | He **is not** ready. |
| Modal | modal + not + V1 | You **cannot swim**. |
| Present Perfect | have/has + not + V3 | She **has not finished**. |

---

# 18. Imperative Sentences

An imperative sentence gives:

- Commands
- Instructions
- Advice
- Requests

The subject **you** is usually understood.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Command | **Open** the door. |
| Instruction | **Turn** left. |
| Advice | **Take** a break. |
| Negative command | **Don't touch** that. |
| Request | **Please sit** down. |

---

# 19. Exclamatory Sentences

Exclamatory sentences express strong emotion.

They often use **what** or **how**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **What + noun phrase + !** | **What a beautiful day!** |
| **How + adjective/adverb + !** | **How beautiful she is!** |

| **S + V + C** | She is happy. |
| **S + V + A** | He lives in London. |

# 21. Word Order in English

English generally follows a relatively fixed word order.

### Basic Order

**Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time**

> She **studies** English **at home** **every evening**.

| Position | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject | She |
| Verb | studies |
| Object | English |
| Place | at home |
| Time | every evening |

### Another Example

> Tom **played** football **at the park** **yesterday**.

---

# 22. Adjective Order

When several adjectives describe one noun, English usually follows a particular order.

**Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose → Noun**

| Position | Example |
|---|---|
| Opinion | beautiful |
| Size | large |
| Age | old |
| Shape | round |
| Color | red |
| Origin | Italian |
| Material | wooden |
| Noun | table |

**Example:**

> She bought a **beautiful large old round red Italian wooden table**.

Not every sentence needs all these categories.

---

# 23. Adverb Position

The position of an adverb depends on its type.

| Adverb Type | Common Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | Before main verb | She **often visits** me. |
| **Frequency with "be"** | After be | She **is always** happy. |
| **Manner** | Usually after object / verb | He speaks English **fluently**. |
| **Time** | Often at the end | I saw him **yesterday**. |
| **Place** | Often after verb/object | She works **at home**. |

---

# 24. Sentence Structure: Quick Comparison

| Sentence Type | Clauses | Example |
|---|---:|---|
| **Simple** | 1 independent | I like coffee. |
| **Compound** | 2+ independent | I like coffee, and she likes tea. |
| **Complex** | 1 independent + 1+ dependent | I drink coffee because I am tired. |
| **Compound-Complex** | 2+ independent + 1+ dependent | I drink coffee because I am tired, and she drinks tea because she likes it. |

---

# 25. How to Identify Sentence Structure

Follow these steps:

1. **Find the verbs.**
2. **Find the subjects.**
3. **Identify the objects and complements.**
4. **Look for independent and dependent clauses.**
5. **Check how the clauses are connected.**

### Example

> Because it was raining, I stayed home, and my brother watched TV.

| Part | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Because it was raining | Dependent clause |
| I stayed home | Independent clause |
| my brother watched TV | Independent clause |
| **and** | Coordinating conjunction |
| Whole sentence | Compound-complex sentence |

|---|---|---|
| **S** | Subject | **She** works. |

| **A** | Adverbial | She works **at home**. |

---

# 27. Easy Way to Remember

**Basic sentence:**

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

**Example:**

> She + reads + books.

**Add information:**

> She + reads + books + at home + every evening.

**Connect clauses:**

> She reads books because she enjoys learning, and her brother watches TV.

The most important idea is:

> **English word order is usually Subject → Verb → Object, while additional information and clauses are added around this basic structure.**
