# Causative Structures in English

## 1. What Is a Causative Structure?

A **causative structure** is used when one person **causes, asks, pays, or arranges for another person to do something**.

### Basic Idea

> **Person A causes Person B to do something.**

### Example

> I **had my brother fix** my computer.

I did not fix the computer myself. I arranged for my brother to fix it.

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **have + person + base verb** | Arrange/ask someone to do something | I had Tom **fix** the car. |
| **get + person + to-infinitive** | Persuade/manage to make someone do something | I got Tom **to fix** the car. |
| **make + person + base verb** | Force someone to do something | The teacher made us **study**. |
| **let + person + base verb** | Allow someone to do something | My parents let me **go**. |
| **have + thing + past participle** | Arrange for something to be done | I had my car **repaired**. |
| **get + thing + past participle** | Arrange/manage to have something done | I got my car **repaired**. |

---

# 2. Have + Person + Base Verb

Use:

> **have + person + base verb**

This means **ask, arrange, or instruct someone to do something**.

### Examples

> I had John **clean** the room.

> She had her assistant **send** the email.

> The manager had the workers **finish** the project.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| have + person + verb | I had John **wash** the car. |
| have + person + verb | She had her assistant **call** me. |
| have + person + verb | We had the technician **check** the computer. |

### Meaning

It usually suggests that the subject **arranges or instructs** someone to perform the action.

---

# 3. Have + Thing + Past Participle

Use:

> **have + thing + past participle**

This means **arrange for someone to do something for you**.

### Examples

> I had my car **repaired**.

> She had her hair **cut**.

> We had the house **painted**.

The subject usually **does not perform the action personally**.

| Causative Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| have + car + repaired | Arrange for someone to repair the car | I had my car **repaired**. |
| have + hair + cut | Arrange for someone to cut the hair | She had her hair **cut**. |
| have + house + painted | Arrange for someone to paint the house | They had their house **painted**. |

---

# 4. Get + Person + To-Infinitive

Use:

> **get + person + to + verb**

This means **persuade, convince, or manage to make someone do something**.

### Examples

> I got John **to help** me.

> She got her brother **to clean** the room.

> We got the children **to go** to bed.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| get + person + to verb | I got him **to help** me. |
| get + person + to verb | She got Sarah **to join** us. |
| get + person + to verb | He got his friend **to drive** him home. |

### Compare

> I **had** John clean the room.  
> → I arranged or asked him to clean it.

> I **got** John to clean the room.  
> → I persuaded or convinced him to clean it.

---

# 5. Get + Thing + Past Participle

Use:

> **get + thing + past participle**

This means **arrange for something to be done**.

### Examples

> I got my car **repaired**.

> She got her hair **cut**.

> He got his phone **fixed**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| get + thing + V3 | I got my car **washed**. |
| get + thing + V3 | She got her hair **cut**. |
| get + thing + V3 | He got his computer **repaired**. |

This is similar to:

> I **had** my car repaired.

> I **got** my car repaired.

Both mean that someone else repaired the car.

---

# 6. Make + Person + Base Verb

Use:

> **make + person + base verb**

This means **force or require someone to do something**.

### Examples

> The teacher made us **stay**.

> My boss made me **work** late.

> The movie made her **cry**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| make + person + verb | The teacher made us **study**. |
| make + person + verb | His parents made him **apologize**. |
| make + person + verb | The joke made everyone **laugh**. |

### Important

After **make**, use the **base verb**, not **to + verb**.

> ✅ She made me **clean**.

> ❌ She made me **to clean**.

---

# 7. Make in the Passive Voice

When **make** is passive, we use:

> **be made + to-infinitive**

### Examples

> We were made **to wait**.

> He was made **to apologize**.

> They were made **to work** late.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The teacher made us **stay**. | We were made **to stay**. |
| The boss made him **work**. | He was made **to work**. |
| They made me **apologize**. | I was made **to apologize**. |

### Key Rule

> Active: **make + person + base verb**

> Passive: **be made + to-infinitive**

---

# 8. Let + Person + Base Verb

Use:

> **let + person + base verb**

This means **allow or give permission**.

### Examples

> My parents let me **go** out.

> She let him **use** her phone.

> The teacher let us **leave** early.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| let + person + verb | Let me **help** you. |
| let + person + verb | She let him **stay**. |
| let + person + verb | They let us **use** the room. |

### Important

After **let**, do not use **to**.

> ✅ Let me **go**.

> ❌ Let me **to go**.

---

# 9. Allow + Person + To-Infinitive

**Allow** has a similar meaning to **let**, but its structure is different.

> **allow + person + to + verb**

### Examples

> My parents allowed me **to go** out.

> The teacher allowed us **to leave** early.

> They allowed him **to use** the computer.

| Verb | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **let** | let + person + base verb | They let me **go**. |
| **allow** | allow + person + to verb | They allowed me **to go**. |
| **permit** | permit + person + to verb | They permitted me **to go**. |

---

# 10. Help + Person + Verb / To-Infinitive

**Help** can be followed by either a bare infinitive or a to-infinitive.

### Examples

> She helped me **carry** the bags.

> She helped me **to carry** the bags.

Both are correct.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| help + person + base verb | He helped me **move** the table. |
| help + person + to verb | He helped me **to move** the table. |

The form without **to** is especially common in everyday English.

---

# 11. Have vs. Get

Both **have** and **get** can describe arranging for another person to do something.

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **have + person + verb** | Arrange/ask someone | I had Tom **repair** my car. |
| **get + person + to verb** | Persuade someone | I got Tom **to repair** my car. |
| **have + thing + V3** | Arrange a service | I had my car **repaired**. |
| **get + thing + V3** | Arrange/manage a service | I got my car **repaired**. |

### Compare

> I **had** my brother clean the room.  
> → I asked/arranged for him to clean it.

> I **got** my brother to clean the room.  
> → I persuaded him to clean it.

---

# 12. Causative with Services

A very common causative structure is:

> **have/get + object + past participle**

It is often used when talking about **services**.

| Service | Causative Example |
|---|---|
| Haircut | I had my hair **cut**. |
| Car repair | She got her car **repaired**. |
| House painting | We had our house **painted**. |
| Computer repair | He got his computer **fixed**. |
| Teeth cleaning | I had my teeth **cleaned**. |
| Documents translated | She got the documents **translated**. |
| Clothes cleaned | He had his suit **cleaned**. |

---

# 13. Causative in Different Tenses

The causative structure can be used in different tenses.

### Have + Object + Past Participle

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | have/has + object + V3 | I **have** my car repaired regularly. |
| Past simple | had + object + V3 | I **had** my car repaired yesterday. |
| Present perfect | have/has had + object + V3 | I **have had** my car repaired. |
| Future | will have + object + V3 | I **will have** my car repaired tomorrow. |
| Present continuous | am/is/are having + object + V3 | I am **having** my car repaired now. |
| Modal | modal + have + object + V3 | I should **have** my car repaired. |

---

# 14. Causative with Questions

Causative structures can also be used in questions.

### Examples

> Did you **have your car repaired**?

> Where did you **get your hair cut**?

> Are you **having the house painted**?

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| Yes/No question | Did you have your car **repaired**? |
| Wh-question | Where did you get your hair **cut**? |
| Present continuous | Are you having your house **painted**? |
| Present perfect | Have you had your computer **fixed**? |

---

# 15. Causative with Negatives

Use normal auxiliary rules to make causative structures negative.

### Examples

> I didn't have my car **repaired**.

> She didn't get her hair **cut**.

> He didn't make me **wait**.

> They didn't let us **leave**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| didn't have + object + V3 | I didn't have my car **repaired**. |
| didn't get + object + V3 | She didn't get her phone **fixed**. |
| didn't make + person + verb | He didn't make me **wait**. |
| didn't let + person + verb | They didn't let us **leave**. |

---

# 16. Causative: Active vs. Passive Meaning

Compare these sentences:

> I **repaired my car**.

I did the repair myself.

> I **had my car repaired**.

Someone else repaired my car.

| Sentence | Who does the action? |
|---|---|
| I repaired my car. | **I** repaired it. |
| I had my car repaired. | **Someone else** repaired it. |
| I got my car repaired. | **Someone else** repaired it. |
| I had John repair my car. | **John** repaired it. |
| I got John to repair my car. | **John** repaired it after I persuaded/asked him. |

---

# 17. Causative vs. Passive

Causative and passive structures can look similar, but their meanings are different.

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Passive: be + V3** | Focuses on the action/result | My car was **repaired**. |
| **Causative: have + object + V3** | Someone arranged for the action | I had my car **repaired**. |
| **Causative: get + object + V3** | Someone arranged/managed for the action | I got my car **repaired**. |

### Compare

> My car was **repaired**.  
> → Focus: the car and the repair.

> I had my car **repaired**.  
> → Focus: I arranged for the repair.

| Causative Verb | Structure | Main Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **have** | have + person + base verb | Ask/arrange | I had him **fix** it. |
| **have** | have + thing + V3 | Arrange a service | I had it **fixed**. |
| **get** | get + person + to + verb | Persuade/manage | I got him **to fix** it. |
| **get** | get + thing + V3 | Arrange a service | I got it **fixed**. |
| **make** | make + person + base verb | Force | She made me **wait**. |
| **let** | let + person + base verb | Allow | She let me **go**. |
| **allow** | allow + person + to + verb | Allow | She allowed me **to go**. |
| **help** | help + person + (to) verb | Assist | She helped me **(to) move** it. |

# 19. Causative: Have vs. Get vs. Make vs. Let

| Verb | Strength / Meaning | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **have** | Arrange/ask | have + person + verb | I had him **call** me. |
| **get** | Persuade | get + person + to verb | I got him **to call** me. |
| **make** | Force | make + person + verb | I made him **call** me. |
| **let** | Allow | let + person + verb | I let him **call** me. |
| **allow** | Give permission | allow + person + to verb | I allowed him **to call** me. |

# 20. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| I had him **to fix** my car. | I had him **fix** my car. | Have + person uses the base verb. |
| I got him **fix** my car. | I got him **to fix** my car. | Get + person uses to-infinitive. |
| She made me **to wait**. | She made me **wait**. | Make + person uses the base verb. |
| They let me **to go**. | They let me **go**. | Let + person uses the base verb. |
| She allowed me **go**. | She allowed me **to go**. | Allow + person uses to-infinitive. |
| I had my car **repair**. | I had my car **repaired**. | Have + thing uses the past participle. |
| He got his hair **cutting**. | He got his hair **cut**. | Get + thing uses the past participle. |

---

# 21. How to Identify a Causative Structure

Ask:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| Did the subject arrange for someone else to do something? | I had my car **repaired**. |
| Did the subject persuade someone to do something? | I got him **to help** me. |
| Did the subject force someone to do something? | She made him **apologize**. |
| Did the subject allow someone to do something? | They let us **leave**. |
| Did the subject arrange a service? | She had her hair **cut**. |

---

# 22. Causative in One Sentence

Consider:

> I **had my car repaired**, and then I **got my brother to drive** me home.

| Structure | Causative Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **had my car repaired** | have + object + V3 | I arranged for someone to repair my car. |
| **got my brother to drive** | get + person + to verb | I persuaded/asked my brother to drive me. |

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **have + person + base verb** | Ask/arrange for someone to do something | I had John **clean** the room. |
| **have + thing + V3** | Arrange for something to be done | I had my car **cleaned**. |
| **get + person + to + verb** | Persuade someone | I got John **to clean** the room. |
| **get + thing + V3** | Arrange/manage a service | I got my car **cleaned**. |
| **make + person + base verb** | Force someone | She made me **apologize**. |
| **let + person + base verb** | Allow someone | She let me **leave**. |
| **allow + person + to + verb** | Allow someone | She allowed me **to leave**. |
| **help + person + (to) verb** | Help someone | He helped me **(to) carry** it. |

# 24. Easy Way to Remember

| Causative Verb | Think of It As |
|---|---|
| **Have** | Arrange |
| **Get** | Persuade |
| **Make** | Force |
| **Let** | Allow |
| **Allow** | Give permission |
| **Help** | Assist |

### Main Patterns

> **Have someone do:**  
> I had John **repair** my car.

> **Have something done:**  
> I had my car **repaired**.

> **Get someone to do:**  
> I got John **to repair** my car.

> **Get something done:**  
> I got my car **repaired**.

> **Make someone do:**  
> She made John **repair** the car.

> **Let someone do:**  
> She let John **repair** the car.

> **Allow someone to do:**  
> She allowed John **to repair** the car.

## Key Point

The most important causative patterns are:

> **have + person + base verb**

> **have + thing + past participle**

> **get + person + to-infinitive**

> **get + thing + past participle**

> **make + person + base verb**

> **let + person + base verb**

> **allow + person + to-infinitive**
