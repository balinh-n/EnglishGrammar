# Prepositions in English

## 1. What Is a Preposition?

A **preposition** is a word that shows the **relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word** in a sentence.

Prepositions can show:

- Place
- Time
- Direction
- Movement
- Position
- Relationship
- Cause
- Purpose
- Method

Common prepositions include:

> in, on, at, to, from, for, with, by, about, under, over, between, among, behind, beside, through, during, before, after

---

# 2. Summary of Prepositions

| Type | Function | Common Prepositions | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Place** | Shows where something is | in, on, at, under, behind | The keys are **on** the table. |
| **Time** | Shows when something happens | at, on, in, before, after | I wake up **at** 7:00. |
| **Direction** | Shows where someone/something goes | to, toward, into | She went **to** school. |
| **Movement** | Shows movement from one place to another | across, through, along, over | We walked **across** the bridge. |
| **Position** | Shows the position of something | beside, between, near, opposite | The bank is **near** the station. |
| **Source** | Shows where something comes from | from, out of | He is **from** Canada. |
| **Connection** | Shows association | with, about, of | I spoke **with** my teacher. |
| **Purpose** | Shows purpose | for | This gift is **for** you. |
| **Method** | Shows how something is done | by, with | I went there **by** bus. |
| **Cause** | Shows a reason | because of, due to | The game was canceled **because of** rain. |

# 3. Prepositions of Place

Prepositions of place show **where something or someone is located**.

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | Inside an area or container | The keys are **in** the bag. |
| **on** | Touching a surface | The book is **on** the table. |
| **at** | A specific point or location | She is **at** the station. |
| **under** | Lower than something | The cat is **under** the chair. |
| **above** | Higher than something | The picture is **above** the sofa. |
| **below** | Lower than something | The temperature is **below** zero. |
| **behind** | At the back of something | The car is **behind** the house. |
| **in front of** | Before or ahead of something | He is **in front of** the building. |
| **beside** | Next to something | She sat **beside** me. |
| **near** | Close to something | The school is **near** my house. |
| **between** | In the space separating two things | The bank is **between** the hotel and the café. |
| **among** | In the middle of several things | He was standing **among** his friends. |
| **opposite** | Facing something | The bank is **opposite** the hospital. |

---

# 4. "In", "On", and "At" for Place

These three prepositions are very common.

| Preposition | Main Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | Inside an area, room, city, country | She lives **in** London. |
| **on** | On a surface | The phone is **on** the desk. |
| **at** | Specific point or location | He is **at** the bus stop. |

### Examples

> She is **in** the room.

> The cup is **on** the table.

> I am **at** the airport.

---

# 5. Prepositions of Time

Prepositions of time show **when something happens**.

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | Exact time | **at** 7:00 |
| **on** | Days and dates | **on** Monday |
| **in** | Months, years, seasons, periods | **in** July |
| **before** | Earlier than a time | **before** lunch |
| **after** | Later than a time | **after** school |
| **during** | Within a period | **during** the meeting |
| **since** | From a point in the past until now | **since** 2020 |
| **for** | Duration | **for** two hours |
| **until / till** | Up to a certain time | **until** Friday |
| **by** | No later than a time | **by** 5:00 |

---

# 6. "At", "On", and "In" for Time

### At

Use **at** for exact times and certain expressions.

> **at 6:00**

> **at noon**

> **at midnight**

> **at night**

### On

Use **on** for days and dates.

> **on Monday**

> **on Friday morning**

> **on July 4th**

### In

Use **in** for months, years, seasons, and longer periods.

> **in January**

> **in 2025**

> **in summer**

> **in the morning**

| Preposition | Time | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | Exact time | **at** 8:30 |
| **on** | Day / date | **on** Tuesday |
| **in** | Month / year / period | **in** March |
| **in** | Part of the day | **in** the morning |
| **at** | Night | **at** night |

# 7. Prepositions of Direction

Prepositions of direction show **where someone or something is going**.

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **to** | Movement toward a place | I went **to** school. |
| **into** | Movement from outside to inside | She walked **into** the room. |
| **onto** | Movement to a surface | The cat jumped **onto** the table. |
| **toward** | Movement in the direction of | He walked **toward** the door. |
| **from** | Starting point | She came **from** work. |
| **out of** | Movement from inside to outside | He walked **out of** the house. |
| **through** | Movement from one side to another within something | We walked **through** the tunnel. |
| **across** | Movement from one side to another | She walked **across** the street. |

# 8. Prepositions of Movement

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **across** | From one side to another | We walked **across** the road. |
| **through** | From one side to another inside something | They drove **through** the tunnel. |
| **along** | Following a line or path | We walked **along** the river. |
| **around** | Moving in a circle or surrounding something | We walked **around** the park. |
| **over** | From one side to the other, above | The bird flew **over** the house. |
| **under** | Moving below something | The dog ran **under** the table. |
| **past** | Moving beyond something | She walked **past** the bank. |
| **up** | Movement toward a higher position | He walked **up** the stairs. |
| **down** | Movement toward a lower position | They walked **down** the hill. |

# 10. Prepositions of Source

A source preposition shows **where someone or something comes from**.

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **from** | Origin or starting point | I am **from** Vietnam. |
| **out of** | From inside | She came **out of** the room. |
| **off** | Away from a surface | The book fell **off** the table. |

### Examples

> This letter is **from** my friend.

> He took the book **off** the shelf.

> She walked **out of** the building.

---

# 11. Prepositions of Purpose

**For** is commonly used to show purpose.

### Examples

> This gift is **for** you.

> I went there **for** work.

> This machine is **for** cutting wood.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Person receiving something | This is **for** Sarah. |
| Purpose | I went there **for** work. |
| Intended use | This tool is **for** cutting paper. |

---

# 12. Prepositions of Method

**By** and **with** are commonly used to describe methods or means.

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **by** | Method of transportation or communication | I went **by** bus. |
| **with** | Tool or instrument | I cut it **with** a knife. |
| **by** | Method of doing something | We traveled **by** train. |

### Compare

> I went **by bus**.

> I opened the door **with a key**.

---

# 13. "By" vs. "With"

| Preposition | Main Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **by** | Method / means | I traveled **by** train. |
| **with** | Tool / instrument | I cut the paper **with** scissors. |
| **by** | Agent in passive voice | The book was written **by** George Orwell. |
| **with** | Accompaniment | I went **with** my friend. |

# 14. Common Prepositional Phrases

A **prepositional phrase** consists of a preposition followed by its object.

### Examples

> **in the house**

> **on the table**

> **after lunch**

> **with my friend**

> **under the bed**

| Prepositional Phrase | Example Sentence |
|---|---|
| **in the room** | She is **in the room**. |
| **on the table** | The book is **on the table**. |
| **after school** | I play football **after school**. |
| **with my friend** | I went there **with my friend**. |
| **under the bed** | The cat is **under the bed**. |

# 17. "In" vs. "Into"

**In** usually describes a **position**.

**Into** usually describes **movement**.

| Preposition | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | Position | She is **in** the room. |
| **into** | Movement | She walked **into** the room. |

### Compare

> The children are **in** the classroom.

> The children walked **into** the classroom.

---

# 18. "On" vs. "Onto"

**On** usually describes a **position**.

**Onto** usually describes **movement toward a surface**.

| Preposition | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **on** | Position | The cat is **on** the table. |
| **onto** | Movement | The cat jumped **onto** the table. |

---

# 19. "Between" vs. "Among"

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **between** | Usually separates two people or things | The bank is **between** the school and the hotel. |
| **among** | Surrounded by or included in a group | She was **among** her friends. |

### Examples

> The ball is **between** the two chairs.

> He was standing **among** the students.

---

# 20. "Since" vs. "For"

Both can be used to talk about time.

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **since** | Starting point | I have lived here **since 2020**. |
| **for** | Duration | I have lived here **for six years**. |

### Compare

> She has worked here **since Monday**.

> She has worked here **for five days**.

---

# 21. "During" vs. "For"

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **during** | A period or event | I slept **during the movie**. |
| **for** | Length of time | I slept **for two hours**. |

### Compare

> We talked **during the meeting**.

> We talked **for two hours**.

---

# 22. "By" vs. "Until"

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **by** | No later than a specific time | Finish it **by Friday**. |
| **until** | Continues up to a specific time | Stay here **until Friday**. |

### Compare

> I will finish the work **by 5:00**.

> I will work **until 5:00**.

## Key Point

Prepositions show **relationships between words**, especially relationships of **place, time, direction, movement, and connection**.

Remember the three most common patterns:

> **in** = inside / longer periods  
> **on** = surface / days and dates  
> **at** = specific point / exact time

Examples:

> The keys are **in** the bag.

> The keys are **on** the table.

> I will meet you **at** 7:00.
