# Adverbs in English

## 1. What Is an Adverb?

An **adverb** is a word that gives more information about a **verb, adjective, another adverb, or a whole sentence**.

Adverbs can tell us:

- **how** something happens
- **when** something happens
- **where** something happens
- **how often** something happens
- **how much / to what degree** something happens
- **how certain** something is

### Examples

| Adverb | What it describes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quickly | verb | She runs **quickly**. |
| very | adjective | She is **very** happy. |
| extremely | adverb | He runs **extremely** quickly. |
| yesterday | verb/sentence | I arrived **yesterday**. |
| here | verb | Come **here**. |
| always | verb | She **always** studies. |
| probably | whole sentence | He will **probably** come. |

---

# 2. Main Types of Adverbs

| Type | Question | Examples | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manner** | How? | slowly, quickly, carefully | She drives **carefully**. |
| **Place** | Where? | here, there, everywhere | Come **here**. |
| **Time** | When? | now, yesterday, soon | I'll call you **later**. |
| **Frequency** | How often? | always, often, never | He **often** exercises. |
| **Degree** | How much? | very, too, quite, extremely | It is **very** cold. |
| **Certainty** | How certain? | certainly, probably, perhaps | She will **probably** come. |
| **Comment/Viewpoint** | What is the speaker's opinion? | fortunately, honestly, personally | **Fortunately**, nobody was hurt. |
| **Question** | Asking about circumstances | when, where, how, why | **Why** are you late? |
| **Relative** | Connects clauses | where, when, why | This is the place **where** we met. |

# 3. Adverbs of Manner

Adverbs of manner describe **how an action happens**.

### Common Examples

| Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| quickly | in a quick way |
| slowly | in a slow way |
| carefully | with care |
| quietly | without much noise |
| loudly | with a lot of noise |
| easily | without difficulty |
| badly | in a bad way |
| well | in a good way |
| politely | in a polite way |
| angrily | in an angry way |

### Examples

> She speaks **quietly**.

> He drives **carefully**.

> They worked **quickly**.

> She sings **beautifully**.

Adverbs of time tell us **when** something happens.

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| now | I am busy **now**. |

| yesterday | She arrived **yesterday**. |
| tomorrow | We will leave **tomorrow**. |

# 6. Adverbs of Frequency

Adverbs of frequency tell us **how often** something happens.

### Frequency Scale

| Adverb | Approximate Frequency |
|---|---:|
| always | 100% |
| usually | about 80–90% |
| normally | about 80–90% |
| often | about 60–70% |
| sometimes | about 40–50% |
| occasionally | about 20–30% |
| rarely | about 10% |
| seldom | about 10% |
| hardly ever | almost 0% |
| never | 0% |

### Examples

> I **always** wake up early.

> She **usually** walks to work.

> They **often** eat together.

> He **sometimes** plays tennis.

> We **rarely** watch television.

> She **never** drinks coffee.

---

# 7. Position of Frequency Adverbs

The position depends on the verb.

## Before the Main Verb

> I **always eat** breakfast.

> She **often studies** at night.

> They **never watch** TV.

### Pattern

**Subject + frequency adverb + main verb**

---

## After the Verb "Be"

> I am **always** happy.

> She is **usually** late.

> They are **never** angry.

### Pattern

**Subject + be + frequency adverb**

---

## After an Auxiliary Verb

> I have **never** seen it.

> She can **always** help.

> They will **usually** arrive early.

### Pattern

**Subject + auxiliary + frequency adverb + main verb**

---

# 8. Adverbs of Degree

Adverbs of degree show **how much or to what extent** something happens.

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| very | very good |
| really | really interesting |
| extremely | extremely difficult |
| quite | quite easy |
| rather | rather expensive |
| fairly | fairly simple |
| too | too hot |
| so | so beautiful |
| enough | good enough |
| almost | almost finished |
| completely | completely wrong |
| absolutely | absolutely certain |
| totally | totally different |
| slightly | slightly different |

### Examples

> The exam was **very** difficult.

> She is **extremely** intelligent.

> The water is **too** hot.

> He is **almost** ready.

---

# 9. Very vs. Too

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **very** | High degree, not necessarily a problem | The bag is **very** heavy. |
| **too** | More than desirable/acceptable | The bag is **too** heavy to carry. |

### Compare

> The coffee is **very hot**.

→ It has a high temperature.

> The coffee is **too hot** to drink.

→ The temperature prevents you from drinking it comfortably.

---

# 10. Enough

**Enough** means "as much as necessary."

### With Adjectives and Adverbs

**Adjective/Adverb + enough**

> She is old **enough**.

> He runs fast **enough**.

> The room is large **enough**.

### With Nouns

**enough + noun**

> We have **enough money**.

> There is **enough food**.

---

# 11. Adverbs of Certainty

Adverbs of certainty show **how sure the speaker is**.

| Adverb | Degree of certainty |
|---|---|
| definitely | very certain |
| certainly | very certain |
| surely | strong belief |
| probably | likely |
| possibly | possible |
| perhaps | possible |
| maybe | possible |

### Examples

> She will **definitely** come.

> He will **probably** arrive soon.

> They will **possibly** join us.

> **Maybe** she is busy.

---

# 12. Position of Certainty Adverbs

### Before the Main Verb

> She **probably knows** the answer.

> He **definitely wants** to go.

### After "Be"

> She is **probably** at home.

> He is **definitely** ready.

### At the Beginning

> **Perhaps**, we should leave.

> **Probably**, he forgot.

---

# 13. Adverbs of Comment

Comment adverbs express the speaker's attitude or opinion about the whole sentence.

| Adverb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fortunately | luckily | **Fortunately**, nobody was hurt. |
| unfortunately | unluckily | **Unfortunately**, we missed the train. |
| honestly | speaking truthfully | **Honestly**, I don't know. |
| personally | according to my opinion | **Personally**, I prefer tea. |
| surprisingly | unexpectedly | **Surprisingly**, he won. |
| luckily | fortunately | **Luckily**, we arrived on time. |

These often appear at the **beginning of a sentence**.

---

# 14. Adverbs of Frequency vs. Time

These are easy to confuse.

| Type | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | How often? | I **often** exercise. |
| Time | When? | I exercise **today**. |

### Compare

> I **often** go there.

→ How often? Frequently.

> I went there **yesterday**.

→ When? Yesterday.

# 15. Adverb Formation

Many adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to adjectives.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| quick | quickly |
| slow | slowly |
| careful | carefully |
| beautiful | beautifully |
| quiet | quietly |
| polite | politely |
| serious | seriously |
| happy | happily |
| easy | easily |
| angry | angrily |

### Examples

> He is **careful**.

> He drives **carefully**.

---

# 16. Spelling Rules for -ly

## Adjective + -ly

> quick → quickly

> careful → carefully

> slow → slowly

## Adjective Ending in -y

Change **y → i** and add **-ly**.

> happy → happily

> easy → easily

> angry → angrily

## Adjective Ending in -le

Often change **-le → -ly**.

> gentle → gently

> simple → simply

## Adjective Ending in -ic

Usually add **-ally**.

> basic → basically

> automatic → automatically

> dramatic → dramatically

# 17. Important Adverbs That Do Not End in -ly

Not all adverbs end in **-ly**.

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| fast | He runs **fast**. |
| hard | She works **hard**. |
| late | He arrived **late**. |
| early | We arrived **early**. |
| well | She sings **well**. |
| soon | He will arrive **soon**. |
| here | Come **here**. |
| there | Put it **there**. |
| often | I **often** go there. |
| never | She **never** complains. |

---

# 18. Good vs. Well

**Good** is normally an adjective.

**Well** is normally an adverb.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| good | well |

### Examples

> She is a **good** singer.

> She sings **well**.

> He is a **good** driver.

> He drives **well**.

### Important Exception

**Well** can also be an adjective meaning "healthy."

> I am **well**.

> She doesn't feel **well**.

---

# 19. Hard vs. Hardly

These words have very different meanings.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **hard** | with a lot of effort | He works **hard**. |
| **hardly** | almost not | He **hardly** works. |

### Compare

> She works **hard**.

→ She puts in a lot of effort.

> She **hardly** works.

→ She almost does not work.

---

# 20. Late vs. Lately

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **late** | after the expected time | He arrived **late**. |
| **lately** | recently | Have you seen him **lately**? |

---

# 21. Near vs. Nearly

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **near** | close | The school is **near**. |
| **nearly** | almost | It is **nearly** finished. |

# 22. Adjective or Adverb?

Some words can function as either adjectives or adverbs.

| Word | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|
| fast | a fast car | run fast |
| hard | hard work | work hard |
| early | an early train | arrive early |
| late | a late train | arrive late |
| daily | a daily routine | exercise daily |
| weekly | a weekly meeting | meet weekly |

### Example

> It is a **fast** car.  
> → adjective

> The car moves **fast**.  
> → adverb

# 23. Adverb Position

Adverbs can appear in different positions depending on their type.

| Type | Common Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Manner | Usually after verb/object | She speaks **quietly**. |
| Place | Usually after verb/object | He lives **here**. |
| Time | Often beginning/end | I saw him **yesterday**. |
| Frequency | Usually before main verb | She **often** studies. |
| Degree | Before adjective/adverb | very **slowly** |
| Certainty | Before main verb | He **probably** knows. |
| Comment | Often beginning | **Fortunately**, we arrived. |

---

# 24. Manner + Place + Time

When several adverbs occur at the end of a sentence, a common order is:

**Manner → Place → Time**

### Example

> She worked **quietly** **at home** **yesterday**.

| Adverb | Type |
|---|---|
| quietly | Manner |
| at home | Place |
| yesterday | Time |

Another example:

> He drove **carefully** **to the office** **this morning**.

---

# 25. Adverbs With Auxiliary Verbs

Adverbs of frequency and certainty often appear between an auxiliary verb and the main verb.

> She has **never seen** it.

> He will **probably come**.

> They can **always help**.

### Pattern

**Subject + auxiliary + adverb + main verb**

Time, place, and manner adverbs commonly occur at the end.

> She sings **beautifully**.

# 28. Adverbs in Questions

Adverbs can be used to ask about circumstances.

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **How** did you do it? | Manner |
| **When** did you arrive? | Time |
| **Where** do you live? | Place |
| **Why** did you leave? | Reason |
| **How often** do you exercise? | Frequency |
| **How quickly** can you finish? | Degree/speed |

### Examples

> **How** did she solve the problem?

> **Where** are you going?

> **When** will they arrive?

> **Why** are you laughing?

---

# 29. Adverbs in Negative Sentences

Some adverbs are commonly used with negative meanings.

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| never | I **never** smoke. |
| hardly | I can **hardly** hear you. |
| rarely | She **rarely** complains. |
| seldom | He **seldom** travels. |
| barely | We **barely** arrived on time. |

### Important

Words such as **never**, **hardly**, and **rarely** already have a negative meaning.

Avoid unnecessary double negatives.

> ❌ I don't never go there.

> ✅ I never go there.

---

# 30. Comparative Adverbs

Some adverbs can be compared.

### Short Adverbs

Use **-er / -est** with some short adverbs.

| Base | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| fast | faster | fastest |
| hard | harder | hardest |
| early | earlier | earliest |
| soon | sooner | soonest |

### Examples

> He runs **faster than** me.

> She arrived **earlier than** us.

---

# 31. More and Most With Adverbs

Many adverbs ending in **-ly** use **more** and **most**.

| Base | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| carefully | more carefully | most carefully |
| slowly | more slowly | most slowly |
| beautifully | more beautifully | most beautifully |
| seriously | more seriously | most seriously |

### Examples

> She works **more carefully than** he does.

> He completed the task **most efficiently**.

---

# 32. Irregular Comparative Adverbs

Some adverbs have irregular forms.

| Base | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| well | better | best |
| badly | worse | worst |
| far | farther/further | farthest/furthest |
| little | less | least |
| much | more | most |

### Examples

> She sings **better than** me.

> He performed **the best**.

> I slept **worse** last night.

# 33. Adverbs and Adjectives: Quick Comparison

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quick | quickly | He is quick. / He runs quickly. |
| careful | carefully | She is careful. / She works carefully. |
| beautiful | beautifully | She is beautiful. / She sings beautifully. |
| happy | happily | He is happy. / He smiled happily. |
| good | well | She is good. / She works well. |

### Easy Rule

**Adjective → describes a noun or follows a linking verb**

**Adverb → usually describes a verb, adjective, adverb, or sentence**

---

# 34. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| She sings **beautiful**. | She sings **beautifully**. | The word describes the verb "sings". |
| He drives **careful**. | He drives **carefully**. | "Carefully" describes how he drives. |
| She is **beautifully**. | She is **beautiful**. | Use an adjective after "be". |
| He runs **quick**. | He runs **quickly**. | "Quickly" is the adverb. |
| She works **hardly**. | She works **hard**. | "Hardly" means almost not. |
| He did **good**. | He did **well**. | "Well" is the standard adverb here. |
| I **always am** tired. | I am **always** tired. | Frequency adverb normally follows "be". |
| He **is often going** there. | He often goes there. | Position depends on tense and meaning. |
| She is **very quickly**. | She is very quick. | "Quickly" cannot normally follow "be" to describe the subject. |

---

# 35. Adverb vs. Adjective After Linking Verbs

After a linking verb, use an **adjective**, not an adverb, when describing the subject.

| Linking Verb | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| look | You look **good**. | You look **well**. |
| feel | I feel **bad**. | I feel **badly**. |
| smell | It smells **nice**. | It smells nicely. |
| taste | It tastes **good**. | It tastes well. |
| sound | That sounds **great**. | That sounds greatly. |
| seem | She seems **happy**. | She seems happily. |

> Note: Some of these verbs can have different meanings in different contexts. For example, **feel well** can mean "be healthy."

# 36. Adverbs Modifying Adjectives

An adverb can describe the **degree of an adjective**.

### Structure

**Adverb + Adjective**

| Adverb | Adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| very | good | very good |
| extremely | difficult | extremely difficult |
| really | interesting | really interesting |
| quite | easy | quite easy |
| too | expensive | too expensive |
| incredibly | beautiful | incredibly beautiful |

---

# 37. Adverbs Modifying Other Adverbs

An adverb can also describe the degree of another adverb.

### Structure

**Adverb + Adverb**

> She runs **very quickly**.

> He works **extremely carefully**.

> They arrived **quite early**.

| Modifier | Adverb | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| very | quickly | very quickly |
| extremely | carefully | extremely carefully |
| quite | slowly | quite slowly |
| really | well | really well |

---

# 38. Adverbs Modifying Whole Sentences

Some adverbs express the speaker's attitude toward the entire sentence.

> **Fortunately**, nobody was injured.

> **Unfortunately**, we missed the bus.

> **Clearly**, he needs help.

> **Probably**, they will arrive tomorrow.

These are often called **sentence adverbs** or **disjunct adverbs**.

---

# 39. Adverb Clauses

An **adverb clause** is a group of words that functions like an adverb.

It can show:

- time
- reason
- condition
- contrast
- purpose

| Type | Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time | when | I'll call you **when I arrive**. |
| Reason | because | I stayed home **because I was sick**. |
| Condition | if | I'll go **if you come**. |
| Contrast | although | **Although it was raining**, we went out. |
| Purpose | so that | I studied **so that I could pass**. |

---

# 40. Adverbs vs. Adverb Phrases

An **adverb** is one word.

> She works **quickly**.

An **adverb phrase** contains multiple words.

> She works **very quickly**.

> She works **at home**.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Adverb | quickly |
| Adverb phrase | very quickly |
| Adverb phrase | at home |
| Adverb clause | because she was tired |

# 45. The Most Important Rules

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Adverbs can describe verbs | She runs **quickly**. |
| Adverbs can describe adjectives | very **happy** |
| Adverbs can describe other adverbs | very **quickly** |
| Adverbs can modify whole sentences | **Fortunately**, we won. |
| Many manner adverbs end in **-ly** | careful → carefully |
| Not all adverbs end in **-ly** | fast, hard, well |
| Frequency adverbs usually come before the main verb | She **often studies**. |
| Frequency adverbs usually come after "be" | She is **often** late. |
| Manner adverbs often come after the verb/object | He speaks **quietly**. |
| Place and time adverbs often come at the end | She went **there yesterday**. |
| "Good" is an adjective | She is a **good** singer. |
| "Well" is usually an adverb | She sings **well**. |
| "Hard" ≠ "hardly" | He works **hard**. |
| "Late" ≠ "lately" | He arrived **late**. |
| "Near" ≠ "nearly" | It is **nearly** finished. |

# 46. Easy Way to Remember

> **Manner = How?** → quickly, carefully, slowly

> **Place = Where?** → here, there, everywhere

> **Time = When?** → now, yesterday, soon

> **Frequency = How often?** → always, often, sometimes, never

> **Degree = How much?** → very, too, extremely, enough

> **Certainty = How sure?** → definitely, probably, perhaps

### Basic Formula

**Verb + adverb**

> She speaks **quietly**.

**Adverb + adjective**

> She is **very happy**.

**Adverb + adverb**

> She speaks **very quietly**.

**Frequency adverb + main verb**

> She **often speaks** English.

**Subject + be + frequency adverb**

> She is **always** happy.
