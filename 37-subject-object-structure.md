# Subject and Object Structure in English

## 1. What Are Subject and Object?

The **subject** is the person, thing, or idea that **does the action** or is being described.

The **object** is the person or thing that **receives the action**.

### Basic Structure

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

### Example

> **Sarah** reads **a book**.

- **Sarah** = Subject
- **reads** = Verb
- **a book** = Object

---

# 2. Subject vs. Object

| Part | Function | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Does the action or is described | Who? / What? | **Tom** opened the door. |
| **Verb** | Shows action or state | What happens? | Tom **opened** the door. |
| **Object** | Receives the action | Whom? / What? | Tom opened **the door**. |

---

# 3. Basic Sentence Structures

| Structure | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject + Verb** | S + V | **Birds fly.** |
| **Subject + Verb + Object** | S + V + O | **She reads books.** |
| **Subject + Verb + Complement** | S + V + C | **He is happy.** |
| **Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object** | S + V + IO + DO | **She gave me a gift.** |
| **Subject + Verb + Object + Complement** | S + V + O + C | **They made him captain.** |
| **Subject + Verb + Adverbial** | S + V + A | **She lives in London.** |

---

# 4. Subject

A **subject** usually comes before the verb in an English sentence.

| Type of Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| Noun | **John** works here. |
| Pronoun | **She** is a doctor. |
| Noun phrase | **The young boy** is sleeping. |
| Gerund | **Swimming** is good exercise. |
| Infinitive | **To travel** is exciting. |
| Clause | **What he said** was surprising. |

### Examples

> **The teacher** explained the lesson.

> **She** works at a hospital.

> **Learning English** takes time.

---

# 5. Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns are used as the subject of a sentence.

| Person | Subject Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| First person singular | **I** | **I** study English. |
| Second person | **you** | **You** look tired. |
| Third person singular | **he** | **He** works here. |
| Third person singular | **she** | **She** is a teacher. |
| Third person singular | **it** | **It** is cold. |
| First person plural | **we** | **We** live here. |
| Third person plural | **they** | **They** play football. |

### Important

Use **subject pronouns** before the verb:

> ✅ **She** likes coffee.

> ❌ **Her** likes coffee.

---

# 6. Object

An **object** receives the action of the verb.

### Structure

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

| Example | Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| She reads books. | She | reads | books |
| John opened the door. | John | opened | the door |
| They watched a movie. | They | watched | a movie |
| I bought a car. | I | bought | a car |

### Question

Ask:

> **Who?** or **What?** after the verb.

Example:

> She bought **a phone**.

She bought **what?**

→ **a phone** = object.

---

# 7. Direct Object

A **direct object** directly receives the action of the verb.

### Structure

> **Subject + Verb + Direct Object**

| Example | Direct Object |
|---|---|
| I read **a book**. | a book |
| She opened **the door**. | the door |
| They watched **a movie**. | a movie |
| He bought **a new car**. | a new car |
| We ate **dinner**. | dinner |

---

# 8. Object Pronouns

Object pronouns are used after verbs and prepositions.

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **me** | She called **me**. |
| you | **you** | I saw **you**. |
| he | **him** | We helped **him**. |
| she | **her** | I met **her**. |
| it | **it** | I fixed **it**. |
| we | **us** | They invited **us**. |
| they | **them** | She called **them**. |

### Compare

> **She** likes **him**.

- She = subject
- him = object

> **They** helped **us**.

- They = subject
- us = object

---

# 9. Subject and Object Pronouns

| Subject | Object | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | **I** like **her**. |
| you | you | **You** like **me**. |
| he | him | **He** called **us**. |
| she | her | **She** helped **him**. |
| it | it | **It** needs **it**. |
| we | us | **We** invited **them**. |
| they | them | **They** visited **us**. |

### Easy Rule

> **Subject → before the verb**

> **Object → after the verb or preposition**

---

# 10. Indirect Object

An **indirect object** usually tells us **who receives something** or **who benefits from an action**.

### Structure

> **Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

| Example | Indirect Object | Direct Object |
|---|---|---|
| She gave **me** a book. | me | a book |
| He sent **her** a letter. | her | a letter |
| They bought **us** dinner. | us | dinner |
| I told **him** the truth. | him | the truth |

---

# 11. Two Ways to Use an Indirect Object

### Structure 1

> **Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

> She gave **me** **a book**.

### Structure 2

> **Subject + Verb + Direct Object + to/for + Indirect Object**

> She gave **a book** **to me**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| S + V + IO + DO | She gave **me a book**. |
| S + V + DO + to + IO | She gave **a book to me**. |
| S + V + IO + DO | He bought **her a gift**. |
| S + V + DO + for + IO | He bought **a gift for her**. |

---

# 12. Verbs with Two Objects

Some verbs commonly take an indirect and direct object.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| give | She **gave me a book**. |
| send | He **sent me an email**. |
| show | She **showed us her house**. |
| tell | He **told me the truth**. |
| buy | I **bought her a gift**. |
| bring | They **brought us some food**. |
| lend | She **lent me her car**. |
| teach | He **taught us English**. |
| offer | They **offered me a job**. |
| ask | She **asked me a question**. |

---

# 13. Subject Complement

A **subject complement** gives information about the subject. It usually follows a **linking verb** such as:

> be, seem, become, feel, look, appear, remain

### Structure

> **Subject + Linking Verb + Subject Complement**

| Example | Subject Complement |
|---|---|
| She is **a doctor**. | a doctor |
| He is **happy**. | happy |
| They became **friends**. | friends |
| The soup smells **good**. | good |
| She looks **tired**. | tired |

### Important

The complement does **not** receive an action.

> She **is** happy.

**happy** describes **she**.

---

# 14. Object Complement

An **object complement** gives more information about the object.

### Structure

> **Subject + Verb + Object + Object Complement**

| Example | Object | Object Complement |
|---|---|---|
| They made **him** **captain**. | him | captain |
| We painted **the door** **red**. | the door | red |
| She found **the book** **interesting**. | the book | interesting |
| They elected **John** **president**. | John | president |

### Example

> They painted **the house** **blue**.

- They = Subject
- painted = Verb
- the house = Object
- blue = Object Complement

---

# 15. Subject + Verb

Some verbs do not need an object.

### Structure

> **S + V**

| Example |
|---|
| **Birds fly.** |
| **The baby cried.** |
| **She laughed.** |
| **He arrived.** |
| **They slept.** |

These verbs are often called **intransitive verbs**.

---

# 16. Subject + Verb + Object

Some verbs need an object to complete the meaning.

### Structure

> **S + V + O**

| Example |
|---|
| **She opened the door.** |
| **He reads books.** |
| **They watched TV.** |
| **I like coffee.** |
| **We need help.** |

These verbs are often called **transitive verbs**.

---

# 17. Subject + Linking Verb + Complement

### Structure

> **S + Linking Verb + C**

| Example |
|---|
| **She is happy.** |
| **He became a doctor.** |
| **They seem tired.** |
| **The food smells delicious.** |
| **The children look excited.** |

---

# 18. Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object

### Structure

> **S + V + IO + DO**

| Example |
|---|
| **She gave me a present.** |
| **He sent her an email.** |
| **They taught us English.** |
| **I bought my mother a gift.** |

---

# 19. Subject + Verb + Object + Complement

### Structure

> **S + V + O + C**

| Example |
|---|
| **They made him angry.** |
| **We painted the wall green.** |
| **She found the movie boring.** |
| **They elected him president.** |

---

# 20. Subject + Verb + Adverbial

Some sentences contain an adverbial that tells us **where, when, how, or under what circumstances** something happens.

### Structure

> **S + V + A**

| Example |
|---|
| **She lives in London.** |
| **He works at home.** |
| **They arrived yesterday.** |
| **We stayed there.** |

---

# 21. Subject in Questions

In a normal question, the auxiliary verb often comes before the subject.

### Example

> **Does Sarah like coffee?**

| Part | Word |
|---|---|
| Auxiliary | Does |
| Subject | Sarah |
| Verb | like |
| Object | coffee |

### More Examples

> **Did John buy the car?**

> **Are they studying English?**

> **Has she finished the work?**

---

# 22. Subject Questions

When the **question word itself is the subject**, we usually do not use **do/does/did**.

### Structure

> **Who/What + verb...?**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Who called you?** | John called me. |
| **Who broke the window?** | Tom broke it. |
| **What happened?** | An accident happened. |
| **Who lives here?** | Sarah lives here. |

### Compare

**Subject question:**

> **Who called you?**

"Who" = subject.

**Object question:**

> **Who did you call?**

"Who" = object.

---

# 23. Object Questions

When the question word is the object, use an auxiliary verb when required.

### Structure

> **Wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb...?**

| Question | Object |
|---|---|
| **Who did you meet?** | Who |
| **What did she buy?** | What |
| **Who does he like?** | Who |
| **What are they watching?** | What |
| **Which book did you read?** | Which book |

---

# 24. Subject vs. Object Questions

| Subject Question | Object Question |
|---|---|
| **Who called you?** | **Who did you call?** |
| Who = subject | Who = object |
| **Who broke the window?** | **Who did you see?** |
| Who = subject | Who = object |
| **What happened?** | **What did you buy?** |
| What = subject | What = object |

### Easy Test

Ask:

> **Who/what did the action?** → Subject

> **Who/what received the action?** → Object

---

# 25. Compound Subjects

A sentence can have two or more subjects.

### Structure

> **Subject + and + Subject + Verb**

| Example |
|---|
| **Tom and Mary** are friends. |
| **My brother and I** play football. |
| **The dog and the cat** are sleeping. |

---

# 26. Compound Objects

A sentence can have two or more objects.

### Structure

> **Subject + Verb + Object + and + Object**

| Example |
|---|
| She bought **bread and milk**. |
| I like **coffee and tea**. |
| He invited **John and Mary**. |
| We watched **a movie and a documentary**. |

---

# 27. Subject and Object in Passive Voice

In an active sentence, the object can become the subject of a passive sentence.

### Active

> **The teacher** praised **the student**.

- Subject = the teacher
- Object = the student

### Passive

> **The student** was praised by **the teacher**.

- Subject = the student
- Agent = the teacher

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The boy kicked the ball. | The ball was kicked by the boy. |
| Sarah wrote the letter. | The letter was written by Sarah. |
| They built the house. | The house was built by them. |

---

# 29. Common Subject/Object Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Me like English. | ✅ **I like English.** | Use subject **I**. |
| ❌ Him called me. | ✅ **He called me.** | Use subject **he**. |
| ❌ She likes I. | ✅ **She likes me.** | Use object **me**. |
| ❌ They helped we. | ✅ **They helped us.** | Use object **us**. |
| ❌ Me and John went home. | ✅ **John and I went home.** | Use subject pronoun. |
| ❌ Between you and I | ✅ Between you and **me** | After a preposition, use object form. |
| ❌ Her is a teacher. | ✅ **She is a teacher.** | Subject = she. |
| ❌ Him is my brother. | ✅ **He is my brother.** | Subject = he. |

# 30. Quick Sentence Structure Summary

| Pattern | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S + V** | Subject + Verb | **Birds fly.** |
| **S + V + O** | Subject + Verb + Object | **She reads books.** |
| **S + V + C** | Subject + Verb + Complement | **He is happy.** |
| **S + V + IO + DO** | Two objects | **She gave me a book.** |
| **S + V + O + C** | Object + complement | **They made him captain.** |
| **S + V + A** | Subject + Verb + Adverbial | **She lives in London.** |
| **S + V + O + A** | Object + Adverbial | **She put the book on the table.** |

---

# 31. How to Find the Subject and Object

Use these steps:

| Step | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find the verb. | Sarah **opened** the door. |
| 2 | Ask **Who/What does the action?** | **Sarah** |
| 3 | Identify the subject. | Sarah = Subject |
| 4 | Ask **Who/What receives the action?** | **the door** |
| 5 | Identify the object. | the door = Object |

### Example

> **The boy kicked the ball.**

- Who kicked? → **The boy** = Subject
- What did he kick? → **The ball** = Object
- Action → **kicked** = Verb

---

# 32. Easy Way to Remember

| Part | Think of It As | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | The doer/topic | **John** opened the door. |
| **Verb** | The action/state | John **opened** the door. |
| **Object** | The receiver of the action | John opened **the door**. |
| **Direct Object** | Direct receiver | She bought **a car**. |
| **Indirect Object** | Receiver/beneficiary | She gave **me** a car. |
| **Complement** | Describes/identifies subject or object | He is **happy**. |
| **Adverbial** | Gives extra information | She lives **in London**. |

## Key Point

The most important pattern is:

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

> **She** + **reads** + **books**.

Remember:

> **Subject = Who/What does the action?**

> **Object = Who/What receives the action?**

> **Subject pronoun:** *I, he, she, we, they*

> **Object pronoun:** *me, him, her, us, them*
