# Imperatives in English

## 1. What Is an Imperative?

An **imperative** is a sentence used to give a **command, instruction, request, warning, advice, or invitation**.

The subject is usually understood to be **you**.

### Examples

> **Open** the door.

> **Please sit** down.

> **Don't touch** that.

> **Be** careful!

---

## 2. Summary of Imperatives

| Type | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Affirmative imperative** | Gives a command or instruction | **Open** the door. |
| **Negative imperative** | Tells someone not to do something | **Don't open** the door. |
| **Please + imperative** | Makes a command more polite | **Please sit** down. |
| **Let's + verb** | Makes a suggestion involving the speaker and others | **Let's go** home. |
| **Let's not + verb** | Makes a negative suggestion | **Let's not argue**. |
| **Be + adjective** | Gives advice or a warning | **Be careful**. |
| **Don't + be + adjective** | Tells someone not to be in a condition | **Don't be late**. |
| **Do + verb** | Adds emphasis or encouragement | **Do come** in. |
| **Imperative + and** | Shows a result | **Study hard, and** you will pass. |
| **Imperative + or** | Shows a warning or consequence | **Hurry up, or** you'll miss the bus. |

---

# 3. Affirmative Imperatives

An affirmative imperative uses the **base form of the verb**.

### Structure

> **Base verb + ...**

### Examples

> **Open** the window.

> **Listen** carefully.

> **Sit** down.

> **Turn** left.

> **Take** your book.

| Verb | Imperative |
|---|---|
| open | **Open** the door. |
| listen | **Listen** carefully. |
| sit | **Sit** here. |
| turn | **Turn** right. |
| wait | **Wait** for me. |

The subject **you** is normally omitted.

> **You open the door.** → Statement

> **Open the door.** → Imperative

---

# 4. Negative Imperatives

A negative imperative tells someone **not to do something**.

### Structure

> **Don't + base verb + ...**

### Examples

> **Don't touch** that.

> **Don't run**.

> **Don't open** the door.

> **Don't forget** your keys.

| Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|
| Open the door. | **Don't open** the door. |
| Run here. | **Don't run** here. |
| Touch it. | **Don't touch** it. |
| Forget your keys. | **Don't forget** your keys. |

---

# 5. Imperatives with "Please"

**Please** makes an imperative more polite.

It can come at the **beginning or end** of the sentence.

### Examples

> **Please open** the window.

> **Please sit** down.

> Come in, **please**.

> Wait here, **please**.

| Direct | More Polite |
|---|---|
| Open the door. | **Please open** the door. |
| Sit down. | **Please sit** down. |
| Wait here. | **Please wait** here. |
| Help me. | **Please help** me. |

---

# 6. "Let's" for Suggestions

**Let's** means **let us** and is used to make a suggestion involving the speaker and other people.

### Structure

> **Let's + base verb**

### Examples

> **Let's go** home.

> **Let's watch** a movie.

> **Let's have** some coffee.

> **Let's study** together.

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| Suggest going somewhere | **Let's go** to the park. |
| Suggest an activity | **Let's play** football. |
| Suggest eating | **Let's have** lunch. |
| Suggest studying | **Let's study** English. |

---

# 7. Negative "Let's"

Use **Let's not** to make a negative suggestion.

### Structure

> **Let's not + base verb**

### Examples

> **Let's not argue**.

> **Let's not be** late.

> **Let's not forget** the meeting.

> **Let's not talk** about it now.

| Positive Suggestion | Negative Suggestion |
|---|---|
| Let's go. | **Let's not go.** |
| Let's argue. | **Let's not argue.** |
| Let's wait. | **Let's not wait.** |
| Let's worry. | **Let's not worry.** |

---

# 8. Imperatives with "Be"

Use **be + adjective** to give advice, instructions, or warnings.

### Examples

> **Be careful!**

> **Be quiet.**

> **Be patient.**

> **Be honest.**

> **Be polite.**

| Purpose | Example |
|---|---|
| Warning | **Be careful!** |
| Request | **Be quiet, please.** |
| Advice | **Be patient.** |
| Behavior | **Be polite.** |
| Attitude | **Be positive.** |

---

# 9. Negative "Be" Imperatives

Use:

> **Don't be + adjective**

### Examples

> **Don't be late.**

> **Don't be angry.**

> **Don't be afraid.**

> **Don't be rude.**

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Be late. | **Don't be late.** |
| Be angry. | **Don't be angry.** |
| Be afraid. | **Don't be afraid.** |
| Be rude. | **Don't be rude.** |

---

# 10. Imperatives with "Do"

**Do + base verb** can add emphasis, encouragement, or politeness.

### Examples

> **Do come** in.

> **Do be** careful.

> **Do tell** me what happened.

> **Do have** some cake.

This form is more common in **formal, polite, or emphatic English**.

---

# 11. Imperatives with "And"

An imperative followed by **and** can show a result.

### Structure

> **Imperative + and + result**

### Examples

> **Study hard, and** you will pass.

> **Hurry up, and** we can catch the bus.

> **Follow me, and** I'll show you the way.

| Imperative | Result |
|---|---|
| **Study hard, and** | you will pass. |
| **Hurry up, and** | we can catch the bus. |
| **Follow me, and** | I'll help you. |

---

# 12. Imperatives with "Or"

An imperative followed by **or** can show a warning or consequence.

### Structure

> **Imperative + or + consequence**

### Examples

> **Hurry up, or** you'll miss the bus.

> **Study hard, or** you may fail.

> **Wear a coat, or** you'll be cold.

| Instruction | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Hurry up, or** | you'll miss the bus. |
| **Study hard, or** | you may fail. |
| **Be careful, or** | you might fall. |

---

# 13. Imperatives for Instructions

### Examples

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|

# 14. Imperatives for Warnings

Imperatives can be used to warn someone about danger.

> **Be careful!**

> **Don't touch that!**

> **Watch out!**

> **Stay away!**

> **Don't go near the fire!**

| Warning | Example |
|---|---|

| Dangerous place | **Stay away!** |

> **Study** regularly.

> **Don't worry** too much.

| Advice | Example |
|---|---|
| Health | **Drink** more water. |

| Study | **Practice** every day. |

---

# 16. Imperatives for Requests

Imperatives can be used to make requests, especially with **please**.

### Examples

> **Please help** me.

> **Please wait** here.

> **Please give** me a minute.

> **Please speak** slowly.

For a more formal or softer request, English often uses:

> **Could you...?**

> **Would you...?**

### Compare

| Direct | Polite |
|---|---|
| Open the window. | **Please open** the window. |
| Help me. | **Could you help** me? |
| Wait here. | **Would you wait** here, please? |

---

# 17. Imperative with "Let"

**Let + object + base verb** can be used to give permission, instructions, or suggestions.

### Examples

> **Let me help** you.

> **Let him go**.

> **Let them speak**.

> **Let us know** if you need help.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Let + me + verb | **Let me explain.** |
| Let + him + verb | **Let him speak.** |
| Let + her + verb | **Let her decide.** |
| Let + them + verb | **Let them go.** |

## Key Point

An imperative normally uses the **base form of the verb** and does not state the subject **you**.

> **Open** the door.  
> **Don't open** the door.  
> **Please open** the door.  
> **Let's open** the door.

The basic patterns to remember are:

> **Verb** → command or instruction  
> **Don't + verb** → negative command  
> **Please + verb** → polite request  
> **Let's + verb** → suggestion  
> **Be + adjective** → advice or warning
