# Participles in English

## 1. What Is a Participle?

A **participle** is a form of a verb that can be used:

- to form verb tenses
- to form the passive voice
- as an adjective
- in participle phrases

There are two main types:

1. **Present participle** → verb + **-ing**
2. **Past participle** → usually the **-ed** form for regular verbs, but irregular verbs have different forms.

| Type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present participle** | verb + -ing | working, running, studying |
| **Past participle** | usually V3 | worked, written, eaten |
| **Perfect participle** | having + V3 | having finished |
| **Passive participle** | being + V3 | being invited |
| **Perfect passive participle** | having been + V3 | having been invited |

---

# 2. Present Participles

A **present participle** is formed with:

> **verb + -ing**

### Examples

> running  
> eating  
> studying  
> working  
> sleeping

Present participles can be used to form **continuous tenses** or act as **adjectives**.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Continuous tense | She is **studying**. |
| Adjective | The **crying** baby needs milk. |
| Participle phrase | **Walking home**, I saw John. |

---

# 3. Present Participle in Continuous Tenses

The present participle is used with forms of **be** to make continuous tenses.

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present continuous | am/is/are + -ing | She is **working**. |
| Past continuous | was/were + -ing | She was **working**. |
| Future continuous | will be + -ing | She will be **working**. |
| Present perfect continuous | have/has been + -ing | She has been **working**. |
| Past perfect continuous | had been + -ing | She had been **working**. |

### Example

> They **are playing** football.

**playing** is a present participle.

---

# 4. Present Participle as an Adjective

A present participle can describe a noun.

### Examples

> a **boring** movie

> an **interesting** book

> a **crying** baby

> a **sleeping** child

| Verb | Present Participle | Example |
|---|---|---|
| bore | boring | It is a **boring** movie. |
| interest | interesting | It is an **interesting** book. |
| excite | exciting | It was an **exciting** game. |
| surprise | surprising | That is a **surprising** result. |
| tire | tiring | It was a **tiring** journey. |

### Important

The **-ing adjective** usually describes the **thing or person that causes the feeling**.

> The movie is **boring**.

> The lesson is **interesting**.

---

# 5. Past Participles

A **past participle** is the **third form of a verb (V3)**.

For regular verbs:

> **verb + -ed**

For irregular verbs, the form changes.

| Base Verb | Past Simple | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| work | worked | worked |
| play | played | played |
| clean | cleaned | cleaned |
| go | went | gone |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| write | wrote | written |
| see | saw | seen |
| take | took | taken |
| break | broke | broken |
| speak | spoke | spoken |

---

# 6. Past Participle in Perfect Tenses

Past participles are used with **have** to form perfect tenses.

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present perfect | have/has + V3 | She has **finished**. |
| Past perfect | had + V3 | She had **finished**. |
| Future perfect | will have + V3 | She will have **finished**. |

### Examples

> I have **eaten** breakfast.

> She had **left** before I arrived.

> They will have **finished** the work by Friday.

---

# 7. Past Participle in Passive Voice

Past participles are used with **be** to form the passive voice.

### Structure

> **be + past participle**

### Examples

> The window was **broken**.

> The book was **written** by George Orwell.

> The car is **washed** every week.

| Tense | Passive Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | am/is/are + V3 | The room is **cleaned**. |
| Past simple | was/were + V3 | The room was **cleaned**. |
| Present continuous | am/is/are being + V3 | The room is being **cleaned**. |
| Present perfect | has/have been + V3 | The room has been **cleaned**. |
| Future | will be + V3 | The room will be **cleaned**. |

---

# 8. Past Participle as an Adjective

A past participle can function as an adjective.

### Examples

> a **broken** window

> a **written** report

> a **closed** door

> a **tired** student

| Past Participle | Example |
|---|---|
| broken | a **broken** chair |
| closed | a **closed** door |
| damaged | a **damaged** car |
| tired | a **tired** worker |
| excited | an **excited** child |
| interested | an **interested** student |
| bored | a **bored** student |
| confused | a **confused** customer |

---

# 9. -ing vs. -ed Adjectives

This is an important difference.

| -ing Adjective | -ed Adjective |
|---|---|
| Describes what **causes** a feeling | Describes the person/thing **experiencing** the feeling |
| The movie is **boring**. | I am **bored**. |
| The lesson is **interesting**. | The students are **interested**. |
| The game is **exciting**. | We are **excited**. |
| The news is **surprising**. | She is **surprised**. |
| The journey is **tiring**. | He is **tired**. |

### Compare

> The movie is **boring**.  
> → The movie causes boredom.

> I am **bored**.  
> → I experience boredom.

---

# 10. Participle Phrases

A **participle phrase** is a phrase beginning with a present or past participle.

### Present Participle Phrase

> **Walking through the park**, I saw my friend.

### Past Participle Phrase

> **Shocked by the news**, she started crying.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Present participle phrase | **Walking home**, I met Tom. |
| Past participle phrase | **Tired after work**, he went to bed. |
| Present participle phrase | **Looking out the window**, she saw a bird. |
| Past participle phrase | **Built in 1900**, the house is very old. |

---

# 11. Present Participle Phrases

Present participle phrases often show an action happening **at the same time** as the main action.

### Examples

> **Walking down the street**, I saw Sarah.

> **Listening to music**, he did his homework.

> **Driving to work**, she called her mother.

| Participial Phrase | Main Clause |
|---|---|
| **Walking down the street** | I saw Sarah. |
| **Listening to music** | He did his homework. |
| **Driving to work** | She called her mother. |
| **Studying hard** | He passed the exam. |

---

# 12. Past Participle Phrases

Past participle phrases often have a **passive** or **completed** meaning.

### Examples

> **Tired from work**, he went home.

> **Written in simple English**, the book is easy to understand.

> **Surprised by the result**, she couldn't speak.

| Participial Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tired from work** | He was tired from work. |
| **Written in English** | It was written in English. |
| **Surprised by the result** | She was surprised by the result. |
| **Built in 1990** | It was built in 1990. |

---

# 13. Perfect Participles

A **perfect participle** shows that one action happened **before** another action.

### Structure

> **having + past participle**

### Examples

> **Having finished** her homework, she watched TV.

> **Having eaten** dinner, they went for a walk.

> **Having completed** the project, he went home.

| Perfect Participle | Example |
|---|---|
| having finished | **Having finished** work, she went home. |
| having eaten | **Having eaten** dinner, we left. |
| having completed | **Having completed** the task, he rested. |
| having seen | **Having seen** the movie, I understood the story. |

---

# 14. Perfect Passive Participles

The perfect passive participle is:

> **having been + past participle**

It shows that something was completed **before another action** and has a passive meaning.

### Examples

> **Having been invited** to the party, she bought a new dress.

> **Having been warned** about the danger, he stayed away.

> **Having been selected** for the team, he was very happy.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| having been + V3 | **Having been invited**, she attended the party. |
| having been + V3 | **Having been warned**, he was careful. |
| having been + V3 | **Having been chosen**, they celebrated. |

---

# 15. Passive Participles

A passive participle phrase can begin with:

> **being + past participle**

### Examples

> **Being invited** to the event, she felt happy.

> **Being surrounded** by mountains, the town is beautiful.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| being + V3 | **Being surrounded** by trees, the house is peaceful. |
| being + V3 | **Being asked** to help, he agreed. |
| being + V3 | **Being watched**, the players felt nervous. |

In many cases, English prefers a simpler past participle phrase:

> **Surrounded by trees**, the house is peaceful.

---

# 16. Participle Clauses

A **participle clause** can replace a longer clause and make writing more concise.

### Full Clause

> Because she was tired, she went home.

### Participle Clause

> **Being tired**, she went home.

### Examples

| Full Clause | Participle Clause |
|---|---|
| Because he was tired, he went home. | **Being tired**, he went home. |
| After she finished her work, she left. | **Having finished her work**, she left. |
| Because he was shocked by the news, he couldn't speak. | **Shocked by the news**, he couldn't speak. |
| While she was walking home, she called me. | **Walking home**, she called me. |

---

# 17. Participle Clauses for Time

Participles can express time relationships.

### Examples

> **Walking home**, I saw John.

> **Having finished dinner**, we watched a movie.

> **After finishing the work**, she went home.

| Relationship | Example |
|---|---|
| Same time | **Walking home**, I called him. |
| Earlier action | **Having finished work**, she went home. |
| After | **After completing** the task, he left. |
| Before | **Before leaving**, turn off the lights. |

---

# 18. Participle Clauses for Reason

A participle clause can give a reason.

### Examples

> **Feeling tired**, I went to bed.

Meaning:

> Because I felt tired, I went to bed.

> **Not knowing the answer**, she stayed quiet.

Meaning:

> Because she didn't know the answer, she stayed quiet.

| Full Clause | Participle Clause |
|---|---|
| Because I was tired, I slept. | **Feeling tired**, I slept. |
| Because she was confused, she asked a question. | **Being confused**, she asked a question. |
| Because he didn't know the answer, he stayed silent. | **Not knowing the answer**, he stayed silent. |

---

# 19. Negative Participles

The negative form is usually:

> **not + participle**

### Examples

> **Not knowing** what to do, he called his friend.

> **Not having finished** the work, she stayed late.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| knowing | **not knowing** |
| having finished | **not having finished** |
| understanding | **not understanding** |
| being invited | **not being invited** |

---

# 20. Present Participle vs. Gerund

Both use the **-ing** form, but they have different functions.

| Form | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Gerund** | Functions as a noun | **Swimming** is fun. |
| **Present participle** | Forms a continuous tense | She is **swimming**. |
| **Present participle** | Acts as an adjective | The **swimming** boy waved. |
| **Present participle** | Begins a participle phrase | **Swimming across the lake**, he became tired. |

### Compare

> **Swimming** is healthy.  
> → Gerund; subject of the sentence.

> She is **swimming**.  
> → Present participle; part of the verb.

> The **swimming** boy is my brother.  
> → Present participle; describes "boy."

---

# 21. Participle vs. Infinitive

| Form | Example | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Infinitive** | I want **to study**. | Verb complement |
| **Present participle** | I am **studying**. | Continuous tense |
| **Past participle** | I have **studied**. | Perfect tense |
| **Past participle** | The work was **studied**. | Passive voice |
| **Gerund** | **Studying** is useful. | Noun |

---

# 22. Dangling Participles

A participle phrase should clearly describe the **subject of the main clause**.

### Incorrect

> **Walking down the street**, the rain started.

This suggests that **the rain was walking**.

### Correct

> **Walking down the street**, I got caught in the rain.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| **Driving to work**, the traffic was terrible. | **Driving to work**, I found the traffic terrible. |
| **Walking home**, the rain started. | **Walking home**, I got caught in the rain. |
| **Looking out the window**, the mountains were beautiful. | **Looking out the window**, I saw the beautiful mountains. |

### Key Rule

The subject of the main clause should normally be the person or thing performing the participial action.

# 24. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| She is **study** English. | She is **studying** English. | Continuous tense needs -ing. |
| I have **wrote** a letter. | I have **written** a letter. | Perfect tense needs the past participle. |
| The window was **broke**. | The window was **broken**. | Passive voice needs the past participle. |
| I am **interesting** in English. | I am **interested** in English. | A person usually experiences the feeling. |
| The movie was **interested**. | The movie was **interesting**. | The movie causes the feeling. |
| **Walking home, the rain started.** | **Walking home, I got wet.** | Avoid a dangling participle. |

---

# 25. How to Identify a Participle

Ask these questions:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| Is it an **-ing** form? | running |
| Is it a **V3** form? | written |
| Is it helping form a continuous tense? | is **running** |
| Is it helping form a perfect tense? | has **written** |
| Is it helping form the passive? | was **written** |
| Is it describing a noun? | a **broken** window |
| Is it beginning a participle phrase? | **Walking home**, I saw him. |

# 26. Participles in One Sentence

Consider:

> **Walking through the park**, I saw a **broken** bicycle while some children were **playing** nearby.

| Word/Phrase | Type | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Walking through the park** | Present participle phrase | Describes the action of "I" |
| **broken** | Past participle | Describes "bicycle" |
| **playing** | Present participle | Part of the past continuous tense |

# 28. Easy Way to Remember

| Participle | Think of It As |
|---|---|
| **Present participle (-ing)** | An action in progress or a description |
| **Past participle (V3)** | A completed/passive form or description |
| **Perfect participle** | An earlier completed action |
| **Passive participle** | A passive action |
| **Participle phrase** | A shortened clause |

### Main Patterns

> **Continuous:**  
> She is **studying**.

> **Perfect:**  
> She has **studied**.

> **Passive:**  
> The lesson was **studied**.

> **Adjective:**  
> It was an **interesting** lesson.

> **Present participle phrase:**  
> **Studying hard**, she passed the exam.

> **Past participle phrase:**  
> **Tired after studying**, she went to bed.

> **Perfect participle:**  
> **Having finished** her homework, she watched TV.

## Key Point

A **participle** is a verb form that can help form **tenses and passive voice** or function as an **adjective/participle phrase**.

> **Present participle = verb + -ing**

> **Past participle = V3**

> **Perfect participle = having + V3**

> **Passive participle = being + V3**

> **Perfect passive participle = having been + V3**
