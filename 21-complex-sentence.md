# Complex Sentences in English

## 1. What Is a Complex Sentence?

A **complex sentence** contains:

- **One independent clause** (main clause)
- **At least one dependent clause** (subordinate clause)

### Basic Structure

> **Independent Clause + Dependent Clause**

or

> **Dependent Clause + Independent Clause**

### Examples

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

> **Because it was raining**, I stayed home.

- **I stayed home** → Independent clause
- **because it was raining** → Dependent clause

---

# 2. Summary of Complex Sentences

| Feature | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | One independent clause + one or more dependent clauses | I stayed home because it rained. |
| **Main clause** | Can stand alone | I stayed home. |
| **Dependent clause** | Cannot normally stand alone | because it rained |
| **Common connectors** | because, although, if, when, while, since, unless | I left because I was tired. |
| **Main purpose** | Shows relationships such as time, reason, condition, contrast, and purpose | I left because I was tired. |

---

# 3. Independent and Dependent Clauses

| Clause Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent clause** | Complete idea | I went home. |
| **Dependent clause** | Incomplete idea | because I was tired |
| **Complex sentence** | Both clauses together | I went home because I was tired. |

### Example

> **I went home** because **I was tired**.

| Clause | Type |
|---|---|
| I went home | Independent |
| because I was tired | Dependent |

---

# 4. Common Structure of Complex Sentences

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Independent + dependent | I stayed home **because it was raining**. |
| Dependent + independent | **Because it was raining**, I stayed home. |
| Independent + relative clause | The man **who lives next door** is a doctor. |
| Independent + noun clause | I know **what he wants**. |

---

# 5. Complex Sentences with "Because"

**Because** introduces a clause that gives a **reason**.

### Examples

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

> She was late **because she missed the bus**.

> **Because it was raining**, we canceled the picnic.

| Main Clause | Reason Clause |
|---|---|
| I stayed home | because I was sick |
| She was late | because she missed the bus |
| we canceled the picnic | Because it was raining |

---

# 6. Complex Sentences with "Although"

**Although** introduces a clause that shows **contrast**.

### Examples

> **Although it was raining**, we went outside.

> I went to work **although I was tired**.

> **Although the exam was difficult**, she passed.

| Contrast Clause | Main Clause |
|---|---|
| Although it was raining | we went outside |
| Although I was tired | I went to work |
| Although the exam was difficult | she passed |

---

# 7. Complex Sentences with "If"

**If** introduces a **condition**.

### Examples

> **If you study hard**, you will pass.

> I will call you **if I have time**.

> **If it rains**, we will stay home.

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| If you study hard | you will pass |
| If I have time | I will call you |
| If it rains | we will stay home |

---

# 8. Complex Sentences with "When"

**When** introduces a **time clause**.

### Examples

> Call me **when you arrive**.

> **When I got home**, I ate dinner.

> I was sleeping **when she called**.

| Time Clause | Main Clause |
|---|---|
| when you arrive | Call me |
| When I got home | I ate dinner |
| when she called | I was sleeping |

---

# 9. Complex Sentences with "While"

**While** usually shows that two things happen at the same time.

### Examples

> I listened to music **while I was studying**.

> **While she was cooking**, I cleaned the kitchen.

> He fell asleep **while he was watching TV**.

| While Clause | Main Clause |
|---|---|
| while I was studying | I listened to music |
| While she was cooking | I cleaned the kitchen |
| while he was watching TV | He fell asleep |

---

# 10. Complex Sentences with "Before" and "After"

These conjunctions show **sequence of events**.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **before** | Wash your hands **before you eat**. |
| **after** | I went home **after I finished work**. |
| **before** | **Before I left**, I locked the door. |
| **after** | **After she arrived**, we started dinner. |

---

# 11. Complex Sentences with "Unless"

**Unless** means **if not**.

### Examples

> **Unless you hurry**, you will be late.

> I won't go **unless you come with me**.

> **Unless it rains**, we will play outside.

| Unless Clause | Main Clause |
|---|---|
| Unless you hurry | you will be late |
| unless you come with me | I won't go |
| Unless it rains | we will play outside |

---

# 12. Complex Sentences with "Since"

**Since** can show **time** or **reason**.

### Time

> I have lived here **since I was a child**.

### Reason

> **Since it was late**, we went home.

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| **Time** | I have known him **since we were children**. |
| **Reason** | **Since it was raining**, we stayed inside. |

---

# 13. Adverb Clauses in Complex Sentences

Adverb clauses give information about the main clause.

| Type | Common Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, while, before, after, until | Call me **when you arrive**. |
| **Reason** | because, since, as | I stayed home **because I was sick**. |
| **Condition** | if, unless | **If you study**, you will pass. |
| **Contrast** | although, though, even though | **Although it was cold**, we went out. |
| **Purpose** | so that, in order that | I studied **so that I could pass**. |
| **Place** | where, wherever | Sit **wherever you like**. |
| **Result** | so...that, such...that | It was **so cold that** we stayed home. |

---

# 14. Relative Clauses in Complex Sentences

A **relative clause** describes or identifies a noun.

Common relative words:

> who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when

### Examples

> The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> I bought the book **that you recommended**.

> This is the house **where I grew up**.

| Noun | Relative Clause | Function |
|---|---|---|
| woman | who lives next door | Describes the woman |
| book | that you recommended | Describes the book |
| house | where I grew up | Gives information about the house |

---

# 15. Noun Clauses in Complex Sentences

A **noun clause** functions like a noun.

### Examples

> I know **what he wants**.

> She explained **why she was late**.

> **What he said** was true.

| Noun Clause | Function |
|---|---|
| **what he wants** | Object |
| **why she was late** | Object |
| **What he said** | Subject |

---

# 16. Commas in Complex Sentences

When a dependent clause comes **before** the independent clause, a comma is normally used.

### Dependent Clause First

> **Because I was tired,** I went to bed.

> **Although it was raining,** we went outside.

### Independent Clause First

> I went to bed **because I was tired**.

> We went outside **although it was raining**.

| Order | Example | Comma |
|---|---|---|
| Dependent + Independent | **Because I was tired,** I went home. | Usually yes |
| Independent + Dependent | I went home **because I was tired**. | Usually no |

---

# 17. Complex Sentence vs. Compound Sentence

| Sentence Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | 1 independent clause | I studied. |
| **Compound** | 2+ independent clauses | I studied, and I passed. |
| **Complex** | 1 independent + 1+ dependent | I passed because I studied. |
| **Compound-complex** | 2+ independent + 1+ dependent | I studied because I wanted to pass, and I succeeded. |

### Compare

**Compound:**

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

Both parts are independent clauses.

**Complex:**

> I continued working **although I was tired**.

The second part is a dependent clause.

---

# 18. Complex Sentence vs. Phrase

A **phrase** does not normally contain a subject and a verb.

A **dependent clause** contains a subject and a verb.

| Type | Example | Subject + Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Phrase | **because of the rain** | No |
| Phrase | **after lunch** | No |
| Dependent clause | **because it was raining** | Yes |
| Dependent clause | **after I ate lunch** | Yes |

### Compare

> **Because of the rain**, we stayed home. → Phrase

> **Because it was raining**, we stayed home. → Dependent clause

# 21. How to Identify a Complex Sentence

Ask these questions:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the sentence contain a subject and verb? | Yes |
| Is there one independent clause? | Yes |
| Is there at least one dependent clause? | Yes |
| Is the dependent clause introduced by a word such as because, although, if, when, or who? | Often yes |
| Can the dependent clause normally stand alone? | No |

### Example

> **Although I was tired, I finished my homework.**

1. **Although I was tired** → Dependent clause
2. **I finished my homework** → Independent clause
3. **Although** → Subordinating conjunction
4. Therefore, it is a **complex sentence**.

# 23. Easy Way to Remember

| Type | Think of It As |
|---|---|
| **Independent clause** | Complete idea |
| **Dependent clause** | Incomplete idea |
| **Complex sentence** | Complete idea + incomplete idea |
| **Adverb clause** | Gives time, reason, condition, contrast, etc. |
| **Relative clause** | Describes a noun |
| **Noun clause** | Acts like a noun |

## Key Point

A **complex sentence** contains **one independent clause and at least one dependent clause**.

### Main Patterns

> **Independent + Dependent**  
> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

> **Dependent + Independent**  
> **Because it was raining**, I stayed home.

> **Independent + Relative Clause**  
> The man **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> **Independent + Noun Clause**  
> I know **what he wants**.
