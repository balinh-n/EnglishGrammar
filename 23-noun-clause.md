# Noun Clauses in English

## 1. What Is a Noun Clause?

A **noun clause** is a dependent clause that functions like a **noun** in a sentence.

It can work as a:

- Subject
- Object
- Subject complement
- Object of a preposition

### Examples

> **What she said** was true.

> I know **what she said**.

> The problem is **that we are late**.

A noun clause usually contains a **subject and a verb**.

---

# 2. Summary of Noun Clauses

| Feature | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | A dependent clause that functions like a noun | I know **what he wants**. |
| **As subject** | Acts as the subject | **What he said** was true. |
| **As object** | Acts as the object of a verb | I know **what he said**. |
| **As complement** | Completes the meaning of the subject | The problem is **that we are late**. |
| **After a preposition** | Acts as the object of a preposition | It depends on **what you want**. |
| **Common words** | what, who, whom, whose, which, when, where, why, how, that, whether, if | I know **where she lives**. |

---

# 3. Common Words That Introduce Noun Clauses

| Word | Meaning / Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **what** | Thing / information | I know **what he wants**. |
| **who** | Person | I don't know **who called**. |
| **whom** | Person as object | Tell me **whom you invited**. |
| **whose** | Possession | I know **whose bag this is**. |
| **which** | Choice / specific thing | Tell me **which you prefer**. |
| **when** | Time | I don't know **when she will arrive**. |
| **where** | Place | Do you know **where he lives**? |
| **why** | Reason | I understand **why she left**. |
| **how** | Method / manner | Tell me **how you did it**. |
| **that** | Statement / fact | I know **that he is busy**. |
| **whether** | Alternative / uncertainty | I don't know **whether he will come**. |
| **if** | Alternative / uncertainty | I don't know **if he will come**. |

---

# 4. Noun Clauses as Subjects

A noun clause can function as the **subject** of a sentence.

### Examples

> **What he said** was true.

> **Where she lives** is unknown.

> **Why he left** is a mystery.

> **How they solved the problem** is interesting.

| Noun Clause | Main Verb |
|---|---|
| **What he said** | was |
| **Where she lives** | is |
| **Why he left** | is |
| **How they solved the problem** | is |

### Pattern

> **Noun Clause + Verb + ...**

Example:

> **What you do** is important.

---

# 5. Noun Clauses as Objects

A noun clause can function as the **object of a verb**.

### Examples

> I know **what he wants**.

> She explained **why she was late**.

> We don't know **where they went**.

> He told me **that he was busy**.

| Main Verb | Noun Clause |
|---|---|
| know | **what he wants** |
| explained | **why she was late** |
| don't know | **where they went** |
| told | **that he was busy** |

### Pattern

> **Subject + Verb + Noun Clause**

Example:

> I understand **what you mean**.

---

# 6. Noun Clauses as Subject Complements

A noun clause can come after a linking verb such as **be** and give more information about the subject.

### Examples

> The problem is **that we are late**.

> The question is **whether he will come**.

> The truth is **that nobody knows**.

> The important thing is **what we do next**.

| Subject | Linking Verb | Noun Clause |
|---|---|---|
| The problem | is | **that we are late** |
| The question | is | **whether he will come** |
| The truth | is | **that nobody knows** |
| The important thing | is | **what we do next** |

---

# 7. Noun Clauses After Prepositions

A noun clause can be the **object of a preposition**.

### Examples

> It depends on **what you want**.

> We talked about **what happened**.

> She is interested in **how the machine works**.

> It depends on **whether he agrees**.

| Preposition | Noun Clause |
|---|---|
| on | **what you want** |
| about | **what happened** |
| in | **how the machine works** |
| on | **whether he agrees** |

---

# 8. Noun Clauses with "What"

**What** often means **the thing(s) that**.

### Examples

> I know **what you mean**.

> **What she said** surprised me.

> Take **what you need**.

> This is **what I wanted**.

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **What you need** | The thing that you need |
| **What she said** | The thing that she said |
| **What I wanted** | The thing that I wanted |

---

# 9. Noun Clauses with "Who"

**Who** introduces a noun clause referring to a person.

### Examples

> I don't know **who called me**.

> Tell me **who you saw**.

> **Who will win** is uncertain.

> I know **who she is**.

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject | **Who will win** is uncertain. |
| Object | I don't know **who called**. |
| Object | Tell me **who you saw**. |

---

# 10. Noun Clauses with "Whom"

**Whom** is used for people when the person is the **object**.

It is more formal than **who**.

### Examples

> I don't know **whom she invited**.

> Tell me **whom you saw**.

> We need to decide **whom we should contact**.

In everyday English, **who** is often used instead:

> I don't know **who she invited**.

---

# 11. Noun Clauses with "Whose"

**Whose** shows possession.

### Examples

> I don't know **whose bag this is**.

> Can you tell me **whose car that is**?

> She asked **whose phone was ringing**.

| Noun Clause | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **whose bag this is** | Whose bag it is |
| **whose car that is** | Whose car it is |
| **whose phone was ringing** | Who owned the phone |

---

# 12. Noun Clauses with "Which"

**Which** is used when choosing between specific possibilities.

### Examples

> I don't know **which one she wants**.

> Tell me **which color you prefer**.

> We need to decide **which route we should take**.

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **which one she wants** | Which one she wants |
| **which color you prefer** | Which color you prefer |
| **which route we should take** | Which route we should take |

---

# 13. Noun Clauses with "When"

**When** introduces a noun clause about **time**.

### Examples

> I don't know **when he will arrive**.

> Tell me **when the meeting starts**.

> **When she will arrive** is uncertain.

### Important

Compare:

> I don't know **when he will arrive**. → Noun clause

> Call me **when he arrives**. → Adverb clause

---

# 14. Noun Clauses with "Where"

**Where** introduces a noun clause about **place**.

### Examples

> I don't know **where she lives**.

> Tell me **where you bought it**.

> We need to decide **where we should go**.

### Compare

> I know **where he lives**. → Noun clause

> I will visit you **wherever you live**. → Adverb clause

---

# 15. Noun Clauses with "Why"

**Why** introduces a noun clause that gives a **reason**.

### Examples

> I understand **why she left**.

> Tell me **why you are angry**.

> Nobody knows **why he disappeared**.

> **Why she left** remains a mystery.

---

# 16. Noun Clauses with "How"

**How** introduces a noun clause about **method, manner, or process**.

### Examples

> I know **how he solved the problem**.

> Tell me **how you did it**.

> She explained **how the machine works**.

> **How they escaped** is still unknown.

---

# 17. Noun Clauses with "That"

**That** introduces a noun clause containing a statement or fact.

### Examples

> I know **that he is busy**.

> She said **that she was tired**.

> We believe **that he is honest**.

> The fact is **that we are late**.

### Important

**That** can often be omitted when the noun clause is the object.

> I know **that he is busy**.

> I know **he is busy**.

Both are correct.

---

# 18. Noun Clauses with "Whether"

**Whether** expresses uncertainty between alternatives.

### Examples

> I don't know **whether he will come**.

> We need to decide **whether we should stay**.

> It depends on **whether she agrees**.

> I am not sure **whether they are ready**.

---

# 19. Noun Clauses with "If"

**If** can also introduce a noun clause expressing uncertainty.

### Examples

> I don't know **if he will come**.

> She asked **if I was ready**.

> We don't know **if they are open**.

### Whether vs. If

| Whether | If |
|---|---|
| More formal | More common in everyday speech |
| Can be used before **or not** | Less commonly used before **or not** |
| I don't know **whether he will come or not**. | I don't know **if he will come**. |
| Used after a preposition | Generally not used after a preposition |

---

# 20. Noun Clauses with "Whether...or"

**Whether...or** presents two or more alternatives.

### Examples

> I don't know **whether he will come or stay**.

> We must decide **whether to go or wait**.

> She asked **whether I wanted tea or coffee**.

| Alternative | Example |
|---|---|
| Come / stay | whether he will **come or stay** |
| Go / wait | whether to **go or wait** |
| Tea / coffee | whether I wanted **tea or coffee** |

---

# 21. Statement Order in Noun Clauses

Noun clauses use **statement word order**, not question word order.

### Correct

> I don't know **where he lives**.

> Do you know **what she wants**?

> I wonder **why he left**.

### Incorrect

> ❌ I don't know **where does he live**.

> ❌ Do you know **what does she want**?

> ❌ I wonder **why did he leave**.

| Direct Question | Noun Clause |
|---|---|
| Where **does he live**? | I know where **he lives**. |
| What **does she want**? | I know what **she wants**. |
| Why **did he leave**? | I know why **he left**. |
| When **will they arrive**? | I know when **they will arrive**. |

---

# 22. Direct Questions vs. Noun Clauses

| Direct Question | Noun Clause |
|---|---|
| Where does he live? | I know **where he lives**. |
| What does she want? | I know **what she wants**. |
| Why did they leave? | I know **why they left**. |
| When will he arrive? | I know **when he will arrive**. |
| Is he coming? | I don't know **if he is coming**. |

### Key Rule

> **Question word + subject + verb**

Not:

> ❌ Question word + auxiliary + subject + verb

---

# 23. Noun Clause vs. Question

A noun clause is **not itself a question**, even when it contains words such as who, what, where, or why.

### Direct Question

> **Where does she live?**

### Noun Clause

> I know **where she lives**.

The noun clause is part of the larger sentence.

---

# 24. Noun Clauses vs. Relative Clauses

These two types of clauses can look similar, but they have different functions.

| Noun Clause | Relative Clause |
|---|---|
| Functions like a noun | Describes a noun |
| I know **what he wants**. | The book **that he wants** is expensive. |
| **What she said** was true. | The woman **who spoke** is my teacher. |
| Usually cannot directly follow a noun to describe it | Usually follows a noun |

### Compare

> I know **what he wants**. → Noun clause

> I know the book **that he wants**. → Relative clause

---

# 25. Noun Clauses vs. Adverb Clauses

| Noun Clause | Adverb Clause |
|---|---|
| Functions like a noun | Modifies a verb, adjective, or sentence |
| I know **when he arrived**. | I left **when he arrived**. |
| I know **where she lives**. | I will go **where she lives**. |
| I understand **why he left**. | I stayed home **because he left**. |

### Important

The same word can introduce different types of clauses depending on its function.

---

# 26. Noun Clauses in One Sentence

Consider this sentence:

> I don't know **what she wants**, but I understand **why she is upset**.

| Noun Clause | Function |
|---|---|
| **what she wants** | Object of "know" |
| **why she is upset** | Object of "understand" |

Another example:

> **What he said** was true, and I understand **why he said it**.

| Clause | Function |
|---|---|
| **What he said** | Subject |
| **why he said it** | Object |

---

# 27. Common Verbs Followed by Noun Clauses

Many verbs can be followed by noun clauses.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| **know** | I know **what he wants**. |
| **understand** | I understand **why she left**. |
| **think** | I think **that he is right**. |
| **believe** | I believe **that she is honest**. |
| **remember** | I remember **what you said**. |
| **forget** | Don't forget **what I told you**. |
| **wonder** | I wonder **where he is**. |
| **ask** | She asked **whether I was ready**. |
| **explain** | He explained **how it works**. |
| **decide** | We decided **what we should do**. |
| **tell** | Tell me **what you need**. |
| **discover** | We discovered **where they went**. |

---

# 28. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| I don't know **where does he live**. | I don't know **where he lives**. | Use statement word order. |
| She asked **what did I want**. | She asked **what I wanted**. | No question inversion. |
| I know **what does she need**. | I know **what she needs**. | Use subject + verb order. |
| I don't know **if will he come**. | I don't know **if he will come**. | Subject comes before verb. |
| I know **that where he lives**. | I know **where he lives**. | Don't normally use both together. |

---

# 29. How to Identify a Noun Clause

Ask these questions:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| Does the clause contain a subject and a verb? | **what she wants** |
| Does it function like a noun? | I know **what she wants**. |
| Can it be the subject? | **What she wants** is unknown. |
| Can it be the object? | I know **what she wants**. |
| Can it follow a linking verb? | The problem is **that we are late**. |
| Does it often begin with what, who, where, when, why, how, that, whether, or if? | I know **where he lives**. |

## Key Point

A **noun clause** is a dependent clause that **acts like a noun**.

### Main Patterns

> **Noun clause as subject**  
> **What she said** was true.

> **Noun clause as object**  
> I know **what she said**.

> **Noun clause as complement**  
> The problem is **that we are late**.

> **Noun clause after a preposition**  
> It depends on **what you want**.

### Most Important Grammar Rule

Noun clauses use **statement word order**:

> ❌ I don't know **where does he live**.

> ✅ I don't know **where he lives**.

> ❌ Tell me **what did she say**.

> ✅ Tell me **what she said**.
