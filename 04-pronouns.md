# Pronouns in English

## 1. What Is a Pronoun?

A **pronoun** is a word that replaces a noun or noun phrase, usually to avoid repetition.

### Example

> Maria is a teacher. **She** works at a school.

- **Maria** = noun
- **She** = pronoun replacing "Maria"

| Pronoun Type | Main Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | Refers to people or things | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| **Object** | Refers to the receiver of an action | me, you, him, her, it, us, them |
| **Possessive** | Shows ownership | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| **Reflexive** | Refers back to the subject | myself, yourself, himself |
| **Demonstrative** | Points to people or things | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | Asks questions | who, whom, whose, what, which |
| **Relative** | Connects a relative clause to a noun | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| **Indefinite** | Refers to people or things generally | someone, anyone, everyone, something |
| **Reciprocal** | Shows a mutual relationship | each other, one another |
| **Distributive** | Refers to members individually | each, either, neither |

---

# 2. Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns refer to specific people or things.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **1st person** | I | we |
| **2nd person** | you | you |
| **3rd person** | he, she, it | they |

### Examples

> **I** am a student.

> **She** works here.

> **They** live in London.

---

# 3. Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns are used as the **subject** of a sentence.

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1st singular | **I** | **I** study English. |
| 2nd singular/plural | **you** | **You** look tired. |
| 3rd singular masculine | **he** | **He** works here. |
| 3rd singular feminine | **she** | **She** is a doctor. |
| 3rd singular thing/animal | **it** | **It** is cold. |
| 1st plural | **we** | **We** live here. |
| 3rd plural | **they** | **They** are students. |

### Basic Structure

**Subject Pronoun + Verb**

> **She** works.

> **They** study.

---

# 4. Object Pronouns

Object pronouns are used as the **object of a verb or preposition**.

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **me** | She called **me**. |
| you | **you** | I called **you**. |
| he | **him** | I saw **him**. |
| she | **her** | We invited **her**. |
| it | **it** | I bought **it**. |
| we | **us** | They helped **us**. |
| they | **them** | I know **them**. |

### Compare

> **She** called **me**.

- She = subject
- me = object

> **They** invited **us**.

- They = subject
- us = object

---

# 5. Subject vs. Object Pronouns

| Subject | Object | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | **I** like **her**. |
| you | you | **You** like **him**. |
| he | him | **He** likes **me**. |
| she | her | **She** likes **them**. |
| it | it | **It** needs **it**. |
| we | us | **We** like **you**. |
| they | them | **They** like **us**. |

### Easy Rule

**Subject → does the action**

> **He** called me.

**Object → receives the action**

> He called **me**.

---

# 6. Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns show **ownership**.

They stand alone and are not followed by a noun.

| Person | Possessive Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **mine** | This book is **mine**. |
| you | **yours** | This bag is **yours**. |
| he | **his** | The car is **his**. |
| she | **hers** | The phone is **hers**. |
| we | **ours** | The house is **ours**. |
| they | **theirs** | The seats are **theirs**. |

### Important

Do not confuse possessive pronouns with possessive determiners.

| Possessive Determiner | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|
| my | mine |
| your | yours |
| his | his |
| her | hers |
| our | ours |
| their | theirs |

### Examples

> This is **my book**.

> This book is **mine**.

> That is **their car**.

> That car is **theirs**.

---

# 7. Reflexive Pronouns

Reflexive pronouns refer back to the subject.

| Subject | Reflexive Pronoun |
|---|---|
| I | **myself** |
| you | **yourself** |
| he | **himself** |
| she | **herself** |
| it | **itself** |
| we | **ourselves** |
| you | **yourselves** |
| they | **themselves** |

### Use 1: The subject and object are the same person

> I hurt **myself**.

> She introduced **herself**.

> They blamed **themselves**.

### Use 2: Emphasis

> I did it **myself**.

> The president **himself** opened the building.

---

# 8. Demonstrative Pronouns

Demonstrative pronouns point to specific people or things.

| Pronoun | Number | Distance | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **this** | Singular | Near | **This** is my book. |
| **that** | Singular | Far | **That** is my car. |
| **these** | Plural | Near | **These** are my shoes. |
| **those** | Plural | Far | **Those** are expensive. |

### Compare

**Pronoun:**

> **This** is beautiful.

**Determiner:**

> **This book** is beautiful.

When **this/that/these/those** stand alone, they are demonstrative pronouns.

---

# 9. Interrogative Pronouns

Interrogative pronouns are used to ask questions.

| Pronoun | Main Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | Person | **Who** called you? |
| **whom** | Person as object | **Whom** did you invite? |
| **whose** | Possession | **Whose** is this? |
| **what** | Thing/information | **What** happened? |
| **which** | Choice | **Which** do you prefer? |

### Who vs. Whom

**Who** is normally used as a subject:

> **Who** called you?

**Whom** is traditionally used as an object:

> **Whom** did you call?

In everyday modern English, **who** is commonly used instead of **whom**:

> Who did you call?

---

# 10. Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns introduce **relative clauses** and connect them to a noun.

| Pronoun | Used For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | People | The woman **who** lives here is a doctor. |
| **whom** | People as objects | The man **whom** I met was friendly. |
| **whose** | Possession | The student **whose** book was lost is upset. |
| **which** | Things/animals | The car **which** I bought is red. |
| **that** | People/things in many defining clauses | The book **that** I bought is interesting. |

### Example

> The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.

- woman = antecedent
- who = relative pronoun
- who lives next door = relative clause

---

# 11. Indefinite Pronouns

Indefinite pronouns refer to people or things without identifying exactly who or what.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **People** | someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, everyone, everybody, no one, nobody |
| **Things** | something, anything, everything, nothing |
| **General** | each, either, neither, both, few, many, several, all, some, none |

### Examples

> **Someone** is calling.

> **Everyone** is ready.

> I don't know **anything**.

> **Nothing** happened.

---

# 12. Indefinite Pronouns Ending in -one / -body

These are normally grammatically **singular**.

| Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|
| everyone | Everyone **is** ready. |
| everybody | Everybody **likes** music. |
| someone | Someone **has** called. |
| somebody | Somebody **is** outside. |
| anyone | Anyone **can** join. |
| anybody | Anybody **can** participate. |
| no one | No one **knows** the answer. |
| nobody | Nobody **was** there. |

### Important

Although they refer to people generally, they take a **singular verb**.

> Everyone **is** happy.

Not:

> ❌ Everyone **are** happy.

---

# 13. Gender-Neutral "They"

**They/them/their** can be used for one person when the person's gender is unknown, irrelevant, or when the person uses they/them pronouns.

> Someone left **their** phone here.

> If a student has a question, **they** can ask me.

This is called **singular they**.

---

# 14. Reciprocal Pronouns

Reciprocal pronouns show that two or more people do something **to each other**.

The two main forms are:

- **each other**
- **one another**

### Examples

> Tom and Mary love **each other**.

> The students helped **one another**.

The distinction between **each other** and **one another** is often not important in modern English.

---

# 15. Distributive Pronouns

Distributive pronouns refer to people or things **individually**.

| Pronoun | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **each** | Every one individually | **Each** has a ticket. |
| **either** | One of two | **Either** is fine. |
| **neither** | Not one or the other | **Neither** is correct. |

### Examples

> **Each** of the students has a book.

> **Either** of these is acceptable.

> **Neither** of them is ready.

---

# 16. Pronoun + of + Noun

Some pronouns are commonly followed by **of + noun/pronoun**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| each of + plural noun | **Each of the students** is ready. |
| either of + plural noun | **Either of the options** is fine. |
| neither of + plural noun | **Neither of the answers** is correct. |
| both of + plural noun | **Both of the students** are ready. |
| many of + plural noun | **Many of the students** are absent. |
| some of + noun | **Some of the water** is gone. |
| all of + noun | **All of the information** is useful. |
| none of + noun | **None of the students** are here. |

---

# 17. Pronoun Agreement

A pronoun should normally agree with its antecedent in **number** and, where relevant, **person and gender**.

| Antecedent | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| John | he | John said **he** was tired. |
| Mary | she | Mary said **she** was tired. |
| The dog | it | The dog hurt **itself**. |
| The students | they | The students finished **their** work. |
| The company | it | The company changed **its** policy. |

### Incorrect

> ❌ The students finished **his** work.

### Correct

> ✅ The students finished **their** work.

---

# 18. Pronouns After Prepositions

Use an **object pronoun** after a preposition.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| between you and **I** | between you and **me** |
| for **she** | for **her** |
| with **he** | with **him** |
| to **they** | to **them** |
| from **we** | from **us** |

### Examples

> This is between you and **me**.

> She went with **him**.

> The gift is for **her**.

---

# 19. Pronouns After "Be"

In formal traditional grammar, subject forms can be used after **be** in certain constructions.

> It was **I** who called.

> It is **she**.

However, in normal modern conversation, object forms are very common:

> It was **me**.

> It's **her**.

Both styles occur, but **me/her/us/them** are especially common in everyday English.

---

# 20. Dummy "It"

Sometimes **it** does not refer to a particular thing. It is used as a grammatical subject.

### Weather

> **It** is raining.

> **It** is cold today.

### Time

> **It** is five o'clock.

### Distance

> **It** is five kilometers to the station.

In these examples, **it** does not refer to a specific object.

---

# 21. Dummy "There"

**There** can be used to introduce the existence of something.

> **There is** a problem.

> **There are** two problems.

Here, **there** is not referring to a location. It introduces the noun that follows.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| There **is** a book. | There **are** books. |
| There **was** a problem. | There **were** problems. |

---

# 22. Pronoun vs. Determiner

Some words can function as either a pronoun or a determiner.

| Word | Determiner | Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| this | **this book** | **This** is mine. |
| that | **that car** | **That** is expensive. |
| these | **these shoes** | **These** are new. |
| those | **those people** | **Those** are my friends. |
| which | **which book?** | **Which** do you want? |
| what | **what time?** | **What** happened? |
| whose | **whose car?** | **Whose** is this? |

### Easy Rule

If the word is followed by a noun, it often functions as a **determiner**.

> **This book** is mine.

If it stands alone, it can function as a **pronoun**.

> **This** is mine.

# 25. Common Pronoun Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Me** went to school. | **I** went to school. | Use a subject pronoun as the subject. |
| She gave it to **I**. | She gave it to **me**. | Use an object pronoun after a preposition. |
| John and **me** went home. | John and **I** went home. | The pronoun is part of the subject. |
| This is **my**. | This is **mine**. | Possessive pronoun stands alone. |
| This is **mine book**. | This is **my book**. | Use a possessive determiner before a noun. |
| She hurt **herself**. | She hurt **herself**. | Reflexive pronoun agrees with the subject. |
| Everyone should bring **his** book. | Everyone should bring **their** book. | Singular "they" is common for gender-neutral reference. |
| Between you and **I** | Between you and **me** | Object form follows a preposition. |
| The students finished **his** work. | The students finished **their** work. | Pronoun agrees with plural antecedent. |

# 26. Easy Way to Remember

| Question | Pronoun Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Who does the action?** | Subject pronoun | **She** called me. |
| **Who receives the action?** | Object pronoun | She called **me**. |
| **Whose?** | Possessive | The book is **mine**. |
| **The same person?** | Reflexive | She hurt **herself**. |
| **Which one?** | Demonstrative | **This** is mine. |
| **Who/what?** | Interrogative | **Who** called? |
| **Which noun?** | Relative | The man **who** called... |
| **Someone/anything?** | Indefinite | **Someone** called. |
| **Each other?** | Reciprocal | They helped **each other**. |
| **Each one individually?** | Distributive | **Each** has a ticket. |

## Key Rules

1. **Subject pronouns** do the action:
   - **She** works.

2. **Object pronouns** receive the action:
   - I called **her**.

3. **Possessive pronouns** show ownership:
   - The book is **hers**.

4. **Reflexive pronouns** refer back to the subject:
   - She taught **herself**.

5. **Demonstrative pronouns** point to something:
   - **This** is mine.

6. **Interrogative pronouns** ask questions:
   - **Who** is there?

7. **Relative pronouns** connect clauses:
   - The woman **who** called is my teacher.

8. **Indefinite pronouns** refer to people or things generally:
   - **Someone** is outside.

9. Pronouns should normally **agree with their antecedents**:
   - The students → **they/their**
   - John → **he/his**
   - Mary → **she/her**

10. After a **preposition**, use an object pronoun:
   - with **me**
   - for **her**
   - between you and **me**
