# Summary: Word Order in English

## 1. What Is Word Order?

**Word order** is the arrangement of words in a sentence. English usually follows a **fixed word order**, so changing the position of words can change the meaning or make the sentence incorrect.

### Basic Pattern

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

| Part | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Who/what does the action | **She** |
| **Verb** | Action/state | **reads** |
| **Object** | Receives the action | **books** |

**Example:**

> **She reads books.**

---

# 2. Basic Sentence Patterns

| Pattern | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **SV** | Subject + Verb | Birds **fly**. |
| **SVO** | Subject + Verb + Object | She **reads books**. |
| **SVC** | Subject + Verb + Complement | He **is happy**. |
| **SVOO** | Subject + Verb + Object + Object | She **gave me a gift**. |
| **SVOC** | Subject + Verb + Object + Complement | They **made him captain**. |
| **SVA** | Subject + Verb + Adverbial | She **lives in London**. |

---

# 3. Subject + Verb + Object

The most common English word order is:

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

| Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|
| I | eat | breakfast. |
| She | reads | books. |
| They | play | football. |
| Tom | bought | a car. |

### Example

> **Sarah studies English.**

❌ English studies Sarah.

---

# 4. Word Order with "Be"

With **be**, the structure is often:

> **Subject + be + Complement**

| Subject | Be | Complement |
|---|---|---|
| She | is | happy. |
| They | are | students. |
| He | was | tired. |
| I | am | ready. |

### Example

> **She is a doctor.**

---

# 5. Adjective Word Order

Adjectives usually come **before the noun** they describe.

> **adjective + noun**

| Example | Correct Order |
|---|---|
| a **big house** | adjective + noun |
| a **beautiful flower** | adjective + noun |
| an **interesting book** | adjective + noun |
| a **red car** | adjective + noun |

### Example

> She bought a **beautiful dress**.

❌ She bought a dress beautiful.

---

# 6. Order of Multiple Adjectives

When several adjectives describe one noun, they commonly follow this order:

> **Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose → Noun**

| Order | Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opinion | beautiful, lovely, ugly |
| 2 | Size | big, small, large |
| 3 | Age | old, young, new |
| 4 | Shape | round, square, thin |
| 5 | Color | red, blue, black |
| 6 | Origin | Vietnamese, French, American |
| 7 | Material | wooden, plastic, metal |
| 8 | Purpose | cooking, sports, sleeping |
| 9 | Noun | table, shoes, bag |

### Example

> a **beautiful small old round red Italian wooden table**

In normal conversation, we usually do not use so many adjectives together.

---

# 7. Adverb Word Order

Adverbs can appear in different positions depending on their type and function.

| Adverb Type | Common Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Manner** | Usually after the verb/object | She speaks **slowly**. |
| **Place** | Usually after the verb/object | He works **here**. |
| **Time** | Often at the end | We met **yesterday**. |
| **Frequency** | Usually before the main verb | She **often** studies. |
| **Degree** | Before adjective/adverb | It is **very** cold. |

---

# 8. Adverbs of Manner, Place, and Time

A common order is:

> **Manner → Place → Time**

### Example

> She sang **beautifully** **at the concert** **last night**.

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **beautifully** | Manner |
| **at the concert** | Place |
| **last night** | Time |

### Another Example

> He worked **quietly** **in his office** **yesterday**.

---

# 9. Frequency Adverbs

Common frequency adverbs include:

> always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never

They usually come **before the main verb**.

| Sentence | Position |
|---|---|
| She **always studies**. | Before main verb |
| He **often plays** football. | Before main verb |
| They **never eat** meat. | Before main verb |

### With "Be"

With **be**, the frequency adverb usually comes **after be**.

> She **is always** happy.

> He **is never** late.

---

# 10. Object Pronoun Word Order

Object pronouns normally come after the verb or preposition.

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | She called **me**. |
| you | you | I saw **you**. |
| he | him | We invited **him**. |
| she | her | He helped **her**. |
| we | us | They called **us**. |
| they | them | I met **them**. |

### Example

> She gave **him** a book.

---

# 11. Two Objects

Some verbs can take two objects.

### Pattern

> **Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

| Example | Indirect Object | Direct Object |
|---|---|---|
| She gave **me a book**. | me | a book |
| He bought **his mother a gift**. | his mother | a gift |
| They sent **us an email**. | us | an email |

### Alternative Pattern

> She gave **a book to me**.

---

# 12. Preposition Word Order

A preposition normally comes **before a noun or pronoun**.

> **preposition + noun/pronoun**

| Preposition | Example |
|---|---|
| in | **in the room** |
| on | **on the table** |
| at | **at school** |
| with | **with my friend** |
| for | **for you** |
| from | **from Canada** |

### Example

> The keys are **on the table**.

---

# 13. Negative Sentence Word Order

With **do/does/did**, use:

> **Subject + auxiliary + not + main verb**

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | S + do/does + not + V | She **does not like** coffee. |
| Past | S + did + not + V | He **did not go** home. |

### Important

After **doesn't/didn't**, use the **base form** of the verb.

> ✅ She **doesn't like** coffee.

> ❌ She doesn't **likes** coffee.

> ✅ He **didn't go**.

> ❌ He didn't **went**.

---

# 14. Questions

Questions often require a change in word order.

### Yes/No Questions

> **Auxiliary + Subject + Main Verb?**

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| You are ready. | **Are you ready?** |
| She is working. | **Is she working?** |
| They can swim. | **Can they swim?** |
| He likes coffee. | **Does he like coffee?** |

---

# 15. Wh-Questions

Common question words:

> who, what, where, when, why, which, how

### Basic Pattern

> **Wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb?**

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| **What** | What **do you want**? |
| **Where** | Where **does she live**? |
| **When** | When **did they arrive**? |
| **Why** | Why **are you crying**? |
| **How** | How **did he do it**? |

---

# 16. Subject Questions

When the question word itself is the subject, we usually **do not use do/does/did**.

| Question | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Who called you?** | Who = subject |
| **Who broke the window?** | Who = subject |
| **What happened?** | What = subject |

Compare:

> **Who called you?** → Who is the subject.

> **Who did you call?** → You is the subject; who is the object.

---

# 17. Imperatives

Commands usually begin with the **base form of the verb**.

> **Verb + object/complement**

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open the door.** | Command |
| **Sit down.** | Command |
| **Please call me.** | Polite request |
| **Don't touch that.** | Negative command |

---

# 18. There Is / There Are

Use:

> **There + be + noun**

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| There **is** a book. | There **are** two books. |
| There **is** a problem. | There **are** many problems. |

### Example

> **There are three students in the classroom.**

---

# 19. Comparative Structures

Common patterns include:

> **Subject + verb + comparative + than + noun/pronoun**

| Example | Structure |
|---|---|
| She is **taller than me**. | comparative + than |
| He runs **faster than I do**. | comparative + than |
| This book is **more interesting than that one**. | more + adjective + than |

---

# 20. "Too" and "Enough"

### Too

> **too + adjective/adverb**

> It is **too expensive**.

### Enough

> **adjective/adverb + enough**

> It is **cheap enough**.

Before a noun:

> **enough + noun**

> We have **enough money**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| too + adjective | too expensive |
| adjective + enough | cheap enough |
| enough + noun | enough time |
| verb + enough | She doesn't study enough. |

---

# 21. Word Order with "Only"

The position of **only** can change the meaning.

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Only John** passed the exam. | John was the only person who passed. |
| John **only passed** the exam. | He passed; the meaning focuses on the action. |
| John passed **only the exam**. | He passed the exam and nothing else. |

### 📌 Key Point

> Put **only** close to the word or phrase you want to limit.

---

# 22. Basic Order of Information

A useful general pattern is:

> **Subject + Verb + Object + Manner + Place + Time**

### Example

> She **finished her homework** **quickly** **at home** **last night**.

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject | She |
| Verb | finished |
| Object | her homework |
| Manner | quickly |
| Place | at home |
| Time | last night |

This is a **general guide**, not an absolute rule.

---

# 23. Common Word Order Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ I coffee drink. | ✅ I **drink coffee**. | S + V + O |
| ❌ She English speaks. | ✅ She **speaks English**. | S + V + O |
| ❌ He very is tired. | ✅ He **is very tired**. | Adverb before adjective |
| ❌ She always is late. | ✅ She **is always late**. | Frequency adverb after be |
| ❌ He doesn't likes it. | ✅ He doesn't **like** it. | Base verb after does |
| ❌ Where you do live? | ✅ Where **do you live?** | Auxiliary before subject |
| ❌ I went yesterday to school. | ✅ I went **to school yesterday**. | Natural place/time order |

---

# 24. Quick Summary

| **Element** | **Typical Position** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Beginning | **She** works. |
| **Verb** | After subject | She **works**. |
| **Object** | After verb | She reads **books**. |
| **Adjective** | Before noun | a **beautiful** house |
| **Manner adverb** | Often after object | She works **carefully**. |
| **Place** | Usually after manner | She works carefully **at home**. |
| **Time** | Often at the end | She works at home **every day**. |
| **Frequency adverb** | Before main verb | She **often works**. |
| **Frequency + be** | After be | She **is often** late. |
| **Preposition** | Before noun/pronoun | **in the room** |
| **Question auxiliary** | Before subject | **Do you** understand? |
| **Negative not** | After auxiliary | She **does not** agree. |

---

## ⭐ Key Point

> **English word order is usually fixed.**

### 💡 Easy Formula

**Statement:**

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

> She **reads books**.

**Negative:**

> **Subject + Auxiliary + not + Verb**

> She **doesn't read books**.

**Question:**

> **Auxiliary + Subject + Verb?**

> **Does she read books?**

**With extra information:**

> **Subject + Verb + Object + Manner + Place + Time**

> She **read the book carefully at home last night**.

### Remember

| **Sentence Type** | **Basic Word Order** |
|---|---|
| Statement | **S + V + O** |
| Negative | **S + Aux + not + V** |
| Yes/No question | **Aux + S + V?** |
| Wh-question | **Wh + Aux + S + V?** |
| Imperative | **V + Object** |
| Adjective phrase | **Adjective + Noun** |
| Common information order | **Manner + Place + Time** |

> **Good word order makes English sentences clear, natural, and grammatically correct.**
