# Adjectives in English

## 1. What Is an Adjective?

An **adjective** is a word that describes or gives more information about a **noun or pronoun**.

Adjectives can describe:

- quality
- size
- color
- shape
- age
- origin
- material
- opinion
- condition
- quantity

### Examples

> a **beautiful** flower

> a **large** house

> a **red** car

> The food is **delicious**.

| Noun | Adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| house | beautiful | a **beautiful house** |
| car | expensive | an **expensive car** |
| student | intelligent | an **intelligent student** |
| weather | cold | The weather is **cold**. |

---

# 2. Types of Adjectives

| Type | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | Describes quality or characteristic | beautiful, intelligent, difficult |
| **Size** | Describes size | big, small, large, tiny |
| **Age** | Describes age | old, young, new, ancient |
| **Color** | Describes color | red, blue, green, black |
| **Shape** | Describes shape | round, square, flat, narrow |
| **Origin** | Shows where something comes from | Vietnamese, American, Japanese |
| **Material** | Describes what something is made of | wooden, metal, plastic |
| **Opinion** | Gives an opinion | wonderful, terrible, amazing |
| **Condition** | Describes condition/state | broken, clean, dirty |
| **Number/quantity** | Gives information about amount | first, second, several |
| **Participial** | Formed from verbs | boring, interested, broken |
| **Compound** | Made from two or more words | well-known, full-time |

---

# 3. Adjectives Before Nouns

Adjectives commonly come **before a noun**.

### Structure

**Adjective + Noun**

> a **beautiful** house

> an **expensive** car

> a **young** teacher

> a **difficult** question

| Adjective | Noun | Full phrase |
|---|---|---|
| beautiful | house | a beautiful house |
| large | city | a large city |
| old | building | an old building |
| interesting | book | an interesting book |
| expensive | car | an expensive car |

---

# 4. Adjectives After Linking Verbs

Adjectives can come after **linking verbs** such as:

- be
- become
- seem
- look
- feel
- appear
- sound
- smell
- taste
- remain
- stay

### Structure

**Subject + Linking Verb + Adjective**

> She is **happy**.

> He looks **tired**.

> The food smells **good**.

> The idea sounds **interesting**.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| be | She is **happy**. |
| become | He became **angry**. |
| seem | It seems **difficult**. |
| look | You look **tired**. |
| feel | I feel **sick**. |
| sound | That sounds **great**. |
| smell | The food smells **delicious**. |
| taste | The soup tastes **good**. |

---

# 5. Adjectives Do Not Usually Change for Plural

English adjectives normally have the **same form** for singular and plural nouns.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| a **beautiful** flower | **beautiful** flowers |
| a **large** house | **large** houses |
| an **old** car | **old** cars |
| a **good** student | **good** students |

Do not add **-s** to adjectives.

> ❌ beautifuls flowers

> ✅ beautiful flowers

---

# 6. Adjective vs. Adverb

Adjectives describe nouns or follow linking verbs.

Adverbs usually describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quick | quickly | He is **quick**. / He runs **quickly**. |
| careful | carefully | She is **careful**. / She works **carefully**. |
| slow | slowly | He is **slow**. / He walks **slowly**. |
| happy | happily | She is **happy**. / She smiled **happily**. |

### Important

After a linking verb, use an adjective:

> She looks **beautiful**.

Not:

> ❌ She looks beautifully.

But:

> She sings **beautifully**.

Here **beautifully** describes the verb "sings."

# 8. Order of Adjectives

When several adjectives describe the same noun, English normally follows a particular order.

### Common Order

**Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose → Noun**

| Order | Category | Example |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Opinion | beautiful |
| 2 | Size | large |
| 3 | Age | old |
| 4 | Shape | round |
| 5 | Color | red |
| 6 | Origin | Italian |
| 7 | Material | wooden |
| 8 | Purpose | dining |
| 9 | Noun | table |

### Example

> a **beautiful large old round red Italian wooden dining table**

This many adjectives together is grammatically possible, although in normal English we usually use fewer adjectives.

---

# 9. Easy Adjective Order

A useful short version is:

> **Opinion → Size → Age → Color → Origin → Material → Noun**

### Example

> a **beautiful small old red Japanese wooden house**

| Word | Function |
|---|---|
| beautiful | Opinion |
| small | Size |
| old | Age |
| red | Color |
| Japanese | Origin |
| wooden | Material |
| house | Noun |

---

# 10. Coordinate Adjectives

Sometimes two or more adjectives have equal importance and are separated by **and** or a comma.

> a **long, difficult** journey

> a **cold, rainy** day

> a **kind and generous** person

### Test

If you can put **and** between the adjectives, they may be coordinate adjectives.

> a long and difficult journey

> a cold and rainy day

---

# 11. Comparative Adjectives

Comparative adjectives compare **two** people or things.

### Short Adjectives

Usually add **-er**.

| Base | Comparative |
|---|---|
| small | smaller |
| fast | faster |
| tall | taller |
| old | older |
| cheap | cheaper |

### Examples

> John is **taller than** Peter.

> This car is **cheaper than** that one.

---

# 12. Longer Comparative Adjectives

For many longer adjectives, use:

**more + adjective**

| Base | Comparative |
|---|---|
| beautiful | more beautiful |
| expensive | more expensive |
| interesting | more interesting |
| difficult | more difficult |
| important | more important |

### Examples

> This book is **more interesting than** that one.

> This car is **more expensive than** mine.

---

# 13. Superlative Adjectives

Superlatives compare **three or more** people or things and show the highest or lowest degree.

### Short Adjectives

Usually add **-est**.

| Base | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| small | smaller | smallest |
| fast | faster | fastest |
| tall | taller | tallest |
| old | older | oldest |
| cheap | cheaper | cheapest |

### Examples

> John is **the tallest** student in the class.

> This is **the cheapest** option.

---

# 14. Longer Superlative Adjectives

Use:

**the most + adjective**

| Base | Superlative |
|---|---|
| beautiful | the most beautiful |
| expensive | the most expensive |
| interesting | the most interesting |
| difficult | the most difficult |
| important | the most important |

### Examples

> She is **the most intelligent** student in the class.

> This is **the most expensive** car here.

---

# 15. Comparative and Superlative Summary

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| small | smaller | smallest |
| fast | faster | fastest |
| large | larger | largest |
| happy | happier | happiest |
| easy | easier | easiest |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| expensive | more expensive | most expensive |
| difficult | more difficult | most difficult |

### General Rule

| Adjective Type | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| Short adjective | **-er** | **-est** |
| Long adjective | **more** | **most** |

---

# 16. Irregular Adjectives

Some adjectives have irregular comparative and superlative forms.

| Base | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| far | farther/further | farthest/furthest |
| little | less | least |
| much | more | most |
| many | more | most |

### Examples

> This book is **better** than that one.

> She is the **best** student.

> Today is **worse** than yesterday.

> This is the **worst** day of my life.

---

# 17. Spelling Rules for Comparatives and Superlatives

## One-Syllable Adjectives

Usually add **-er/-est**.

> tall → taller → tallest

> short → shorter → shortest

## Adjectives Ending in -e

Add **-r/-st**.

> large → larger → largest

> nice → nicer → nicest

## Adjectives Ending in Consonant + y

Change **y → i**.

> happy → happier → happiest

> easy → easier → easiest

> busy → busier → busiest

## Short Adjectives Ending in Consonant-Vowel-Consonant

Often double the final consonant.

> big → bigger → biggest

> hot → hotter → hottest

> thin → thinner → thinnest

# 18. "Than" With Comparatives

Comparatives are often followed by **than**.

### Structure

**Subject + be + comparative + than + noun/pronoun**

> John is **taller than** Peter.

> This car is **more expensive than** that one.

| Comparative | Example |
|---|---|
| taller than | He is taller than me. |
| faster than | This car is faster than mine. |
| more interesting than | This book is more interesting than that one. |
| less expensive than | This option is less expensive than the other one. |

---

# 19. "The" With Superlatives

Superlatives normally use **the**.

> She is **the best** student.

> This is **the biggest** house.

> He is **the most experienced** person here.

### Pattern

**the + superlative adjective + noun**

> the tallest building

> the most beautiful city

---

# 20. Less and Least

Use **less** for a lower degree when comparing two things.

> This car is **less expensive than** that one.

Use **the least** for the lowest degree among three or more.

> This is **the least expensive** option.

| Degree | Example |
|---|---|
| Positive | expensive |
| Comparative | less expensive |
| Superlative | the least expensive |

---

# 21. As ... As

Use **as + adjective + as** to show equal degree.

### Structure

**as + adjective + as**

> John is **as tall as** Peter.

> This book is **as interesting as** that one.

### Negative

> John is **not as tall as** Peter.

> This car is **not as expensive as** that one.

---

# 22. Too + Adjective

**Too + adjective** means more than is desirable or acceptable.

> The water is **too hot**.

> This bag is **too heavy**.

> The question is **too difficult**.

### Structure

**too + adjective**

---

# 23. Adjective + Enough

**Adjective + enough** means sufficient to meet a requirement.

> The room is **big enough**.

> She is **old enough** to drive.

> The water is **warm enough**.

### Structure

**adjective + enough**

Not:

> ❌ enough big

Correct:

> ✅ big enough

---

# 24. Enough + Noun

When **enough** modifies a noun, it comes before the noun.

> We have **enough money**.

> There are **enough chairs**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| adjective + enough | strong enough |
| enough + noun | enough money |

---

# 25. Too + Adjective + To-Infinitive

A common structure is:

**too + adjective + to + verb**

> The box is **too heavy to carry**.

> He is **too tired to work**.

> The water is **too hot to drink**.

Meaning: the degree of the adjective prevents the action.

---

# 26. Adjective + Enough + To-Infinitive

Another common structure is:

**adjective + enough + to + verb**

> He is **strong enough to lift** it.

> She is **old enough to drive**.

> The room is **large enough to hold** 100 people.

---

# 27. Participial Adjectives: -ing and -ed

Some adjectives come from verbs.

### -ing Adjectives

Usually describe the **thing/person that causes a feeling**.

> The movie is **boring**.

> The lesson is **interesting**.

> The news is **exciting**.

### -ed Adjectives

Usually describe the **person who experiences the feeling**.

> I am **bored**.

> She is **interested**.

> They are **excited**.

---

# 28. -ing vs. -ed Adjectives

| -ing | -ed |
|---|---|
| boring | bored |
| interesting | interested |
| exciting | excited |
| confusing | confused |
| tiring | tired |
| surprising | surprised |
| frightening | frightened |
| disappointing | disappointed |

### Compare

> The movie is **boring**.

The movie causes boredom.

> I am **bored**.

I experience boredom.

### Easy Rule

**-ing = causes the feeling**

**-ed = experiences the feeling**

---

# 29. Participial Adjective Examples

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| Thing causes feeling | The book is **interesting**. |
| Person experiences feeling | I am **interested**. |
| Thing causes feeling | The game is **exciting**. |
| Person experiences feeling | The children are **excited**. |
| Thing causes feeling | The work is **tiring**. |
| Person experiences feeling | I am **tired**. |

---

# 30. Compound Adjectives

A compound adjective is made from two or more words that work together to describe a noun.

| Compound Adjective | Example |
|---|---|
| well-known | a well-known actor |
| full-time | a full-time job |
| part-time | a part-time worker |
| five-year-old | a five-year-old child |
| English-speaking | an English-speaking country |
| high-quality | high-quality products |
| user-friendly | a user-friendly website |

### Hyphens

Compound adjectives before nouns are often hyphenated.

> a **five-year-old** child

> a **high-quality** product

But:

> The child is **five years old**.

No hyphens are normally used after the noun in this structure.

---

# 31. Attributive vs. Predicative Adjectives

### Attributive

The adjective comes before the noun.

> a **beautiful** house

### Predicative

The adjective comes after a linking verb.

> The house is **beautiful**.

| Position | Example |
|---|---|
| Before noun | a beautiful house |
| After linking verb | The house is beautiful. |

# 36. Gradable Adjectives

Gradable adjectives can have different degrees.

Examples:

- hot
- cold
- big
- small
- expensive
- difficult
- interesting

They can be modified by words such as:

- very
- quite
- rather
- extremely
- fairly
- slightly
- too

### Examples

> very hot

> extremely expensive

> slightly difficult

> too small

---

# 37. Non-Gradable Adjectives

Some adjectives describe an absolute or extreme condition and are not normally used with ordinary degree words.

Examples:

- perfect
- unique
- impossible
- dead
- complete
- empty

Instead of:

> ❌ very impossible

Use:

> completely impossible

Instead of:

> ❌ very perfect

Use:

> absolutely perfect

| Adjective | Common Modifier |
|---|---|
| impossible | completely/absolutely |
| perfect | absolutely |
| empty | completely |
| dead | completely |
| unique | truly/completely |

# 39. So vs. Such

### So + Adjective

> It is **so beautiful**.

> She is **so intelligent**.

### Such + Noun Phrase

> It is **such a beautiful place**.

> She is **such an intelligent student**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| so + adjective | so beautiful |
| such + a/an + adjective + singular noun | such a beautiful place |
| such + adjective + plural noun | such beautiful places |
| such + adjective + uncountable noun | such good advice |

# 41. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| She is **happily**. | She is **happy**. | Use an adjective after "be". |
| He looks **happily**. | He looks **happy**. | "Look" is a linking verb here. |
| I am **interesting** in art. | I am **interested** in art. | The person experiences the feeling. |
| The movie is **interested**. | The movie is **interesting**. | The movie causes the feeling. |
| He is **more taller** than me. | He is **taller** than me. | Do not normally use "more" with -er. |
| She is **the most tallest**. | She is **the tallest**. | Do not use "most" with -est. |
| This is **more easier**. | This is **easier**. | Use the correct comparative form. |
| She is **beautifuller**. | She is **more beautiful**. | Use "more" with longer adjectives. |
| He is **enough strong**. | He is **strong enough**. | Enough follows the adjective. |
| It is **too much difficult**. | It is **too difficult**. | Use "too + adjective". |

## Key Rules

1. **Adjectives describe nouns or pronouns.**
   - a **beautiful** house

2. Adjectives can come **before nouns**.
   - a **large** city

3. Adjectives can come **after linking verbs**.
   - The city is **large**.

4. Adjectives normally do **not** change for plural nouns.
   - a **beautiful** flower
   - **beautiful** flowers

5. Use **-er/more** for comparatives.
   - taller
   - more expensive

6. Use **-est/most** for superlatives.
   - tallest
   - most expensive

7. Use **as + adjective + as** for equal comparison.
   - as good as

8. **-ing** adjectives usually describe what causes a feeling.
   - The movie is **boring**.

9. **-ed** adjectives usually describe the person experiencing a feeling.
   - I am **bored**.

10. With multiple adjectives, a common order is:
   - **Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Noun**

11. **Enough** comes after an adjective.
   - strong **enough**

12. **Too** comes before an adjective.
   - **too** difficult
