# Clauses in English

## 1. What Is a Clause?

A **clause** is a group of words that contains a **subject** and a **verb**.

### Examples

> **She works hard.**

> **because she works hard**

> **when I arrived**

A clause can be:

1. **Independent clause**
2. **Dependent clause**

---

# 2. Summary of Clauses

| Type of Clause | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent clause** | Expresses a complete idea | She is happy. |
| **Dependent clause** | Cannot usually stand alone | because she is happy |
| **Noun clause** | Functions like a noun | I know **what he wants**. |
| **Adjective clause** | Describes a noun/pronoun | The man **who lives next door** is a doctor. |
| **Adverb clause** | Modifies a verb, adjective, or sentence | I stayed home **because it was raining**. |
| **Relative clause** | Gives information about a noun | The book **that I bought** is interesting. |
| **Conditional clause** | Expresses a condition | **If you study**, you will pass. |

---

# 3. Independent Clauses

An **independent clause** contains a subject and a verb and expresses a **complete idea**.

It can stand alone as a sentence.

### Examples

> **She works every day.**

> **I like coffee.**

> **They went home.**

| Subject | Verb | Complete Idea |
|---|---|---|
| She | works | Yes |
| I | like | Yes |
| They | went | Yes |

### Example

> **I was tired**, but I continued working.

**I was tired** is an independent clause.

---

# 4. Dependent Clauses

A **dependent clause** contains a subject and a verb, but it **does not normally express a complete idea by itself**.

It often begins with a subordinating conjunction or relative word.

### Examples

> **because I was tired**

> **when she arrived**

> **if you study hard**

> **although it was raining**

| Dependent Clause | Why It Is Dependent |
|---|---|
| **because I was tired** | Begins with because |
| **when she arrived** | Begins with when |
| **if you study** | Begins with if |
| **although it was raining** | Begins with although |

### Example

❌ **Because I was tired.**

This is incomplete.

✅ **I went to bed because I was tired.**

---

# 5. Independent vs. Dependent Clauses

| Feature | Independent Clause | Dependent Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Has a subject | Yes | Yes |
| Has a verb | Yes | Yes |
| Complete idea | Yes | Usually no |
| Can stand alone | Yes | No |
| Example | **I went home.** | **because I was tired** |

---

# 6. Noun Clauses

A **noun clause** is a dependent clause that functions like a **noun**.

It can be a:

- Subject
- Object
- Complement

Common words introducing noun clauses include:

> what, who, whom, whose, which, where, when, why, how, that, whether, if

### Examples

> I know **what she wants**.

> **What he said** was true.

> I don't know **where he lives**.

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject | **What she said** was interesting. |
| Object | I know **what she said**. |
| Complement | The problem is **that we are late**. |

---

# 7. Noun Clauses as Subjects

A noun clause can be the **subject** of a sentence.

### Examples

> **What he said** was true.

> **Where she lives** is unknown.

> **Why he left** is a mystery.

| Noun Clause | Main Verb |
|---|---|
| **What he said** | was |
| **Where she lives** | is |
| **Why he left** | is |

---

# 8. Noun Clauses as Objects

A noun clause can be the **object of a verb**.

### Examples

> I know **what he wants**.

> She explained **why she was late**.

> We don't know **where they went**.

| Main Verb | Noun Clause |
|---|---|
| know | **what he wants** |
| explained | **why she was late** |
| don't know | **where they went** |

---

# 9. Adjective Clauses

An **adjective clause** describes or gives more information about a **noun or pronoun**.

It is also commonly called a **relative clause**.

Common relative words include:

> who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when

### Examples

> The man **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> I bought a book **that is very interesting**.

> This is the house **where I grew up**.

| Noun | Adjective Clause | Information |
|---|---|---|
| the man | **who lives next door** | Describes the man |
| a book | **that is very interesting** | Describes the book |
| the house | **where I grew up** | Gives information about the house |

---

# 10. Relative Pronouns

| Relative Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | People | The woman **who called me** is my teacher. |
| **whom** | People as objects | The man **whom I met** was friendly. |
| **whose** | Possession | The student **whose phone rang** apologized. |
| **which** | Things/animals | The car **which I bought** is new. |
| **that** | People or things | The book **that I bought** is useful. |
| **where** | Places | This is the house **where I grew up**. |
| **when** | Times | I remember the day **when we met**. |

---

# 11. Defining Relative Clauses

A **defining relative clause** gives essential information about a noun.

Without the clause, the meaning may be unclear.

### Examples

> The man **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> The book **that you gave me** is excellent.

> The students **who studied** passed the exam.

| Sentence | Defining Information |
|---|---|
| The man **who lives next door** is a doctor. | Which man? |
| The book **that you gave me** is excellent. | Which book? |
| The students **who studied** passed. | Which students? |

**No commas** are normally used around defining relative clauses.

---

# 12. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

A **non-defining relative clause** gives extra information that is not essential to identify the noun.

It is normally separated by **commas**.

### Examples

> My brother, **who lives in London**, is a doctor.

> Paris, **which is the capital of France**, is beautiful.

> Mr. Smith, **who teaches English**, is very kind.

| Main Noun | Extra Information |
|---|---|
| My brother | **who lives in London** |
| Paris | **which is the capital of France** |
| Mr. Smith | **who teaches English** |

---

# 13. Adverb Clauses

An **adverb clause** modifies a verb, adjective, or whole sentence.

It usually tells us:

- When
- Where
- Why
- How
- Under what condition
- Despite what contrast
- For what purpose

### Examples

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

> Call me **when you arrive**.

> **If you study**, you will pass.

| Function | Common Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, while, before, after, until | Call me **when you arrive**. |
| **Reason** | because, since, as | I left **because I was tired**. |
| **Condition** | if, unless | **If you study**, you will pass. |
| **Contrast** | although, though, even though | **Although it was raining**, we went out. |
| **Purpose** | so that, in order that | I studied **so that I could pass**. |
| **Result** | so...that, such...that | It was **so cold that** we stayed home. |
| **Place** | where, wherever | Sit **wherever you like**. |
| **Comparison** | than, as...as | She is taller **than I am**. |

---

# 14. Clauses of Time

Time clauses tell us **when something happens**.

Common conjunctions:

> when, while, before, after, until, since, as soon as

### Examples

> Call me **when you arrive**.

> I listened to music **while I was studying**.

> Wash your hands **before you eat**.

> We went home **after the movie ended**.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **when** | Call me **when you arrive**. |
| **while** | She cooked **while I cleaned**. |
| **before** | Wash your hands **before you eat**. |
| **after** | We left **after dinner ended**. |
| **until** | Wait here **until I return**. |
| **as soon as** | Call me **as soon as you arrive**. |

---

# 15. Clauses of Reason

Reason clauses explain **why something happens**.

Common conjunctions:

> because, since, as

### Examples

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

> **Since it was raining**, we stayed inside.

> **As I was tired**, I went to bed.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **because** | I left **because I was tired**. |
| **since** | **Since it was late**, we went home. |
| **as** | **As she was sick**, she stayed home. |

---

# 16. Clauses of Contrast

Contrast clauses show that two ideas are different or unexpected.

Common conjunctions:

> although, though, even though, whereas, while

### Examples

> **Although it was raining**, we went outside.

> **Even though he was tired**, he continued working.

> John likes coffee, **whereas Mary prefers tea**.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **although** | **Although it was difficult**, I tried. |
| **though** | **Though he was tired**, he worked. |
| **even though** | **Even though it rained**, we played. |
| **whereas** | Tom likes tea, **whereas Jane likes coffee**. |

---

# 17. Conditional Clauses

A **conditional clause** expresses a **condition**.

Common words:

> if, unless, provided that, as long as

### Examples

> **If you study**, you will pass.

> **Unless you hurry**, you will be late.

> You can come **as long as you finish your work**.

| Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **if** | A condition | **If it rains**, we will stay home. |
| **unless** | If not | **Unless you hurry**, you'll be late. |
| **provided that** | Only if | You can go **provided that you finish**. |
| **as long as** | On the condition that | You can stay **as long as you are quiet**. |

---

# 18. Types of Conditional Clauses

| Conditional Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Zero conditional** | If + present, present | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| **First conditional** | If + present, will + verb | If it rains, we will stay home. |
| **Second conditional** | If + past, would + verb | If I had money, I would travel. |
| **Third conditional** | If + past perfect, would have + past participle | If I had studied, I would have passed. |

---

# 19. Main Clause + Dependent Clause

A sentence can combine an independent clause with a dependent clause.

### Pattern 1

> **Independent clause + dependent clause**

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

### Pattern 2

> **Dependent clause + independent clause**

> **Because it was raining**, I stayed home.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Main + dependent | I stayed home **because it was raining**. |
| Dependent + main | **Because it was raining**, I stayed home. |

When a dependent clause comes first, a comma is commonly used before the main clause.

---

# 20. Compound Sentences

A **compound sentence** contains **two or more independent clauses** joined by a coordinating conjunction.

### Examples

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

> She studied hard, **and** she passed the exam.

> It was raining, **so** we stayed home.

| Clause 1 | Conjunction | Clause 2 |
|---|---|---|
| I was tired | **but** | I continued working. |
| She studied hard | **and** | she passed the exam. |
| It was raining | **so** | we stayed home. |

---

# 21. Complex Sentences

A **complex sentence** contains:

- One independent clause
- At least one dependent clause

### Examples

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

> **Although he was tired**, he continued working.

> I will call you **when I arrive**.

| Independent Clause | Dependent Clause |
|---|---|
| I stayed home | because it was raining |
| he continued working | Although he was tired |
| I will call you | when I arrive |

---

# 22. Compound-Complex Sentences

A **compound-complex sentence** contains:

- At least two independent clauses
- At least one dependent clause

### Example

> **Although I was tired**, I finished my work, **and** I went to bed.

| Clause | Type |
|---|---|
| Although I was tired | Dependent |
| I finished my work | Independent |
| I went to bed | Independent |

---

# 23. Clauses vs. Phrases

A **clause** normally contains a **subject and a verb**.

A **phrase** does not normally contain a complete subject-verb combination.

| Type | Example | Subject + Verb? |
|---|---|---|
| Phrase | **in the morning** | No |
| Phrase | **to study English** | No complete subject + verb |
| Clause | **when I arrived** | Yes |
| Clause | **because she was tired** | Yes |
| Independent clause | **She went home.** | Yes |

### Compare

> **After lunch** → Phrase

> **After I ate lunch** → Clause

> **Because of the rain** → Phrase

> **Because it was raining** → Clause

---

# 24. Clauses in One Sentence

Consider this sentence:

> **Although it was raining, the students who studied hard passed the exam because they wanted to succeed.**

| Clause | Type | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Although it was raining** | Adverb clause | Shows contrast |
| **the students ... passed the exam** | Independent clause | Main idea |
| **who studied hard** | Adjective/relative clause | Describes students |
| **because they wanted to succeed** | Adverb clause | Shows reason |

---

# 25. Quick Summary

| Clause Type | Main Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent clause** | Complete idea | She is happy. |
| **Dependent clause** | Incomplete idea | because she is happy |
| **Noun clause** | Acts like a noun | I know **what he wants**. |
| **Adjective clause** | Describes a noun | The man **who called me** is here. |
| **Adverb clause** | Modifies a sentence/verb | I left **because I was tired**. |
| **Relative clause** | Gives information about a noun | The book **that I bought** is good. |
| **Conditional clause** | Shows a condition | **If you study**, you will pass. |
| **Time clause** | Shows time | Call me **when you arrive**. |
| **Reason clause** | Shows reason | I stayed home **because I was sick**. |
| **Contrast clause** | Shows contrast | **Although it rained**, we went out. |

---

# 26. Easy Way to Remember

| Clause | Think of It As |
|---|---|
| **Independent clause** | A complete sentence |
| **Dependent clause** | An incomplete idea |
| **Noun clause** | Acts like a noun |
| **Adjective clause** | Describes a noun |
| **Adverb clause** | Gives information such as time, reason, or condition |
| **Relative clause** | Describes or identifies a noun |
| **Conditional clause** | Shows a condition |

## Key Point

A **clause** contains a **subject and a verb**.

Remember:

> **Independent clause** → complete idea  
> **Dependent clause** → incomplete idea  
> **Noun clause** → acts like a noun  
> **Adjective clause** → describes a noun  
> **Adverb clause** → gives information about time, reason, condition, contrast, etc.

### The three main sentence patterns

> **Compound:** Independent + Independent  
> *I was tired, but I continued working.*

> **Complex:** Independent + Dependent  
> *I went home because I was tired.*

> **Compound-complex:** Independent + Independent + Dependent  
> *Although I was tired, I finished my work, and I went home.*
