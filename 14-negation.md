# Negation in English

## 1. What Is Negation?

**Negation** is used to say that something is **not true**, **does not happen**, or **does not exist**.

The most common negative words in English are:

- **not**
- **no**
- **never**
- **nobody**
- **nothing**
- **nowhere**
- **neither**
- **none**
- **hardly**
- **rarely**
- **seldom**

---

## 2. Summary of Negation

| Type | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **not** | Makes a verb, adjective, or sentence negative | She is **not** happy. |
| **do not / don't** | Negative with present simple verbs | I **don't** like coffee. |
| **does not / doesn't** | Negative with he/she/it in present simple | He **doesn't** work here. |
| **did not / didn't** | Negative in past simple | They **didn't** go home. |
| **am not / is not / are not** | Negative with **be** | She **isn't** ready. |
| **was not / were not** | Negative with past **be** | We **weren't** tired. |
| **have not / has not** | Negative with perfect tenses | She **hasn't** finished. |
| **will not / won't** | Negative future | I **won't** come tomorrow. |
| **cannot / can't** | Negative ability or possibility | He **can't** swim. |
| **must not / mustn't** | Prohibition | You **mustn't** smoke here. |
| **never** | Not at any time | I **never** drink alcohol. |
| **no** | Shows zero or absence | There is **no** water. |
| **nobody / no one** | Refers to zero people | **Nobody** knows the answer. |
| **nothing** | Refers to zero things | There is **nothing** here. |
| **nowhere** | Refers to no place | I have **nowhere** to go. |
| **neither** | Not one and not the other | **Neither** answer is correct. |

---

# 3. Negation with "Be"

Use **not** after forms of the verb **be**.

### Present

> I **am not** tired.

> He **is not** ready.

> They **are not** at home.

### Past

> She **was not** happy.

> We **were not** late.

| Positive | Negative | Short Form |
|---|---|---|
| I am | I am not | I'm not |
| He is | He is not | He isn't / He's not |
| They are | They are not | They aren't / They're not |
| He was | He was not | He wasn't |
| They were | They were not | They weren't |

---

# 4. Negation with the Present Simple

For most present simple verbs, use:

> **do not (don't)**

or

> **does not (doesn't)**

### Examples

> I **don't like** coffee.

> You **don't speak** French.

> She **doesn't work** here.

> He **doesn't play** tennis.

### Structure

> Subject + do/does + not + base verb

| Subject | Negative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **don't + verb** | They **don't eat** meat. |
| He / She / It | **doesn't + verb** | She **doesn't eat** meat. |

### Important

After **doesn't**, use the **base form** of the verb.

> She **doesn't like** coffee. ✅

> She **doesn't likes** coffee. ❌

---

# 5. Negation with the Past Simple

Use:

> **did not (didn't) + base verb**

### Examples

> I **didn't go** to school.

> She **didn't call** me.

> They **didn't watch** the movie.

### Structure

> Subject + did not + base verb

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| I went. | I **didn't go**. |
| She saw him. | She **didn't see** him. |
| They played. | They **didn't play**. |

### Important

After **didn't**, use the **base form**.

> He **didn't go**. ✅

> He **didn't went**. ❌

---

# 6. Negation with Present Continuous

Use:

> **am/is/are + not + verb-ing**

### Examples

> I **am not working**.

> She **isn't sleeping**.

> They **aren't studying**.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| I am working. | I **am not working**. |
| She is sleeping. | She **isn't sleeping**. |
| They are studying. | They **aren't studying**. |

---

# 7. Negation with Past Continuous

Use:

> **was/were + not + verb-ing**

### Examples

> I **wasn't sleeping**.

> He **wasn't working**.

> They **weren't watching** TV.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| He was working. | He **wasn't working**. |
| We were sleeping. | We **weren't sleeping**. |

---

# 8. Negation with Present Perfect

Use:

> **have/has + not + past participle**

### Examples

> I **haven't finished**.

> She **hasn't arrived**.

> They **haven't eaten** yet.

| Subject | Negative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **haven't + past participle** | We **haven't finished**. |
| He / She / It | **hasn't + past participle** | She **hasn't finished**. |

---

# 9. Negation with Future "Will"

Use:

> **will not (won't) + base verb**

### Examples

> I **won't go** tomorrow.

> She **won't be** late.

> They **won't come** to the party.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| I will go. | I **won't go**. |
| She will come. | She **won't come**. |
| They will help. | They **won't help**. |

---

# 10. Negation with Modal Verbs

Put **not** after the modal verb.

Common modal verbs include:

> can, could, may, might, must, should, would

### Examples

> I **can't swim**.

> You **shouldn't worry**.

> He **mustn't leave**.

> They **might not come**.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| can | cannot / can't |
| could | could not / couldn't |
| may | may not |
| might | might not |
| must | must not / mustn't |
| should | should not / shouldn't |
| would | would not / wouldn't |

---

# 11. "No" and "Not"

**No** is usually used before a noun.

**Not** is usually used with a verb, adjective, adverb, or other structure.

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| **no + noun** | There is **no food**. |
| **not + verb** | I do **not** understand. |
| **not + adjective** | She is **not happy**. |
| **not + adverb** | He did **not** speak loudly. |

### Compare

> I have **no money**.

> I **don't have any money**.

Both sentences have the same basic meaning.

---

# 12. "Never"

**Never** means **not at any time**.

### Examples

> I **never** smoke.

> She has **never** been to Japan.

> He **never** arrives late.

### Position

Usually, **never** comes:

- Before the main verb
- After the verb **be**
- After the first auxiliary verb

| Sentence | Example |
|---|---|
| Before main verb | I **never eat** breakfast. |
| After be | She is **never** late. |
| After auxiliary | I have **never seen** it. |

---

# 13. Negative Pronouns

Some words are already negative.

| Negative Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Nobody** | No person | **Nobody** called me. |
| **No one** | No person | **No one** knows. |
| **Nothing** | No thing | **Nothing** happened. |
| **Nowhere** | No place | He has **nowhere** to go. |
| **Neither** | Not one or the other | **Neither** answer is correct. |
| **None** | Not any | **None** of them came. |

### Important

Avoid using two negative words together in standard English.

> Nobody **didn't** come. ❌

> Nobody **came**. ✅

---

# 14. Negative Questions

Negative questions often use:

> **Isn't / Aren't / Don't / Doesn't / Didn't / Haven't / Won't**, etc.

### Examples

> **Isn't** she your sister?

> **Don't** you like coffee?

> **Didn't** he call you?

> **Haven't** they arrived?

> **Won't** you come with us?

Negative questions can express **surprise, expectation, or confirmation**.

---

# 15. Double Negatives

A **double negative** uses two negative forms in one clause.

In standard English, this is usually incorrect when the two negatives are intended to make one negative meaning.

### Incorrect in standard English

> I **don't know nothing**.

### Correct

> I **don't know anything**.

or

> I **know nothing**.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| I don't know nothing. | I don't know **anything**. |
| She doesn't have no money. | She doesn't have **any money**. |
| We didn't see nobody. | We didn't see **anybody**. |

---

# 16. Negative Words with "Any"

After a negative verb, we commonly use:

> **any, anybody, anyone, anything, anywhere**

### Examples

> I don't have **any money**.

> She doesn't know **anybody** here.

> We didn't see **anything**.

> He doesn't go **anywhere**.

| Positive / Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|
| some | **any** |
| someone / somebody | **anyone / anybody** |
| something | **anything** |
| somewhere | **anywhere** |

---

# 17. Negation with "Have"

The negative form depends on how **have** is being used.

### Possession

> I **don't have** a car.

> She **doesn't have** a brother.

### Present Perfect

> I **haven't finished**.

> He **hasn't arrived**.

| Use | Negative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Possession | don't/doesn't have | I **don't have** a car. |
| Present perfect | haven't/hasn't + past participle | She **hasn't finished**. |

---

# 18. Negation with "Used to"

The negative form is:

> **didn't use to + base verb**

### Example

> I **didn't use to** like coffee.

> She **didn't use to** live here.

**Important:**

> I didn't **use** to... ✅

> I didn't **used** to... ❌

# 20. Negation in Different Tenses

| Tense | Negative Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | do/does + not + verb | She **doesn't work** here. |
| **Past Simple** | did + not + verb | She **didn't work** yesterday. |
| **Present Continuous** | am/is/are + not + V-ing | She **isn't working** now. |
| **Past Continuous** | was/were + not + V-ing | She **wasn't working** then. |
| **Present Perfect** | have/has + not + past participle | She **hasn't worked** today. |
| **Past Perfect** | had + not + past participle | She **hadn't worked** before. |
| **Future Simple** | will + not + verb | She **won't work** tomorrow. |
| **Modal** | modal + not + verb | She **can't work** today. |

# 22. Negation in One Sentence

Consider this sentence:

> **Tom doesn't usually eat breakfast because he isn't hungry in the morning.**

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **doesn't** | Makes the main verb negative |
| **usually** | Shows frequency |
| **eat** | Base form after doesn't |
| **because** | Gives a reason |
| **isn't** | Negative form of "be" |
| **hungry** | Adjective |
| **in the morning** | Shows time |

# 24. Easy Way to Remember

| Positive Idea | Negative Form |
|---|---|
| Be | **be + not** |
| Present simple | **do/does + not + verb** |
| Past simple | **did + not + verb** |
| Continuous | **be + not + V-ing** |
| Perfect | **have/has/had + not + past participle** |
| Future | **will + not + verb** |
| Modal | **modal + not + verb** |
| Any person | **nobody / no one** |
| Any thing | **nothing** |
| Any place | **nowhere** |
| Any time | **never** |

## Key Point

To make a sentence negative, English usually uses **not** with an auxiliary or modal verb.

> She **is** happy. → She **isn't** happy.

> She **works** here. → She **doesn't work** here.

> She **worked** yesterday. → She **didn't work** yesterday.

> She **will come**. → She **won't come**.

Remember:

> **Be + not**  
> **Do/does/did + not + base verb**  
> **Auxiliary/modal + not + main verb**
