# Subject–Verb Agreement Summary

## 1. What Is Subject–Verb Agreement?

Subject–verb agreement means that the **verb must agree with the subject in number and person**.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular subject | Singular verb | She **works** here. |
| Plural subject | Plural verb | They **work** here. |
| I | Special form | I **am** happy. |
| You | Plural-form verb | You **are** happy. |

---

## 2. Basic Rule

| Subject | Present Simple Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | V1 | I **work**. |
| You | V1 | You **work**. |
| We | V1 | We **work**. |
| They | V1 | They **work**. |
| He | V-s/-es | He **works**. |
| She | V-s/-es | She **works**. |
| It | V-s/-es | It **works**. |

### Main Rule

**Singular subject → singular verb**

> The boy **plays** football.

**Plural subject → plural verb**

> The boys **play** football.

---

## 3. Be Verb Agreement

| Subject | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | was |
| You | are | were |
| He | is | was |
| She | is | was |
| It | is | was |
| We | are | were |
| They | are | were |

### Examples

| Subject | Correct Example |
|---|---|
| I | I **am** tired. |
| He | He **is** tired. |
| They | They **are** tired. |
| She | She **was** happy. |
| We | We **were** happy. |

---

## 4. Have and Has

| Subject | Correct Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | have | I **have** a car. |
| You | have | You **have** a car. |
| We | have | We **have** a car. |
| They | have | They **have** a car. |
| He | has | He **has** a car. |
| She | has | She **has** a car. |
| It | has | It **has** four legs. |

### Rule

**He / She / It → has**

**I / You / We / They → have**

---

## 5. Do and Does

| Subject | Correct Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | do | I **do** my homework. |
| You | do | You **do** your homework. |
| We | do | We **do** our homework. |
| They | do | They **do** their homework. |
| He | does | He **does** his homework. |
| She | does | She **does** her homework. |
| It | does | It **does** the job. |

### Question Examples

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| Does she **work** here? | Does she **work** here? |
| Does she **works** here? | ❌ |
| Do they **works** here? | ❌ |
| Do they **work** here? | ✓ |

After **do/does**, use the **base verb (V1)**.

---

## 6. Singular Nouns

A singular noun normally takes a singular verb.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The boy | plays | The boy **plays** football. |
| The girl | studies | The girl **studies** English. |
| My brother | works | My brother **works** here. |
| The dog | runs | The dog **runs** fast. |
| The car | needs | The car **needs** repair. |

---

## 7. Plural Nouns

Plural nouns normally take a plural verb.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The boys | play | The boys **play** football. |
| The girls | study | The girls **study** English. |
| My brothers | work | My brothers **work** here. |
| The dogs | run | The dogs **run** fast. |
| The cars | need | The cars **need** repair. |

---

## 8. Two Subjects Joined by "And"

Two subjects joined by **and** normally take a plural verb.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tom and John | are | Tom and John **are** friends. |
| Mary and Anna | work | Mary and Anna **work** together. |
| The teacher and student | are | The teacher and student **are** talking. |
| My brother and sister | live | My brother and sister **live** here. |

### Rule

**A + and + B → plural verb**

> Tom and Jack **are** students.

---

## 9. Subjects Joined by "Or" or "Nor"

When subjects are joined by **or/nor**, the verb usually agrees with the subject closest to it.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Singular + or + singular | Either John or Mary **is** coming. |
| Plural + or + plural | Either the boys or the girls **are** coming. |
| Singular + or + plural | Either John or the boys **are** coming. |
| Plural + or + singular | Either the boys or John **is** coming. |

### Rule

**Verb agrees with the nearest subject.**

> Either the teacher or the students **are** responsible.

> Either the students or the teacher **is** responsible.

---

## 10. Either...or / Neither...nor

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Either A or B | Either Tom or John **is** coming. |
| Neither A nor B | Neither Tom nor John **is** coming. |
| Either plural or plural | Either the boys or the girls **are** ready. |
| Neither plural nor plural | Neither the boys nor the girls **are** ready. |

---

## 11. Collective Nouns

Collective nouns refer to a group.

| Collective Noun | Example |
|---|---|
| team | The team **is** ready. |
| family | My family **is** large. |
| class | The class **is** quiet. |
| committee | The committee **has** decided. |
| company | The company **is** growing. |
| government | The government **has** announced a plan. |

In standard American English, collective nouns are commonly treated as singular when the group acts as one unit.

---

## 12. Words Between Subject and Verb

Do not let words between the subject and verb confuse you.

| Subject | Extra Words | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| The box | of books | is |
| The students | in the classroom | are |
| The teacher | with her students | is |
| The list | of names | is |

### Examples

> The box of books **is** heavy.

> The students in the classroom **are** studying.

The verb agrees with the **main subject**, not the noun in the prepositional phrase.

---

## 13. "Of" Phrases

The noun before **of** usually determines the verb.

| Subject | Correct Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The box of books | is | The box of books **is** heavy. |
| The boxes of books | are | The boxes of books **are** heavy. |
| A group of students | is | A group of students **is** waiting. |
| Groups of students | are | Groups of students **are** waiting. |
| The list of names | is | The list of names **is** long. |

---

## 14. Indefinite Pronouns That Are Singular

These words normally take singular verbs.

| Singular Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|
| everyone | Everyone **is** ready. |
| everybody | Everybody **knows** him. |
| someone | Someone **is** calling. |
| somebody | Somebody **has** taken my pen. |
| anyone | Anyone **can** join. |
| anybody | Anybody **is** welcome. |
| no one | No one **knows** the answer. |
| nobody | Nobody **was** there. |
| each | Each **has** a book. |
| either | Either **is** acceptable. |
| neither | Neither **is** correct. |

### Important

Although these words refer to multiple people, they are grammatically treated as **singular**.

> Everyone **is** happy.

Not:

> Everyone **are** happy. ❌

---

## 15. Each and Every

**Each** and **every** normally take a singular noun and singular verb.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Each + singular noun | Each student **has** a book. |
| Every + singular noun | Every student **has** a book. |
| Each of + plural noun | Each of the students **has** a book. |
| Every one of + plural noun | Every one of the students **has** a book. |

### Rule

**Each/Every → singular verb**

> Each student **is** ready.

> Every student **is** ready.

---

## 16. "One of"

The structure **one of + plural noun** takes a singular verb.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| One of + plural noun | One of the students **is** absent. |
| One of + plural noun | One of my friends **lives** in Hanoi. |
| One of + plural noun | One of these books **belongs** to me. |

The subject is **one**, not the plural noun after **of**.

---

## 17. "A Number of" vs. "The Number of"

These two expressions have different meanings and verb agreement.

| Expression | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **A number of + plural noun** | Plural | A number of students **are** absent. |
| **The number of + plural noun** | Singular | The number of students **is** increasing. |

### Easy Rule

**A number of → many → plural verb**

**The number of → the total → singular verb**

---

## 18. There Is / There Are

The verb agrees with the noun that follows it.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| There is + singular noun | There **is** a book on the table. |
| There are + plural noun | There **are** three books on the table. |
| There is + uncountable noun | There **is** some water in the bottle. |

### Examples

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| There **are** a book. | There **is** a book. |
| There **is** three books. | There **are** three books. |

---

## 19. Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns normally take singular verbs.

| Uncountable Noun | Example |
|---|---|
| information | The information **is** useful. |
| advice | The advice **is** helpful. |
| furniture | The furniture **is** expensive. |
| equipment | The equipment **is** new. |
| news | The news **is** surprising. |
| money | The money **is** mine. |
| water | The water **is** cold. |

### Important

Do not normally use a plural verb with these nouns.

> The information **is** useful.

Not:

> The information **are** useful. ❌

---

## 20. Words Ending in "-s" That Are Singular

Some nouns look plural because they end in **-s**, but they take singular verbs.

| Noun | Example |
|---|---|
| news | The news **is** good. |
| mathematics | Mathematics **is** difficult. |
| physics | Physics **is** interesting. |
| economics | Economics **is** important. |
| politics | Politics **is** complicated. |

---

## 21. Plural-Only Nouns

Some nouns normally take plural verbs.

| Noun | Example |
|---|---|
| scissors | The scissors **are** on the table. |
| trousers | My trousers **are** dirty. |
| jeans | These jeans **are** expensive. |
| clothes | My clothes **are** wet. |
| glasses | His glasses **are** broken. |
| police | The police **are** investigating. |

---

## 22. Nouns That Can Change Meaning

Some nouns can take singular or plural verbs depending on the meaning.

| Noun | Singular Meaning | Plural Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| family | The family **is** large. | The family **are** arguing. |
| team | The team **is** winning. | The team **are** wearing different shirts. |
| staff | The staff **is** large. | The staff **are** discussing the issue. |

In American English, collective nouns are often treated as singular. British English may use plural agreement when focusing on individual members.

---

## 23. Amounts of Time, Money, Distance, and Weight

When an expression represents **one total amount**, it usually takes a singular verb.

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| Ten dollars | Ten dollars **is** enough. |
| Five years | Five years **is** a long time. |
| Ten kilometers | Ten kilometers **is** too far to walk. |
| Two hours | Two hours **is** enough. |
| One hundred dollars | One hundred dollars **is** expensive for this item. |

---

## 24. Fractions and Percentages

The verb depends on the noun after **of**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Percentage + of + singular noun | 50% of the water **is** clean. |
| Percentage + of + plural noun | 50% of the students **are** absent. |
| Fraction + of + singular noun | Half of the cake **is** gone. |
| Fraction + of + plural noun | Half of the students **are** absent. |
| Some of + singular noun | Some of the money **is** missing. |
| Some of + plural noun | Some of the students **are** absent. |

---

## 25. Gerunds as Subjects

A gerund phrase used as the subject normally takes a singular verb.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Swimming | is | Swimming **is** good exercise. |
| Reading books | is | Reading books **is** useful. |
| Learning English | takes | Learning English **takes** time. |
| Exercising regularly | helps | Exercising regularly **helps** your health. |

---

## 26. Infinitives as Subjects

An infinitive phrase used as the subject normally takes a singular verb.

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| To exercise | To exercise regularly **is** important. |
| To learn English | To learn English **takes** time. |
| To travel | To travel alone **is** exciting. |

---

## 27. Titles and Names

Titles of books, movies, songs, newspapers, and other works are usually treated as singular.

| Title | Example |
|---|---|
| *The Lord of the Rings* | *The Lord of the Rings* **is** a famous book. |
| *Friends* | *Friends* **is** a popular TV series. |
| *The United States* | The United States **is** a country. |

---

## 28. Subject–Verb Agreement in Questions

The subject still controls the verb in questions.

| Question | Subject | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Does she work here? | she | **Does** |
| Do they work here? | they | **Do** |
| Is he ready? | he | **Is** |
| Are they ready? | they | **Are** |
| Has she finished? | she | **Has** |
| Have they finished? | they | **Have** |

---

## 29. Subject–Verb Agreement in Negative Sentences

| Subject | Correct Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| He | doesn't + V1 | He **doesn't work** here. |
| They | don't + V1 | They **don't work** here. |
| She | isn't | She **isn't** ready. |
| They | aren't | They **aren't** ready. |
| He | hasn't | He **hasn't** arrived. |
| They | haven't | They **haven't** arrived. |

---

## 30. Relative Clauses

The verb inside a relative clause agrees with the noun it refers to.

| Example | Explanation |
|---|---|
| The student who **works** here is my friend. | who = student, singular |
| The students who **work** here are my friends. | who = students, plural |
| The book that **is** on the table is mine. | that = book, singular |
| The books that **are** on the table are mine. | that = books, plural |

---

## 31. Subject–Verb Agreement with "Who"

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| Singular person | She is the woman who **works** here. |
| Plural people | They are the people who **work** here. |

The verb agrees with the noun that **who** refers to.

---

## 32. Compound Subjects

| Structure | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A and B | Plural | Tom and Mary **are** friends. |
| A or B | Depends on nearest subject | Tom or his friends **are** coming. |
| Neither A nor B | Depends on nearest subject | Neither the students nor the teacher **is** ready. |
| Either A or B | Depends on nearest subject | Either the teacher or the students **are** responsible. |

---

## 33. Common Agreement Rules

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Singular subject → singular verb | The boy **runs**. |
| Plural subject → plural verb | The boys **run**. |
| He/She/It → V-s in Present Simple | She **works**. |
| I/You/We/They → V1 | They **work**. |
| A + and + B → plural verb | Tom and John **are** here. |
| Each → singular verb | Each student **has** a book. |
| Every → singular verb | Every student **has** a book. |
| One of + plural noun → singular verb | One of the boys **is** absent. |
| A number of + plural noun → plural verb | A number of students **are** absent. |
| The number of + plural noun → singular verb | The number of students **is** increasing. |
| There is + singular noun | There **is** a problem. |
| There are + plural noun | There **are** problems. |
| Uncountable noun → singular verb | The information **is** useful. |
| Gerund subject → singular verb | Swimming **is** fun. |
| Or/Nor → usually nearest subject | Either John or the students **are** coming. |

---

## 34. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| He **work** every day. | He **works** every day. |
| They **works** every day. | They **work** every day. |
| She **don't** like coffee. | She **doesn't** like coffee. |
| They **doesn't** like coffee. | They **don't** like coffee. |
| Everyone **are** ready. | Everyone **is** ready. |
| Each student **have** a book. | Each student **has** a book. |
| The students **has** books. | The students **have** books. |
| Tom and John **is** here. | Tom and John **are** here. |
| The box of books **are** heavy. | The box of books **is** heavy. |
| A number of students **is** absent. | A number of students **are** absent. |
| The number of students **are** increasing. | The number of students **is** increasing. |
| There **is** three problems. | There **are** three problems. |
| The information **are** useful. | The information **is** useful. |
| One of the students **are** absent. | One of the students **is** absent. |

---

## 35. Quick Decision Guide

| Step | Question | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the main subject? | Find the real subject. |
| 2 | Is it singular or plural? | Choose the matching verb. |
| 3 | Is there an "of" phrase? | Ignore the noun after "of" when finding the main subject. |
| 4 | Is there "and"? | Usually use a plural verb. |
| 5 | Is there "or/nor"? | Usually match the nearest subject. |
| 6 | Is the subject each/every/everyone? | Use a singular verb. |
| 7 | Is it uncountable? | Usually use a singular verb. |
| 8 | Is it a question with do/does/did? | Use V1 after the auxiliary. |
