# Relative Clauses in English

## 1. What Is a Relative Clause?

A **relative clause** is a type of dependent clause that gives more information about a **noun or pronoun**.

It usually begins with a **relative pronoun** or **relative adverb**.

### Common Relative Words

> who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when

### Examples

> The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> I bought the book **that you recommended**.

> This is the house **where I grew up**.

---

# 2. Summary of Relative Clauses

| Relative Word | Refers To | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **who** | People | Subject | The man **who called me** is my uncle. |
| **whom** | People | Object | The woman **whom I met** was friendly. |
| **whose** | People/things | Possession | The student **whose phone rang** apologized. |
| **which** | Things/animals | Subject/object | The car **which I bought** is new. |
| **that** | People/things | Subject/object | The book **that I bought** is interesting. |
| **where** | Places | Place | This is the school **where I studied**. |
| **when** | Time | Time | I remember the day **when we met**. |

---

# 3. Defining Relative Clauses

A **defining relative clause** gives **essential information** about a noun.

Without the clause, we may not know exactly which person or thing is being discussed.

### Examples

> The man **who lives next door** is a doctor.

> The book **that you gave me** is excellent.

> The students **who studied hard** passed the exam.

| Sentence | Relative Clause | Question Answered |
|---|---|---|
| The man **who lives next door** is a doctor. | who lives next door | Which man? |
| The book **that you gave me** is excellent. | that you gave me | Which book? |
| The students **who studied hard** passed. | who studied hard | Which students? |

### Important

Defining relative clauses **do not normally use commas**.

> The woman **who teaches English** is my friend.

---

# 4. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

A **non-defining relative clause** gives **extra information** about a noun.

The noun is already clearly identified without the clause.

### Examples

> My brother, **who lives in London**, is a doctor.

> Paris, **which is the capital of France**, is beautiful.

> Mr. Brown, **who teaches English**, is very kind.

| Main Noun | Extra Information |
|---|---|
| My brother | who lives in London |
| Paris | which is the capital of France |
| Mr. Brown | who teaches English |

### Important

Non-defining relative clauses are separated by **commas**.

> My brother, **who lives in London**, is a doctor.

---

# 5. Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

| Feature | Defining | Non-Defining |
|---|---|---|
| Information | Essential | Extra |
| Commas | No | Yes |
| Can use **that**? | Yes | No |
| Example | The man **who lives here** is a doctor. | John, **who lives here**, is a doctor. |
| Meaning | Identifies which man | Gives extra information about John |

### Compare

> The students **who studied** passed the exam.

This means **only the students who studied** passed.

> The students, **who studied**, passed the exam.

This suggests **all the students studied** and passed.

---

# 6. Relative Pronoun: WHO

**Who** is used for **people**.

It can be the subject of the relative clause.

### Examples

> The woman **who called me** is my teacher.

> The man **who lives here** is a doctor.

> I know the person **who helped you**.

| Example | Who Refers To |
|---|---|
| The woman **who called me** | woman |
| The man **who lives here** | man |
| The person **who helped you** | person |

---

# 7. Relative Pronoun: WHOM

**Whom** is used for **people as the object** of a verb or preposition.

It is more formal than **who**.

### Examples

> The man **whom I met** was friendly.

> The woman **whom you called** is my teacher.

> The person **to whom I spoke** was helpful.

In everyday English, **who** is often used instead of **whom**:

> The man **who I met** was friendly.

---

# 8. Relative Pronoun: WHOSE

**Whose** shows **possession**.

It can refer to people and sometimes things.

### Examples

> The student **whose phone rang** apologized.

> I met a woman **whose son is a doctor**.

> We visited a house **whose roof was damaged**.

| Noun | Possession |
|---|---|
| student | whose phone |
| woman | whose son |
| house | whose roof |

---

# 9. Relative Pronoun: WHICH

**Which** is normally used for **things and animals**.

### Examples

> The car **which I bought** is new.

> The book **which you recommended** is excellent.

> The dog **which lives next door** is friendly.

It is also commonly used in **non-defining relative clauses**:

> My car, **which is very old**, still works well.

---

# 10. Relative Pronoun: THAT

**That** can refer to **people, animals, or things** in defining relative clauses.

### Examples

> The man **that called me** is my uncle.

> The book **that I bought** is interesting.

> The dog **that lives next door** is noisy.

### Important

**That** is normally **not used** in non-defining relative clauses.

❌ My car, **that is very old**, still works.

✅ My car, **which is very old**, still works.

---

# 11. Relative Adverb: WHERE

**Where** refers to a **place**.

### Examples

> This is the house **where I grew up**.

> That is the school **where she studied**.

> Do you remember the restaurant **where we had dinner**?

| Place | Relative Clause |
|---|---|
| house | where I grew up |
| school | where she studied |
| restaurant | where we had dinner |

---

# 12. Relative Adverb: WHEN

**When** refers to a **time**.

### Examples

> I remember the day **when we met**.

> Sunday is the day **when I relax**.

> Do you remember the year **when you started school**?

| Time Expression | Relative Clause |
|---|---|
| the day | when we met |
| Sunday | when I relax |
| the year | when you started school |

---

# 13. Relative Adverb: WHY

**Why** is commonly used after **reason**.

### Examples

> I don't know the reason **why he left**.

> Tell me the reason **why you are upset**.

> That is the reason **why I called you**.

**That** or no relative word can also be used:

> I don't know the reason **that he left**.

> I don't know the reason **he left**.

---

# 14. Subject Relative Clauses

The relative pronoun acts as the **subject** of the relative clause.

### Examples

> The man **who lives here** is my uncle.

> The book **that is on the table** is mine.

> The dog **which barked all night** belongs to my neighbor.

| Relative Word | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| who | lives | The man **who lives here** |
| that | is | The book **that is on the table** |
| which | barked | The dog **which barked all night** |

The relative pronoun **cannot normally be omitted** when it is the subject.

> The man **who lives here** is my uncle.

Not:

> ❌ The man **lives here** is my uncle.

---

# 15. Object Relative Clauses

The relative pronoun acts as the **object** of the relative clause.

### Examples

> The book **that I bought** is excellent.

> The woman **who I met** was friendly.

> The movie **which we watched** was interesting.

The relative pronoun can often be omitted when it is the object.

> The book **that I bought** is excellent.

> The book **I bought** is excellent.

Both are correct.

---

# 16. Subject vs. Object Relative Clauses

| Type | Example | Can Relative Pronoun Be Omitted? |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | The man **who lives here** is a doctor. | No |
| **Object** | The man **who I met** is a doctor. | Yes |
| **Subject** | The book **that is on the table** is mine. | No |
| **Object** | The book **that I bought** is mine. | Yes |

### Easy Test

If another noun/pronoun comes before the verb inside the relative clause, the relative pronoun is often the **object**.

> The book **that I bought**

**I** = subject  
**bought** = verb  
**that** = object

---

# 17. Prepositions in Relative Clauses

Prepositions can appear before **which** or **whom** in formal English.

### Formal

> The person **to whom I spoke** was helpful.

> The company **for which I work** is large.

### Informal

> The person **who I spoke to** was helpful.

> The company **which I work for** is large.

| Formal | Informal |
|---|---|
| to whom I spoke | who I spoke to |
| for which I work | which I work for |
| with whom she traveled | who she traveled with |

---

# 18. Relative Clauses with "Whose"

**Whose** can show possession.

### Structure

> **noun + whose + noun + verb**

### Examples

> The girl **whose father is a doctor** is my friend.

> I know a man **whose car was stolen**.

> The company **whose products are popular** is expanding.

| Main Noun | Possessed Noun |
|---|---|
| girl | whose father |
| man | whose car |
| company | whose products |

---

# 19. Reduced Relative Clauses

Sometimes a relative clause can be shortened.

### Full Relative Clause

> The man **who is standing there** is my father.

### Reduced Form

> The man **standing there** is my father.

Another example:

> The books **that were written by this author** are popular.

→ The books **written by this author** are popular.

| Full Form | Reduced Form |
|---|---|
| who is standing there | standing there |
| that was written by him | written by him |
| who is working here | working here |

---

# 20. Relative Clauses in One Sentence

Consider:

> The woman **who lives next door** bought the car **that I wanted** from the shop **where we met**.

| Relative Clause | Relative Word | Describes |
|---|---|---|
| **who lives next door** | who | woman |
| **that I wanted** | that | car |
| **where we met** | where | shop |

---

# 21. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The man **which** lives here is a doctor. | The man **who** lives here is a doctor. | Use **who** for people. |
| The book **who** I bought is good. | The book **that/which** I bought is good. | Use **that/which** for things. |
| My brother, **that** lives in London, is a doctor. | My brother, **who** lives in London, is a doctor. | Don't use **that** in non-defining clauses. |
| The man **who I met him** was friendly. | The man **who I met** was friendly. | Don't use an extra object pronoun. |
| The person **which called** me is here. | The person **who called** me is here. | Use **who** for people. |

# 23. Relative Clauses in Everyday English

| Purpose | Example |
|---|---|
| Describe a person | The woman **who works here** is friendly. |
| Describe a thing | The phone **that I bought** is expensive. |
| Show possession | The student **whose bag was lost** is upset. |
| Describe a place | This is the restaurant **where we ate**. |
| Describe a time | I remember the day **when we met**. |
| Give extra information | John, **who lives nearby**, is my friend. |

---

# 24. How to Identify a Relative Clause

Ask these questions:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| Does it contain a subject and a verb? | **who lives here** |
| Does it give information about a noun? | The man **who lives here** |
| Does it often begin with who, which, that, whose, where, or when? | The book **that I bought** |
| Can it normally stand alone? | No |
| Does it describe or identify a noun? | Yes |

### Example

> The woman **who teaches English** is my sister.

1. **The woman** → noun
2. **who teaches English** → relative clause
3. **who** → refers to the woman
4. **teaches** → verb
5. The clause describes **the woman**

## Key Point

A **relative clause** gives more information about a **noun or pronoun**.

### Main Patterns

> **Person + who + verb**  
> The man **who lives here** is a doctor.

> **Person + whom/who + subject + verb**  
> The man **whom I met** was friendly.

> **Noun + whose + noun + verb**  
> The student **whose phone rang** apologized.

> **Thing + which/that + subject + verb**  
> The book **that I bought** is interesting.

> **Place + where + subject + verb**  
> This is the house **where I grew up**.

> **Time + when + subject + verb**  
> I remember the day **when we met**.

### Remember

**Defining:** essential information → **no commas**

> The students **who studied** passed.

**Non-defining:** extra information → **commas**

> The students, **who studied**, passed.
