# Summary: Substitution in English

## 1. What Is Substitution?

**Substitution** means replacing a word, phrase, or clause with another word to **avoid repetition**.

Unlike **ellipsis**, where words are left out, **substitution uses another word in their place**.

### Example

**Without substitution:**

> I need a pen. Do you have a **pen**?

**With substitution:**

> I need a pen. Do you have **one**?

Here, **one** substitutes for **pen**.

---

## 2. Substitution vs. Ellipsis

| **Feature** | **Substitution** | **Ellipsis** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Replaces repeated information | Omits repeated information |
| Uses another word? | **Yes** | **No** |
| Example | I need a pen. I need **one**. | I need a pen, and she **does too**. |
| Common substitutes | one, ones, do, so, not | Words are simply omitted |

### ⭐ Key Difference

> **Substitution = replace it.**  
> **Ellipsis = leave it out.**

---

# 3. Main Types of Substitution

| **Type** | **What It Replaces** | **Common Words** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nominal substitution** | A noun or noun phrase | one, ones | I need a pen. Do you have **one**? |
| **Verbal substitution** | A verb or verb phrase | do, does, did | She studies more than I **do**. |
| **Clausal substitution** | An entire clause | so, not | I think **so**. |
| **Negative clausal substitution** | A negative clause | not | I hope **not**. |
| **Comparative substitution** | A repeated noun | one, ones | I prefer the red **one**. |

---

# 4. Nominal Substitution: One / Ones

**One** and **ones** can replace a previously mentioned countable noun.

### Singular: One

| **Without Substitution** | **With Substitution** |
|---|---|
| I need a pen. Do you have a **pen**? | I need a pen. Do you have **one**? |
| Which shirt do you want? I want the blue **shirt**. | Which shirt do you want? I want the blue **one**. |
| My phone is broken. I need a new **phone**. | My phone is broken. I need a new **one**. |

### Plural: Ones

| **Without Substitution** | **With Substitution** |
|---|---|
| I like these shoes, not those **shoes**. | I like these shoes, not those **ones**. |
| Which cookies do you want? The chocolate **cookies**. | Which cookies do you want? The chocolate **ones**. |

### 📌 Rule

> **one = singular countable noun**  
> **ones = plural countable noun**

---

# 5. One / Ones with Adjectives

**One/ones** can be used after an adjective to identify a particular noun.

| **Example** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| I want the **red one**. | the red item |
| She bought the **expensive one**. | the expensive item |
| I prefer the **smaller one**. | the smaller item |
| Take the **blue ones**. | the blue items |
| These shoes are better than the **black ones**. | the black shoes |

### Example

> There are two bags. I want the **black one**.

**One** replaces **bag**.

---

# 6. Verbal Substitution: Do / Does / Did

**Do, does, and did** can substitute for a previously mentioned verb or verb phrase.

| **Tense** | **Example** |
|---|---|
| Present | She works harder than I **do**. |
| Present | He likes coffee, and I **do** too. |
| Present | She speaks English better than he **does**. |
| Past | Tom finished first, and Mary **did** too. |
| Past | I studied more than he **did**. |

### Example

> Sarah speaks English better than I **do**.

**Do** substitutes for:

> speak English

---

# 7. Do / Does / Did in Comparison

Verbal substitution is especially common in comparisons.

| **Full Form** | **Substitution** |
|---|---|
| John works harder than I work. | John works harder than I **do**. |
| She runs faster than he runs. | She runs faster than he **does**. |
| They studied more than we studied. | They studied more than we **did**. |
| He sings better than I sing. | He sings better than I **do**. |

### ⭐ Pattern

> **Subject + verb + more/better/faster/etc. + than + subject + do/does/did**

Example:

> She sings better than I **do**.

---

# 8. Clausal Substitution: So

**So** can substitute for an entire positive clause.

### Structure

> **Subject + verb + so**

| **Full Response** | **With Substitution** |
|---|---|
| "Is he coming?" — I think **he is coming**. | "Is he coming?" — I think **so**. |
| "Will they win?" — I believe **they will win**. | "Will they win?" — I believe **so**. |
| "Is she ready?" — I hope **she is ready**. | "Is she ready?" — I hope **so**. |
| "Did he pass?" — I suppose **he passed**. | "Did he pass?" — I suppose **so**. |

### Example

> **A:** Do you think it will rain?  
> **B:** I think **so**.

**So** replaces:

> it will rain

---

# 9. Clausal Substitution: Not

**Not** can substitute for a negative clause.

| **Full Form** | **Substitution** |
|---|---|
| I hope that it doesn't rain. | I hope **not**. |
| I think he won't come. | I think **not**. |
| She may not pass the exam. | She may **not**. |
| Will they cancel the meeting? I hope they don't cancel it. | I hope **not**. |

### Example

> **A:** Do you think he will be late?  
> **B:** I hope **not**.

**Not** replaces:

> he will not be late

---

# 10. So / Not in Clausal Substitution

| **Positive** | **Negative** |
|---|---|
| I think **so**. | I think **not**. |
| I hope **so**. | I hope **not**. |
| I believe **so**. | I believe **not**. |
| I suppose **so**. | I suppose **not**. |

### Example

> **A:** Will she come?  
> **B:** I think **so**.

> **A:** Will she be late?  
> **B:** I hope **not**.

---

# 11. Substitution with "The Same"

**The same** can substitute for previously mentioned information.

| **Example** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| I ordered the same **as you**. | I ordered the same thing as you. |
| We had the same **as last time**. | We had the same thing as last time. |
| She chose the same **one**. | She chose the same item. |

### Example

> I ordered the chicken, and John ordered **the same**.

Meaning:

> John ordered the same **dish**.

---

# 12. Common Substitution Words

| **Substitute** | **Replaces** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **one** | Singular countable noun | I need a new phone. I'll buy **one**. |
| **ones** | Plural countable noun | I like the red shoes, not the black **ones**. |
| **do** | Present verb/verb phrase | She works harder than I **do**. |
| **does** | Present third-person verb/verb phrase | He runs faster than she **does**. |
| **did** | Past verb/verb phrase | They arrived earlier than we **did**. |
| **so** | Positive clause | I think **so**. |
| **not** | Negative clause | I hope **not**. |
| **the same** | Previously mentioned thing/action | I'll have **the same**. |

---

# 13. Substitution in Conversation

Substitution makes conversations shorter and more natural.

| **Question / Statement** | **Natural Response** |
|---|---|
| Do you want a coffee? | Yes, I'll have **one**. |
| Do you think he'll come? | I think **so**. |
| Do you think she'll be late? | I hope **not**. |
| Who runs faster, Tom or Jack? | Jack **does**. |
| Which shirt do you prefer? | I prefer the blue **one**. |
| Which shoes do you like? | I like the black **ones**. |

---

# 14. Substitution vs. Repetition

| **Repetition** | **Substitution** |
|---|---|
| I need a pen. Do you have a **pen**? | I need a pen. Do you have **one**? |
| She works harder than I **work**. | She works harder than I **do**. |
| I think he will come. | I think **so**. |
| I hope he won't be late. | I hope **not**. |
| I want the red shirt, not the blue shirt. | I want the red **one**, not the blue **one**. |

---

# 15. Quick Summary

| **Type** | **Key Idea** | **Common Substitute** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nominal substitution** | Replaces a noun | **one / ones** | I want the red **one**. |
| **Verbal substitution** | Replaces a verb/verb phrase | **do / does / did** | She works harder than I **do**. |
| **Positive clausal substitution** | Replaces a positive clause | **so** | I think **so**. |
| **Negative clausal substitution** | Replaces a negative clause | **not** | I hope **not**. |
| **Same substitution** | Refers to the same thing/action | **the same** | I'll have **the same**. |

---

## ⭐ Key Point

> **Substitution = replacing repeated words or information with a shorter substitute.**

### Easy Rule to Remember

| **What You Want to Replace** | **Use** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| A **noun** | **one / ones** | I like the blue **one**. |
| A **verb** | **do / does / did** | She sings better than I **do**. |
| A **positive clause** | **so** | I think **so**. |
| A **negative clause** | **not** | I hope **not**. |
| The **same thing/action** | **the same** | I'll have **the same**. |

### 💡 Remember

**Substitution = replace.**  
**Ellipsis = omit.**

> **Substitution:** I need a pen. Do you have **one**?  
> **Ellipsis:** I can swim, and she **can too**.
