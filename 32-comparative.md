# Comparative in English

## 1. What Is a Comparative?

A **comparative** is used to compare **two people, things, places, or ideas**.

### Basic Idea

> A is **more/less ... than** B.

### Examples

> John is **taller than** Tom.

> This book is **more interesting than** that book.

> Today is **less cold than** yesterday.

---

# 2. Main Comparative Structures

| Structure | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **adjective + -er + than** | Short adjectives | John is **taller than** Tom. |
| **more + adjective + than** | Long adjectives | This book is **more interesting than** that one. |
| **less + adjective + than** | Less of a quality | This test is **less difficult than** the last one. |
| **as + adjective + as** | Equal comparison | She is **as tall as** her sister. |
| **not as + adjective + as** | Unequal comparison | He is **not as tall as** his brother. |
| **the + comparative, the + comparative** | Shows related change | The **more** you study, the **better** you become. |

---

# 3. Comparative Adjectives with Short Words

For most **one-syllable adjectives**, add **-er**.

### Structure

> **adjective + -er + than**

| Adjective | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | John is **taller than** Tom. |
| small | smaller | My house is **smaller than** yours. |
| fast | faster | A car is **faster than** a bicycle. |
| cheap | cheaper | This phone is **cheaper than** that one. |
| old | older | My brother is **older than** me. |
| young | younger | Sarah is **younger than** Anna. |

---

# 4. Spelling Rules for -er

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Add **-er** | tall → **taller** |
| Adjective ending in **-e** → add **-r** | large → **larger** |
| Consonant + vowel + consonant → double final consonant | big → **bigger** |
| Ending in **-y** → change y to i + **-er** | happy → **happier** |

### Examples

> large → **larger**

> nice → **nicer**

> big → **bigger**

> hot → **hotter**

> happy → **happier**

> easy → **easier**

---

# 5. Comparative with Long Adjectives

For most adjectives with **two or more syllables**, use:

> **more + adjective + than**

### Examples

| Adjective | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| beautiful | more beautiful | She is **more beautiful than** her sister. |
| expensive | more expensive | This car is **more expensive than** that one. |
| interesting | more interesting | This book is **more interesting than** the other one. |
| difficult | more difficult | English is **more difficult than** I expected. |
| comfortable | more comfortable | This chair is **more comfortable than** that one. |

---

# 6. Less + Adjective

Use:

> **less + adjective + than**

It means that something has **a lower degree of a quality**.

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| This car is **less expensive than** that one. | It costs less. |
| The test is **less difficult than** the previous one. | It is easier. |
| Today is **less cold than** yesterday. | Today is warmer. |
| This room is **less comfortable than** the other one. | It is not as comfortable. |

### Compare

> This book is **more interesting than** that one.

> This book is **less interesting than** that one.

---

# 7. Equal Comparison

Use:

> **as + adjective + as**

This means two things are **equal in a particular quality**.

### Examples

> John is **as tall as** Peter.

> This car is **as expensive as** that one.

> She is **as intelligent as** her brother.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| as + adjective + as | Tom is **as tall as** John. |
| as + adjective + as | This test is **as difficult as** the last one. |
| as + adjective + as | My phone is **as good as** yours. |

---

# 8. Negative Equal Comparison

Use:

> **not as + adjective + as**

This means something has **less of a quality** than another thing.

### Examples

> John is **not as tall as** Peter.

> This car is **not as expensive as** that one.

> The movie is **not as interesting as** the book.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| She is **as tall as** me. | She is **not as tall as** me. |
| This is **as good as** that. | This is **not as good as** that. |
| He is **as fast as** Tom. | He is **not as fast as** Tom. |

---

# 9. Irregular Comparatives

Some adjectives have irregular comparative forms.

| Adjective | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| good | **better** | This book is **better than** that one. |
| bad | **worse** | Today is **worse than** yesterday. |
| far | **farther/further** | My house is **farther than** yours. |
| little | **less** | I have **less money than** you. |
| much/many | **more** | She has **more books than** me. |

### Important

Do not use:

> ❌ gooder

> ❌ badder

> ❌ more good

Use:

> ✅ better

> ✅ worse

---

# 10. Comparative with Nouns

Comparisons can also be made with nouns.

### More + Countable Noun

> **more + plural countable noun + than**

> She has **more books than** I do.

### More + Uncountable Noun

> **more + uncountable noun + than**

> I have **more money than** he does.

### Fewer + Countable Noun

> **fewer + plural countable noun + than**

> I have **fewer books than** you.

### Less + Uncountable Noun

> **less + uncountable noun + than**

> I have **less money than** you.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| more + countable noun | She has **more books than** me. |
| more + uncountable noun | He has **more money than** me. |
| fewer + countable noun | We have **fewer students than** last year. |
| less + uncountable noun | I have **less time than** you. |

---

# 11. Comparative with Adverbs

Adverbs can also have comparative forms.

| Adverb | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fast | faster | He runs **faster than** me. |
| hard | harder | She works **harder than** him. |
| early | earlier | I arrived **earlier than** John. |
| carefully | more carefully | She drives **more carefully than** me. |
| slowly | more slowly | He walks **more slowly than** his brother. |

### Examples

> She speaks **more clearly than** I do.

> He works **harder than** his colleagues.

---

# 12. More and More / -er and -er

Use a repeated comparative to show that something is **increasing or changing continuously**.

### Short Adjectives

> **comparative + and + comparative**

> The weather is getting **colder and colder**.

### Long Adjectives

> **more and more + adjective**

> English is becoming **more and more important**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| -er and -er | The days are getting **longer and longer**. |
| more and more + adjective | She is becoming **more and more confident**. |
| less and less + adjective | The situation is becoming **less and less clear**. |

---

# 13. The Comparative, the Comparative

This structure shows that **two changes are connected**.

### Structure

> **The + comparative ..., the + comparative ...**

### Examples

> The **more** you study, the **better** you become.

> The **harder** you work, the **more** you earn.

> The **earlier** you arrive, the **better**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| the + comparative, the + comparative | The **more** you practice, the **better** you become. |
| the + comparative, the + comparative | The **harder** you work, the **more** successful you become. |
| the + comparative, the + comparative | The **less** you worry, the **happier** you are. |

---

# 14. Much / Far / A Lot + Comparative

Words such as **much, far, a lot, even, slightly** can strengthen a comparative.

### Examples

> This car is **much faster than** mine.

> She is **far more experienced than** me.

> This exercise is **a lot easier than** the previous one.

> Today's weather is **slightly warmer than** yesterday's.

| Modifier | Example |
|---|---|
| much | much **better** |
| far | far **more expensive** |
| a lot | a lot **faster** |
| even | even **better** |
| slightly | slightly **cheaper** |

---

# 15. Comparative with Less / Fewer

**Less** is normally used with uncountable nouns and adjectives.

**Fewer** is normally used with plural countable nouns.

| Word | Used With | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **less** | Uncountable nouns | I have **less money than** you. |
| **fewer** | Plural countable nouns | I have **fewer books than** you. |
| **less** | Adjectives | This is **less expensive than** that. |

### Compare

> I have **less time** than you.

> I have **fewer hours** than you.

---

# 16. Comparative of Two Things

Comparatives are normally used when comparing **two** things.

### Examples

> Tom is **taller than** John.

> This phone is **cheaper than** that phone.

> My car is **more comfortable than** yours.

| Thing A | Comparison | Thing B |
|---|---|---|
| Tom | is taller than | John |
| This phone | is cheaper than | that phone |
| My car | is more comfortable than | yours |

---

# 17. Double Comparatives

Avoid using two comparative markers together.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| ❌ more taller | ✅ taller |
| ❌ more better | ✅ better |
| ❌ more easier | ✅ easier |
| ❌ more faster | ✅ faster |
| ❌ less cheaper | ✅ less expensive / cheaper |

### Example

> ❌ This car is **more cheaper** than that one.

> ✅ This car is **cheaper than** that one.

---

# 18. Comparative + Than

The word **than** is normally used to introduce the second person or thing being compared.

### Examples

> She is taller **than** me.

> This book is more interesting **than** that one.

> He works harder **than** his brother.

| Comparative | Than + Comparison |
|---|---|
| taller | taller **than John** |
| faster | faster **than me** |
| more expensive | more expensive **than that one** |
| better | better **than before** |

---

# 19. Common Comparative Patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| A + be + comparative + than + B | John is **taller than** Tom. |
| A + be + more + adjective + than + B | This is **more useful than** that. |
| A + be + less + adjective + than + B | This is **less expensive than** that. |
| A + be + as + adjective + as + B | Tom is **as tall as** John. |
| A + be + not as + adjective + as + B | Tom is **not as tall as** John. |
| A + verb + comparative adverb + than + B | She runs **faster than** me. |
| the + comparative, the + comparative | The **more** you practice, the **better** you become. |
| comparative + and + comparative | It is getting **colder and colder**. |
| more and more + adjective | She is becoming **more and more confident**. |

---

# 20. Comparative vs. Superlative

| Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Usually compares **two** things | Usually compares **three or more** |
| taller | tallest |
| more expensive | most expensive |
| better | best |
| worse | worst |
| This car is **faster than** that one. | This is the **fastest** car. |

### Examples

> John is **taller than** Tom.  
> → Two people.

> John is the **tallest** student in the class.  
> → More than two students.

---

# 21. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| She is **more taller** than me. | She is **taller than** me. | Do not use two comparative markers. |
| This is **more better**. | This is **better**. | "Better" is already comparative. |
| He is **more fast** than me. | He is **faster than** me. | Use -er with this short adjective. |
| This book is **interestinger**. | This book is **more interesting**. | Use more with this adjective. |
| She is **as taller as** me. | She is **as tall as** me. | Use the base adjective after as. |
| I have **less books**. | I have **fewer books**. | Books are countable. |
| I have **fewer money**. | I have **less money**. | Money is uncountable. |
| He is **gooder** than me. | He is **better than** me. | Good → better. |

---

# 22. How to Choose the Correct Comparative

| Adjective Type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Most short adjectives | adjective + **-er** | tall → **taller** |
| Ends in -e | adjective + **-r** | large → **larger** |
| Short CVC adjective | double consonant + **-er** | big → **bigger** |
| Ends in consonant + y | y → i + **-er** | happy → **happier** |
| Most long adjectives | **more + adjective** | beautiful → **more beautiful** |
| Negative comparison | **less + adjective** | less expensive |
| Equal comparison | **as + adjective + as** | as tall as |
| Irregular | Special form | good → better |

---

# 23. Comparative Structures in One Sentence

Consider:

> My new car is **much faster than** my old car, but it is **not as comfortable as** my old car.

| Structure | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **much + comparative** | much **faster** | Stronger comparison |
| **comparative + than** | faster **than** | Compares two cars |
| **not as + adjective + as** | not as **comfortable as** | Shows lower degree |

---

# 24. Quick Summary

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-er + than** | Compare short adjectives | Tom is **taller than** John. |
| **more + adjective + than** | Compare long adjectives | This is **more expensive than** that. |
| **less + adjective + than** | Lower degree | This is **less expensive than** that. |
| **as + adjective + as** | Equal | She is **as tall as** me. |
| **not as + adjective + as** | Not equal | She is **not as tall as** me. |
| **more + noun + than** | Greater quantity | I have **more books than** you. |
| **fewer + noun + than** | Smaller number | I have **fewer books than** you. |
| **less + noun + than** | Smaller amount | I have **less money than** you. |
| **comparative + and + comparative** | Continuous change | It is getting **colder and colder**. |
| **more and more + adjective** | Increasing degree | It is becoming **more and more difficult**. |
| **the + comparative, the + comparative** | Related change | The **more** you study, the **better** you become. |
| **much/far/a lot + comparative** | Strong comparison | This one is **much better**. |

---

# 25. Easy Way to Remember

| Comparative Form | Think of It As |
|---|---|
| **-er + than** | Short adjective comparison |
| **more + adjective + than** | Long adjective comparison |
| **less + adjective + than** | Lower degree |
| **as ... as** | Equal |
| **not as ... as** | Unequal |
| **more/fewer/less + noun** | Quantity comparison |
| **-er and -er** | Increasing change |
| **more and more** | Increasing degree |
| **the more ..., the more ...** | One change affects another |
| **much/far/a lot + comparative** | Stronger comparison |

## Key Point

The most important comparative patterns are:

> **A + be + adjective-er + than + B**

> **A + be + more + adjective + than + B**

> **A + be + less + adjective + than + B**

> **A + be + as + adjective + as + B**

> **A + be + not as + adjective + as + B**

> **The + comparative, the + comparative**

> **Comparative + and + comparative**
