# Verbs in English

## 1. What Is a Verb?

A **verb** is a word that expresses an **action, event, process, or state**.

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Action** | Shows an action | run, eat, write, speak |
| **State** | Shows a condition or situation | know, like, believe, understand |
| **Event** | Shows something that happens | happen, occur, change |
| **Process** | Shows development or change | grow, improve, develop |
| **Existence** | Shows being or existence | be, exist, remain |

### Examples

> She **runs** every morning.

> I **know** the answer.

> The weather **changed** suddenly.

> He **became** a doctor.

---

# 2. Main Types of Verbs

| Type | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Main verb** | Carries the main meaning | eat, work, sleep |
| **Auxiliary verb** | Helps form tense, questions, negatives, etc. | be, do, have |
| **Modal verb** | Expresses ability, possibility, obligation, etc. | can, should, must |
| **Linking verb** | Connects subject to information about it | be, seem, become |
| **Transitive verb** | Takes a direct object | eat, buy, open |
| **Intransitive verb** | Does not take a direct object | sleep, arrive, laugh |
| **Regular verb** | Past usually ends in -ed | work → worked |
| **Irregular verb** | Past form changes irregularly | go → went |
| **Phrasal verb** | Verb + particle | give up, look after |
| **Stative verb** | Describes a state | know, believe, love |
| **Dynamic verb** | Describes an action/activity | run, write, swim |

---

# 3. Main Verb

A **main verb** expresses the primary action or state in a sentence.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| eat | I **eat** breakfast. |
| work | She **works** here. |
| sleep | The baby **sleeps**. |
| study | They **study** English. |
| know | I **know** the answer. |
| like | He **likes** coffee. |

### Example

> She **works** at a bank.

**works** = main verb

---

# 4. Auxiliary Verbs

The three primary auxiliary verbs are:

- **be**
- **do**
- **have**

They help form different grammatical structures.

| Auxiliary | Main Uses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **be** | Continuous and passive | She **is working**. / It **was built**. |
| **do** | Questions and negatives | **Do** you work here? |
| **have** | Perfect tenses | She **has finished**. |

---

# 5. The Verb "Be"

Forms of **be**:

| Time/Form | Forms |
|---|---|
| Present | am, is, are |
| Past | was, were |
| Past participle | been |
| -ing form | being |

### Examples

> I **am** tired.

> She **is** happy.

> They **are** ready.

> He **was** late.

> We **were** busy.

---

# 6. Be as a Main Verb

"Be" can be the main verb and describe a state or identity.

### Structure

**Subject + be + complement**

> She **is** a teacher.

> He **is** tired.

> They **are** students.

> I **am** at home.

---

# 7. Be as an Auxiliary Verb

"Be" helps form:

### Continuous Tenses

> She **is working**.

> They **were studying**.

### Passive Voice

> The car **was repaired**.

> The letters **are sent** every day.

---

# 8. The Verb "Do"

Forms of **do**:

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Present | do / does |
| Past | did |
| Past participle | done |
| -ing form | doing |

### Main Uses

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Main verb | I **do** my homework. |
| Question auxiliary | **Do** you like coffee? |
| Negative auxiliary | I **do not** like coffee. |
| Emphasis | I **do** understand. |

---

# 9. The Verb "Have"

Forms of **have**:

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Present | have / has |
| Past | had |
| Past participle | had |
| -ing form | having |

### Uses

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Possession | I **have** a car. |
| Perfect tense | She **has finished**. |
| Experience | We **have visited** Japan. |
| Obligation with "have to" | I **have to** leave. |

---

# 10. Modal Verbs

Modal verbs express ideas such as:

- ability
- possibility
- permission
- obligation
- advice
- prediction
- necessity

| Modal | Main Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can | ability/possibility | I **can** swim. |
| could | past ability/polite possibility | **Could** you help me? |
| may | permission/possibility | You **may** leave. |
| might | possibility | It **might** rain. |
| must | strong obligation/certainty | You **must** study. |
| shall | suggestion/future | **Shall** we go? |
| should | advice/expectation | You **should** rest. |
| will | future/willingness | I **will** help. |
| would | polite request/hypothetical | **Would** you help me? |

---

# 11. Modal Verb Structure

After a modal verb, use the **base form** of the verb.

### Structure

**Subject + modal + base verb**

> She **can swim**.

> He **should study**.

> They **must leave**.

> I **will call** you.

### Incorrect

> ❌ She can swims.

> ❌ He should studied.

### Correct

> ✅ She can swim.

> ✅ He should study.

---

# 12. Verb Forms

Most English verbs can appear in several forms.

Using **work**:

| Form | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| work | Base form | I **work** here. |
| works | 3rd-person singular | She **works** here. |
| worked | Past simple | She **worked** yesterday. |
| working | Present participle/-ing | She is **working**. |
| worked | Past participle | She has **worked** here for years. |

Using **go**:

| Form | Name |
|---|---|
| go | Base |
| goes | 3rd-person singular |
| went | Past |
| going | -ing form |
| gone | Past participle |

---

# 13. Base Form

The **base form** is the basic form of a verb.

| Base Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| work | I work here. |
| study | They study English. |
| eat | We eat together. |
| go | I go to school. |
| write | You write well. |

The base form is also used after modal verbs.

> She can **speak** English.

> He should **study** more.

---

# 14. Third-Person Singular

In the present simple, **he, she, and it** usually require **-s/-es**.

| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| I work | work |
| You work | work |
| He works | works |
| She works | works |
| It works | works |
| We work | work |
| They work | work |

### Examples

> I **play** tennis.

> She **plays** tennis.

> They **watch** TV.

> He **watches** TV.

---

# 15. Spelling Rules for Third-Person Singular

| Verb Ending | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Most verbs | + s | work → works |
| -s | + es | pass → passes |
| -sh | + es | wash → washes |
| -ch | + es | watch → watches |
| -x | + es | fix → fixes |
| -o | + es | go → goes |
| consonant + y | y → ies | study → studies |
| vowel + y | + s | play → plays |

# 16. Regular Verbs

Regular verbs usually form the past simple and past participle with **-ed**.

| Base | Past | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| work | worked | worked |
| play | played | played |
| clean | cleaned | cleaned |
| watch | watched | watched |
| open | opened | opened |

### Example

> I **work** every day.

> I **worked** yesterday.

> I have **worked** here for five years.

---

# 17. Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs do not follow the normal **-ed** pattern.

| Base | Past | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|
| be | was/were | been |
| go | went | gone |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| see | saw | seen |
| take | took | taken |
| write | wrote | written |
| speak | spoke | spoken |
| do | did | done |
| have | had | had |
| make | made | made |
| come | came | come |
| give | gave | given |

### Example

> I **go** to school every day.

> I **went** yesterday.

> I have **gone** there before.

---

# 18. Transitive Verbs

A **transitive verb** takes a direct object.

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Object**

> She **bought a car**.

> He **opened the door**.

> I **read a book**.

| Verb | Object |
|---|---|
| buy | a car |
| open | the door |
| read | a book |
| eat | lunch |
| write | a letter |

---

# 19. Intransitive Verbs

An **intransitive verb** does not take a direct object.

> The baby **slept**.

> He **arrived**.

> She **laughed**.

> They **left**.

You cannot normally put a direct object immediately after these verbs.

> ❌ He arrived the station.

> ✅ He arrived **at the station**.

---

# 20. Verbs Can Sometimes Be Both

Some verbs can be transitive or intransitive depending on the sentence.

| Verb | Intransitive | Transitive |
|---|---|---|
| open | The door opened. | She opened the door. |
| change | The weather changed. | They changed the plan. |
| start | The movie started. | She started the car. |
| stop | The car stopped. | He stopped the car. |
| grow | The plant grew. | They grew vegetables. |

---

# 21. Linking Verbs

A **linking verb** connects the subject with information that describes or identifies it.

Common linking verbs:

| Linking Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| be | She **is** happy. |
| become | He **became** angry. |
| seem | It **seems** difficult. |
| appear | She **appears** tired. |
| look | You **look** great. |
| feel | I **feel** sick. |
| sound | That **sounds** good. |
| smell | It **smells** delicious. |
| taste | It **tastes** sweet. |
| remain | He **remained** calm. |

---

# 22. Stative Verbs

**Stative verbs** describe states rather than physical actions.

Common examples:

| Category | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Thinking | know, understand, believe, remember |
| Emotion | love, hate, like, prefer |
| Possession | own, have, belong |
| Senses | hear, smell, taste |
| Opinion | agree, disagree |
| Existence | exist, seem |

### Examples

> I **know** the answer.

> She **likes** coffee.

> This book **belongs** to me.

Stative verbs are often not normally used in continuous forms when they express a state.

> ✅ I **know** him.

> ❌ I am knowing him.

---

# 23. Dynamic Verbs

**Dynamic verbs** describe actions or activities.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| run | He is running. |
| eat | She is eating. |
| write | I am writing. |
| study | They are studying. |
| work | He is working. |
| swim | She is swimming. |

Dynamic verbs can commonly be used in continuous tenses.

---

# 24. Action Verb vs. Stative Verb

| Action/Dynamic | Stative |
|---|---|
| run | know |
| eat | believe |
| write | understand |
| work | like |
| swim | own |
| study | need |

### Compare

> She **is running**.

→ Action in progress.

> She **knows** the answer.

→ State, not normally "is knowing."

---

# 25. Phrasal Verbs

A **phrasal verb** consists of a verb plus one or more particles.

### Examples

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| give up | stop trying | Don't **give up**. |
| wake up | stop sleeping | I **wake up** at 7. |
| turn on | activate | **Turn on** the light. |
| turn off | deactivate | **Turn off** the TV. |
| look for | search for | I'm **looking for** my keys. |
| look after | take care of | She **looks after** her brother. |
| find out | discover | I **found out** the truth. |
| put off | postpone | They **put off** the meeting. |

---

# 26. Separable Phrasal Verbs

Some phrasal verbs can separate the verb and particle.

> Turn **off** the light.

> Turn the light **off**.

With a pronoun, separation is usually required.

> Turn **it off**.

Not:

> ❌ Turn off it.

---

# 27. Verb + Preposition

Some verbs are followed by a specific preposition.

| Verb + Preposition | Example |
|---|---|
| listen to | Listen **to** me. |
| wait for | I'm waiting **for** you. |
| look at | Look **at** this. |
| depend on | It depends **on** the weather. |
| believe in | I believe **in** you. |
| talk about | We talked **about** work. |
| agree with | I agree **with** you. |
| belong to | This belongs **to** me. |

---

# 28. Infinitive

The **to-infinitive** is:

**to + base verb**

| Infinitive | Example |
|---|---|
| to go | I want **to go**. |
| to study | She needs **to study**. |
| to work | He hopes **to work** abroad. |
| to learn | We want **to learn**. |

---

# 29. Bare Infinitive

A **bare infinitive** is the base verb without **to**.

It commonly occurs after modal verbs.

> You can **go**.

> She must **leave**.

> He should **study**.

It can also occur after certain verbs such as **let** and **make**.

> Let me **help**.

> The teacher made him **stay**.

---

# 30. Gerund / -ing Form

The **-ing form** can function as a noun or occur in verb structures.

### As a Gerund

> **Swimming** is good exercise.

> I enjoy **reading**.

### In Continuous Tenses

> She is **working**.

> They were **studying**.

---

# 31. Verb Patterns

Different verbs can be followed by different structures.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + to-infinitive | I want **to leave**. |
| verb + gerund | I enjoy **reading**. |
| verb + object + to-infinitive | She told me **to wait**. |
| verb + object + bare infinitive | Let me **go**. |
| verb + that-clause | I think **that he is right**. |
| verb + preposition + gerund | She is interested in **learning**. |

---

| decide | He decided **to leave**. |

# 33. Common Verbs + Gerund

These verbs are commonly followed by **-ing**.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|

# 34. Active Voice

In the **active voice**, the subject performs the action.

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Object**

> The boy **kicked** the ball.

> The company **built** the house.

| Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|
| The boy | kicked | the ball |
| She | wrote | a letter |
| They | built | the house |

---

# 35. Passive Voice

In the **passive voice**, the subject receives the action.

### Basic Structure

**Subject + be + past participle**

> The ball **was kicked** by the boy.

> The house **was built** by the company.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They built the house. | The house was built. |
| She wrote the letter. | The letter was written. |
| Someone stole the car. | The car was stolen. |

---

# 36. Verb Tenses

English has several major tense/aspect patterns.

| Tense | Basic Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | base / -s | She **works**. |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + -ing | She **is working**. |
| Present Perfect | have/has + past participle | She **has worked**. |
| Present Perfect Continuous | have/has been + -ing | She **has been working**. |
| Past Simple | past form | She **worked**. |
| Past Continuous | was/were + -ing | She **was working**. |
| Past Perfect | had + past participle | She **had worked**. |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been + -ing | She **had been working**. |
| Future Simple | will + base | She **will work**. |
| Future Continuous | will be + -ing | She **will be working**. |
| Future Perfect | will have + past participle | She **will have worked**. |
| Future Perfect Continuous | will have been + -ing | She **will have been working**. |

---

# 37. Present Simple

### Structure

**Subject + base verb**

For **he/she/it**, add **-s/-es**.

### Uses

- habits
- routines
- facts
- general truths
- permanent situations

### Examples

> I **work** every day.

> She **works** every day.

> Water **boils** at 100°C.

---

# 38. Present Continuous

### Structure

**am/is/are + verb-ing**

### Uses

- happening now
- temporary situations
- changing situations
- planned near-future events

### Examples

> I **am studying** now.

> She **is working** today.

> They **are moving** to a new city.

---

# 39. Present Perfect

### Structure

**have/has + past participle**

### Uses

- past action connected to the present
- life experience
- unfinished time
- recent events

### Examples

> I **have finished** my homework.

> She **has visited** Japan.

> They **have lived** here for five years.

---

# 40. Past Simple

### Structure

Use the past form.

### Uses

- completed past actions
- past events
- past habits

### Examples

> I **visited** Hanoi last year.

> She **went** home yesterday.

> They **played** football on Sunday.

---

# 41. Past Continuous

### Structure

**was/were + verb-ing**

### Uses

- action in progress at a past time
- background action
- interrupted action

### Examples

> I **was studying** at 8 p.m.

> She **was cooking** when I arrived.

---

# 42. Past Perfect

### Structure

**had + past participle**

Used for an action that happened **before another past action**.

> She **had left** before I arrived.

> They **had finished** dinner when we got there.

### Timeline

**Earlier past → Later past**

> She **had left** → I **arrived**.

---

# 43. Future With Will

### Structure

**will + base verb**

### Uses

- predictions
- decisions made at the moment
- promises
- offers

### Examples

> I **will help** you.

> It **will rain** tomorrow.

> I **will call** you later.

---

# 44. Be Going To

### Structure

**am/is/are + going to + base verb**

### Uses

- plans/intention
- predictions based on present evidence

### Examples

> I **am going to study** tonight.

> Look at those clouds. It **is going to rain**.

---

# 45. Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with the subject.

| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| I | work |
| You | work |
| He | works |
| She | works |
| It | works |
| We | work |
| They | work |

### Examples

> She **works** here.

> They **work** here.

---

# 46. Negative Verbs

Use auxiliary verbs to form many negative structures.

### Present Simple

**do/does + not + base verb**

> I **do not work** here.

> She **does not work** here.

### Past Simple

**did not + base verb**

> They **did not go**.

Not:

> ❌ They did not went.

---

# 47. Questions With Verbs

### Present Simple

**Do/Does + subject + base verb?**

> **Do** you work here?

> **Does** she speak English?

### Past Simple

**Did + subject + base verb?**

> **Did** you go there?

> **Did** he call you?

### With "Be"

Invert the subject and "be".

> **Are** you ready?

> **Is** she home?

> **Were** they late?

---

# 48. Do-Support

English uses **do/does/did** to form questions and negatives with most ordinary verbs in the simple present and past.

| Statement | Question | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| You work. | Do you work? | You don't work. |
| She works. | Does she work? | She doesn't work. |
| They went. | Did they go? | They didn't go. |

---

# 49. Verb + Object + Object

Some verbs can take two objects.

### Structure

**Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

> She **gave me a book**.

> He **sent her a message**.

| Verb | Indirect Object | Direct Object |
|---|---|---|
| gave | me | a book |
| sent | her | a message |
| bought | him | a gift |
| told | me | the truth |

# 51. Common Verb Errors

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| She **work** here. | She **works** here. | Third-person singular needs -s. |
| He **can goes**. | He **can go**. | Modal + base verb. |
| She **doesn't likes** coffee. | She **doesn't like** coffee. | Does + base verb. |
| They **didn't went**. | They **didn't go**. | Did + base verb. |
| I **am know** him. | I **know** him. | "Know" is normally stative. |
| He **is work** now. | He **is working** now. | Be + -ing for continuous. |
| She **has went** home. | She **has gone** home. | Have + past participle. |
| I **have saw** it. | I **have seen** it. | Have + past participle. |
| He **suggested to go**. | He **suggested going**. | Suggest is commonly followed by a gerund. |
| I **want going**. | I **want to go**. | Want + to-infinitive. |

# 54. Key Verb Rules

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| He/she/it + present simple → usually **-s/-es** | She **works**. |
| Modal + base verb | She can **work**. |
| Do/does/did + base verb | She doesn't **work**. |
| Have/has + past participle | She has **worked**. |
| Be + -ing → continuous | She is **working**. |
| Be + past participle → passive | It was **built**. |
| Verb + object → transitive | She opened **the door**. |
| No direct object → intransitive | She laughed. |
| Linking verb + complement | She looks **tired**. |
| Many stative verbs are not normally continuous | I **know** him. |
| To + base verb → infinitive | I want **to leave**. |
| Modal + base verb | You should **study**. |

## Final Summary

**Verb = action, state, event, or process.**

The most important things to learn about English verbs are:

1. **Verb forms** — base, -s, past, -ing, past participle.
2. **Verb types** — main, auxiliary, modal, linking, transitive, intransitive.
3. **Verb tenses** — present, past, and future with their aspects.
4. **Verb patterns** — infinitive, gerund, object, and complement.
5. **Subject-verb agreement** — especially **he/she/it + -s**.
6. **Auxiliary verbs** — **be, do, have**.
7. **Modal verbs** — **can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would**.
8. **Regular and irregular verbs** — worked vs. went.
9. **Active and passive voice** — performs the action vs. receives the action.
10. **Stative and dynamic verbs** — states vs. actions.
