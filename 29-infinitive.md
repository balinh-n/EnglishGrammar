# Infinitives in English

## 1. What Is an Infinitive?

An **infinitive** is the basic form of a verb, usually introduced by **to**.

### Basic Structure

> **to + base verb**

### Examples

> to eat  
> to study  
> to work  
> to learn  
> to speak

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **to + verb** | to study |
| **to + verb** | to learn |
| **to + verb** | to work |
| **to + verb** | to speak |

---

# 2. Two Main Types of Infinitives

There are two main types:

1. **To-infinitive**
2. **Bare infinitive**

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **To-infinitive** | to + base verb | I want **to learn**. |
| **Bare infinitive** | base verb without "to" | She can **swim**. |

---

# 3. To-Infinitive

The **to-infinitive** is:

> **to + base verb**

### Examples

> I want **to learn** English.

> She decided **to leave**.

> He hopes **to win**.

| Verb | To-Infinitive | Example |
|---|---|---|
| learn | to learn | I want **to learn**. |
| go | to go | She wants **to go**. |
| study | to study | He needs **to study**. |
| help | to help | They agreed **to help**. |

---

# 4. Infinitive as the Subject

An infinitive can function as the **subject** of a sentence.

### Examples

> **To learn** a language takes time.

> **To exercise** regularly is important.

> **To travel** is my dream.

| Infinitive | Example |
|---|---|
| To learn | **To learn** English is useful. |
| To exercise | **To exercise** regularly is healthy. |
| To travel | **To travel** the world is exciting. |
| To help others | **To help others** is rewarding. |

In everyday English, **it** is often used as a more natural subject:

> **It is useful to learn English.**

---

# 5. Infinitive as an Object

Some verbs are followed by a **to-infinitive**.

### Examples

> I want **to sleep**.

> She decided **to stay**.

|---|---|

| **hope** | He hopes **to succeed**. |
| **decide** | They decided **to stay**. |
| **plan** | We plan **to travel**. |
| **agree** | He agreed **to help**. |
| **promise** | She promised **to call**. |
| **refuse** | He refused **to answer**. |

# 7. Infinitive of Purpose

The infinitive can show **purpose** or explain **why** someone does something.

### Structure

> **verb + to + base verb**

### Examples

> I went to the store **to buy** some milk.

> She studies hard **to pass** the exam.

> He called me **to ask** a question.

| Purpose | Example |
|---|---|
| to buy | I went shopping **to buy** clothes. |
| to learn | She studies **to learn** English. |
| to help | He came **to help** us. |
| to save | We work hard **to save** money. |

### Question

Ask:

> **Why?**

> Why did she study?

> She studied **to pass the exam**.

# 8. Infinitive After Adjectives

We often use:

> **adjective + to-infinitive**

### Examples

> It is easy **to understand**.

> She is happy **to help**.

> He is ready **to leave**.

| Adjective | Example |
|---|---|
| easy | It is easy **to learn**. |
| difficult | It is difficult **to explain**. |
| happy | I am happy **to help**. |
| ready | She is ready **to go**. |
| excited | They are excited **to travel**. |
| afraid | He is afraid **to ask**. |
| surprised | She was surprised **to see** him. |
| sorry | I am sorry **to hear** that. |

---

# 9. Adjective + for + Object + Infinitive

We can use:

> **adjective + for + person + to-infinitive**

### Examples

> It is important **for students to study**.

> It is difficult **for me to understand**.

> It is easy **for children to learn**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| adjective + for + person + to verb | It is important **for you to practice**. |
| adjective + for + person + to verb | It is difficult **for him to decide**. |
| adjective + for + person + to verb | It is easy **for them to understand**. |

---

# 10. Infinitive After a Noun

An infinitive can describe or explain a noun.

### Examples

> I have a lot of work **to do**.

> She needs a place **to stay**.

> He has something **to tell** you.

| Noun | Infinitive | Example |
|---|---|---|
| work | to do | I have work **to do**. |
| place | to stay | We need a place **to stay**. |
| something | to say | I have something **to say**. |
| book | to read | I need a book **to read**. |
| time | to study | I need time **to study**. |

---

# 11. Too + Adjective + Infinitive

We use:

> **too + adjective + to-infinitive**

to show that something is excessive or impossible.

### Examples

> The box is **too heavy to carry**.

> He is **too tired to work**.

> The coffee is **too hot to drink**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| too + adjective + to verb | It is **too difficult to explain**. |
| too + adjective + to verb | She is **too young to drive**. |
| too + adjective + to verb | The bag is **too heavy to lift**. |

---

# 12. Adjective + Enough + Infinitive

We use:

> **adjective + enough + to-infinitive**

to show that something has a sufficient quality.

### Examples

> He is **old enough to drive**.

> The room is **large enough to hold** 50 people.

> She is **strong enough to lift** it.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| adjective + enough + to verb | He is **strong enough to help**. |
| adjective + enough + to verb | She is **old enough to vote**. |
| adjective + enough + to verb | The room is **big enough to fit** everyone. |

---

# 13. Enough + Noun + Infinitive

We can also use:

> **enough + noun + to-infinitive**

### Examples

> We have **enough time to finish**.

> She has **enough money to buy** a car.

> There is **enough food to feed** everyone.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| enough + time + to verb | We have enough time **to finish**. |
| enough + money + to verb | He has enough money **to travel**. |
| enough + food + to verb | There is enough food **to feed** everyone. |

---

# 14. Infinitive After Question Words

We can use an infinitive after:

> **what, where, when, who, how, which**

### Examples

> I don't know **what to do**.

> She showed me **how to use** the computer.

> We need to decide **where to go**.

| Question Word | Example |
|---|---|
| **what** | I don't know **what to say**. |
| **where** | She doesn't know **where to go**. |
| **when** | We don't know **when to leave**. |
| **how** | He learned **how to cook**. |
| **which** | I don't know **which to choose**. |
| **who** | She wasn't sure **who to ask**. |

---

# 15. Object + To-Infinitive

Some verbs follow this pattern:

> **verb + object + to-infinitive**

### Examples

> She told **me to wait**.

> I want **you to come**.

> He asked **her to help**.

| Verb | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **want** | want + object + to verb | I want **you to stay**. |
| **ask** | ask + object + to verb | She asked **me to help**. |
| **tell** | tell + object + to verb | He told **us to wait**. |
| **advise** | advise + object + to verb | She advised **him to rest**. |
| **invite** | invite + object + to verb | They invited **me to join**. |
| **allow** | allow + object + to verb | They allowed **us to enter**. |
| **encourage** | encourage + object + to verb | He encouraged **me to try**. |
| **remind** | remind + object + to verb | She reminded **me to call**. |

---

# 16. Bare Infinitive

A **bare infinitive** is the base form of a verb **without "to."**

### Examples

> She can **swim**.

> Let him **go**.

> I saw him **leave**.

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| After modal verbs | She can **swim**. |
| After **let** | Let me **help**. |
| After **make** | She made me **laugh**. |
| After some perception verbs | I saw him **leave**. |
| After **had better** | You had better **go**. |
| After **would rather** | I'd rather **stay**. |

---

# 17. Infinitive After Modal Verbs

Modal verbs are followed by a **bare infinitive**.

> **modal + base verb**

### Examples

> She can **swim**.

> You should **study**.

> He must **leave**.

| Modal | Example |
|---|---|
| **can** | I can **drive**. |
| **could** | She could **help**. |
| **may** | He may **come**. |
| **might** | They might **stay**. |
| **must** | You must **listen**. |
| **should** | We should **leave**. |
| **will** | I will **call**. |
| **would** | She would **help**. |

### Important

> ❌ She can **to swim**.

> ✅ She can **swim**.

---

# 18. Make, Let, and Help

### Make

> **make + object + bare infinitive**

> The teacher made us **study**.

### Let

> **let + object + bare infinitive**

> My parents let me **go**.

### Help

Help can be followed by either form:

> She helped me **to carry** the bags.

> She helped me **carry** the bags.

| Verb | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **make** | make + object + verb | He made me **laugh**. |
| **let** | let + object + verb | She let me **leave**. |
| **help** | help + object + (to) verb | He helped me **(to) clean**. |

---

# 19. Perception Verbs + Bare Infinitive

Common verbs:

> see, hear, watch, notice, feel

### Examples

> We heard her **sing**.

> She watched the children **play**.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| see | I saw him **run**. |
| hear | We heard her **sing**. |
| watch | They watched us **play**. |
| notice | I noticed him **leave**. |

# 20. Infinitive or Gerund?

Some verbs can be followed by either a **gerund** or an **infinitive**.

| Verb | Gerund | Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| like | I like **reading**. | I like **to read**. |
| love | She loves **cooking**. | She loves **to cook**. |
| hate | He hates **waiting**. | He hates **to wait**. |
| prefer | I prefer **walking**. | I prefer **to walk**. |
| start | She started **working**. | She started **to work**. |
| begin | He began **studying**. | He began **to study**. |
| continue | They continued **talking**. | They continued **to talk**. |

The meaning is often similar, but some verbs have important differences.

---

# 21. Gerund vs. Infinitive: Meaning Changes

Some verbs change meaning depending on the form used.

### Stop

> He stopped **smoking**.  
> → He quit smoking.

> He stopped **to smoke**.  
> → He stopped another activity in order to smoke.

### Remember

> I remember **meeting** her.  
> → I have a memory of meeting her.

> Remember **to meet** her.  
> → Don't forget to meet her.

### Try

> Try **restarting** the computer.  
> → Experiment with this solution.

> Try **to restart** the computer.  
> → Make an effort to restart it.

| Verb | Gerund | Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| **stop** | stop an activity | stop in order to do something |
| **remember** | remember a past event | remember to do something |
| **forget** | forget a past event | forget to do something |
| **try** | experiment with an action | make an effort |
| **regret** | feel sorry about a past action | be sorry to give information |

# 22. Negative Infinitive

The negative form is:

> **not + to + verb**

### Examples

> I decided **not to go**.

> She promised **not to tell** anyone.

> He tried **not to laugh**.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| to go | **not to go** |
| to tell | **not to tell** |
| to laugh | **not to laugh** |
| to worry | **not to worry** |

---

# 23. Perfect Infinitive

The **perfect infinitive** is:

> **to have + past participle**

It refers to an action that happened **before another time or action**.

### Examples

> She seems **to have forgotten**.

> He is believed **to have left**.

> I am happy **to have finished** the work.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| to have + V3 | She seems **to have forgotten**. |
| to have + V3 | He claims **to have seen** it. |
| to have + V3 | They are happy **to have won**. |

---

# 24. Continuous Infinitive

The continuous infinitive is:

> **to be + verb-ing**

It describes an action in progress.

### Examples

> She seems **to be sleeping**.

> He appears **to be working**.

> They are expected **to be arriving** soon.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| to be + -ing | He seems **to be sleeping**. |
| to be + -ing | She appears **to be working**. |
| to be + -ing | They seem **to be waiting**. |

---

# 25. Perfect Continuous Infinitive

The perfect continuous infinitive is:

> **to have been + verb-ing**

### Examples

> He seems **to have been working** all day.

> She appears **to have been waiting** for hours.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| to have been + -ing | He seems **to have been working**. |
| to have been + -ing | She appears **to have been waiting**. |

---

# 26. Passive Infinitive

The passive infinitive is:

> **to be + past participle**

### Examples

> I want **to be invited**.

> She expects **to be promoted**.

> He needs **to be informed**.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| to invite | **to be invited** |
| to promote | **to be promoted** |
| to inform | **to be informed** |
| to help | **to be helped** |

### Example

> Active: They expect **to hire** him.

> Passive: He expects **to be hired**.

---

# 27. Perfect Passive Infinitive

The perfect passive infinitive is:

> **to have been + past participle**

### Examples

> He is believed **to have been injured**.

> She claims **to have been cheated**.

> They are lucky **to have been chosen**.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| to have been + V3 | He is believed **to have been invited**. |
| to have been + V3 | She claims **to have been misunderstood**. |
| to have been + V3 | They are happy **to have been selected**. |

# 30. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| I want **going** home. | I want **to go** home. | Want is followed by a to-infinitive. |
| She decided **going**. | She decided **to go**. | Decide is followed by a to-infinitive. |
| He can **to swim**. | He can **swim**. | Modal verbs use the bare infinitive. |
| She made me **to laugh**. | She made me **laugh**. | Make + object takes the bare infinitive. |
| Let me **to help** you. | Let me **help** you. | Let + object takes the bare infinitive. |
| I look forward to **meet** you. | I look forward to **meeting** you. | Here, "to" is a preposition. |
| He told me **go**. | He told me **to go**. | Tell + object + to-infinitive. |

---

# 31. How to Identify an Infinitive

Ask:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| Does it use **to + base verb**? | **to study** |
| Does it express purpose? | I went there **to study**. |
| Does it follow certain verbs? | I want **to study**. |
| Does it follow an adjective? | It is easy **to study**. |
| Does it follow a modal without "to"? | I can **study**. |

### Example

> She went to the library **to study**.

1. **to study** = to + base verb.
2. It explains the **purpose** of going.
3. Therefore, **to study** is a **to-infinitive**.

---

# 32. Infinitive in One Sentence

Consider:

> She decided **to go** to the library **to study** English.

| Infinitive | Function |
|---|---|
| **to go** | Object/complement after "decided" |
| **to study** | Shows purpose |

# 34. Easy Way to Remember

| Infinitive | Think of It As |
|---|---|
| **to study** | An action you want/plan/need to do |
| **to learn** | A purpose or goal |
| **to help** | An action after certain verbs |
| **to be** | A state or condition |
| **to have done** | An earlier action |
| **to be doing** | An action in progress |
| **to be done** | A passive action |

### Main Patterns

> **After a verb:**  
> I want **to learn**.

> **For purpose:**  
> I study **to improve** my English.

> **After an adjective:**  
> It is easy **to understand**.

> **After a noun:**  
> I have something **to say**.

> **After a modal:**  
> She can **swim**.

> **Negative:**  
> He decided **not to go**.

> **Passive:**  
> She wants **to be invited**.

## Key Point

An **infinitive** is usually **to + the base form of a verb**.

> **to + verb**

Examples:

> **to study**

> **to work**

> **to learn**

> **to speak**

But after modal verbs such as **can, could, may, might, must, should, will,** and **would**, we normally use the **bare infinitive**:

> She can **swim**.

> You should **study**.

> He will **come**.
