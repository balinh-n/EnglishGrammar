# Conjunctions in English

## 1. What Is a Conjunction?

A **conjunction** is a word that connects **words, phrases, or clauses**.

Conjunctions help us express relationships between ideas, such as:

- Addition
- Contrast
- Choice
- Reason
- Result
- Condition
- Time
- Purpose

English has three main types of conjunctions:

1. Coordinating conjunctions
2. Subordinating conjunctions
3. Correlative conjunctions

---

# 2. Summary of Conjunctions

| Type | Function | Common Examples | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coordinating** | Connects equal words, phrases, or clauses | and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor | I was tired, **but** I worked. |
| **Subordinating** | Connects a dependent clause to a main clause | because, although, if, when, while, before, after | I stayed home **because** it was raining. |
| **Correlative** | Uses pairs of conjunctions | both...and, either...or, neither...nor | **Both** Tom **and** Mary came. |

---

# 3. Coordinating Conjunctions

Coordinating conjunctions connect **equal grammatical elements**.

The seven main coordinating conjunctions are remembered by:

> **FANBOYS**

- **F** = for
- **A** = and
- **N** = nor
- **B** = but
- **O** = or
- **Y** = yet
- **S** = so

---

# 4. "And"

**And** shows **addition** or connects similar ideas.

### Examples

> I like tea **and** coffee.

> She opened the door **and** walked inside.

> Tom is smart **and** hardworking.

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Add things | I bought bread **and** milk. |
| Add actions | She came **and** sat down. |
| Connect ideas | He is kind **and** helpful. |

---

# 5. "But"

**But** shows **contrast** between two ideas.

### Examples

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

> She is young, **but** she is very experienced.

> He studied hard, **but** he failed the test.

| First Idea | Contrast |
|---|---|
| I was tired | **but** I continued working. |
| It was raining | **but** we went outside. |
| He is rich | **but** he isn't happy. |

---

# 6. "Or"

**Or** shows a **choice or alternative**.

### Examples

> Do you want tea **or** coffee?

> You can stay here **or** come with us.

> Hurry up, **or** you'll miss the bus.

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Choice | Tea **or** coffee? |
| Alternative | Call me **or** send me a message. |
| Warning | Hurry up, **or** you'll be late. |

---

# 7. "So"

**So** shows a **result or consequence**.

### Examples

> I was tired, **so** I went to bed.

> It was raining, **so** we stayed home.

> She studied hard, **so** she passed the exam.

| Cause | Result |
|---|---|
| I was hungry | **so** I ate. |
| It was cold | **so** I stayed inside. |
| He studied hard | **so** he passed. |

---

# 8. "Yet"

**Yet** shows **contrast**, similar to **but**.

It is often used when something is surprising or unexpected.

### Examples

> He is young, **yet** he is very wise.

> She studied hard, **yet** she failed.

> It was expensive, **yet** I bought it.

---

# 9. "For"

**For** can be used as a coordinating conjunction to give a **reason or explanation**.

It is more formal and less common in everyday speech.

### Examples

> I went to bed, **for** I was tired.

> He stayed home, **for** he was sick.

In everyday English, **because** is usually more natural.

> I went to bed **because** I was tired.

---

# 10. "Nor"

**Nor** connects negative alternatives.

It is often used after **neither** or another negative idea.

### Examples

> She doesn't drink coffee, **nor** does she drink tea.

> He can neither read **nor** write.

> I don't know him, **nor** do I know his brother.

---

# 11. FANBOYS Summary

| Conjunction | Meaning / Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **For** | Reason | I stayed home, **for** I was sick. |
| **And** | Addition | I bought bread **and** milk. |
| **Nor** | Negative alternative | He doesn't smoke, **nor** does he drink. |
| **But** | Contrast | I was tired, **but** I worked. |
| **Or** | Choice / alternative | Do you want tea **or** coffee? |
| **Yet** | Unexpected contrast | He is young, **yet** very wise. |
| **So** | Result | It was raining, **so** we stayed home. |

---

# 12. Subordinating Conjunctions

A **subordinating conjunction** connects a **dependent clause** to a **main clause**.

Common subordinating conjunctions include:

> because, although, if, unless, when, while, before, after, since, until, as, whereas

### Example

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

The clause **because it was raining** cannot normally stand alone as a complete sentence.

---

# 13. Subordinating Conjunctions by Function

| Function | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Reason** | because, since, as | I stayed home **because** I was sick. |
| **Contrast** | although, though, even though, whereas | **Although** it was raining, we went out. |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided that | **If** you study, you will pass. |
| **Time** | when, while, before, after, until, since | Call me **when** you arrive. |
| **Purpose** | so that, in order that | Study hard **so that** you can pass. |
| **Result** | so...that, such...that | It was **so** cold **that** we stayed inside. |
| **Comparison** | than, as...as | She is taller **than** I am. |
| **Place** | where, wherever | Sit **wherever** you like. |

---

# 14. Conjunctions of Reason

Common conjunctions:

> because, since, as

They explain **why something happens**.

### Examples

> I stayed home **because** I was sick.

> **Since** it was raining, we stayed inside.

> **As** I was tired, I went to bed.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **because** | I left **because** I was tired. |
| **since** | **Since** it was late, we went home. |
| **as** | **As** she was sick, she stayed home. |

---

# 15. Conjunctions of Contrast

Common conjunctions:

> although, though, even though, whereas, while

### Examples

> **Although** it was raining, we went outside.

> **Even though** he was tired, he continued working.

> John likes coffee, **whereas** Mary prefers tea.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **although** | **Although** it was difficult, I tried. |
| **though** | **Though** he was tired, he worked. |
| **even though** | **Even though** it rained, we played. |
| **whereas** | Tom likes tea, **whereas** Jane likes coffee. |

---

# 16. Conjunctions of Condition

Condition conjunctions introduce a condition.

Common examples:

> if, unless, provided that, as long as

### Examples

> **If** you study, you will pass.

> **Unless** you hurry, you'll be late.

> You can go **as long as** you finish your work.

| Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **if** | A condition | **If** it rains, we will stay home. |
| **unless** | If not | **Unless** you hurry, you'll be late. |
| **provided that** | Only if | You can come **provided that** you finish. |
| **as long as** | On the condition that | You can stay **as long as** you are quiet. |

---

# 17. Conjunctions of Time

Time conjunctions show **when something happens**.

Common examples:

> when, while, before, after, until, since, as soon as

### Examples

> Call me **when** you arrive.

> I listened to music **while** I was studying.

> Wash your hands **before** you eat.

> We went home **after** the movie ended.

| Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|
| **when** | Call me **when** you arrive. |
| **while** | She cooked **while** I cleaned. |
| **before** | Wash your hands **before** you eat. |
| **after** | We left **after** dinner. |
| **until** | Wait here **until** I return. |
| **since** | I haven't seen him **since** he left. |
| **as soon as** | Call me **as soon as** you arrive. |

---

# 18. Conjunctions of Purpose

Purpose conjunctions explain **why an action is done**.

Common examples:

> so that, in order that

### Examples

> I studied hard **so that** I could pass.

> She spoke slowly **so that** everyone could understand.

> He saved money **in order that** he could buy a car.

---

# 19. Correlative Conjunctions

**Correlative conjunctions** work in pairs.

Common pairs include:

- both...and
- either...or
- neither...nor
- not only...but also
- whether...or

---

# 20. "Both...and"

**Both...and** connects two positive or similar ideas.

### Examples

> **Both** Tom **and** Mary are students.

> She speaks **both** English **and** French.

> He is **both** smart **and** hardworking.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| both + noun + and + noun | **Both** Tom **and** Mary came. |
| both + adjective + and + adjective | She is **both** kind **and** patient. |

---

# 21. "Either...or"

**Either...or** presents two alternatives or choices.

### Examples

> You can **either** stay **or** leave.

> **Either** John **or** Peter will help us.

> We can go **either** today **or** tomorrow.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| either + noun + or + noun | **Either** tea **or** coffee. |
| either + verb + or + verb | You can **either stay or leave**. |

---

# 22. "Neither...nor"

**Neither...nor** means **not one and not the other**.

### Examples

> **Neither** Tom **nor** Mary came.

> I like **neither** coffee **nor** tea.

> She can **neither** swim **nor** drive.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Both Tom and Mary came. | **Neither** Tom **nor** Mary came. |
| I like tea and coffee. | I like **neither** tea **nor** coffee. |

---

# 23. "Not Only...but Also"

This structure emphasizes **two positive ideas**.

### Examples

> She is **not only** smart **but also** hardworking.

> He speaks **not only** English **but also** French.

> The movie was **not only** funny **but also** educational.

---

# 24. "Whether...or"

**Whether...or** shows two possibilities or alternatives.

### Examples

> I don't know **whether** he will come **or** not.

> Tell me **whether** you want tea **or** coffee.

> **Whether** you like it **or** not, you must finish it.

---

# 25. Conjunctions with Clauses

A **clause** contains a subject and a verb.

Conjunctions can connect clauses.

### Example

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

The sentence contains two clauses:

1. I was tired.
2. I continued working.

**But** connects them.

| Clause 1 | Conjunction | Clause 2 |
|---|---|---|
| I was tired | **but** | I continued working. |
| It was raining | **so** | we stayed home. |
| I stayed home | **because** | I was sick. |

---

# 26. Commas with Conjunctions

When a coordinating conjunction connects two complete independent clauses, a comma is commonly used before the conjunction.

### Examples

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

> It was raining, **so** we stayed home.

> She studied hard, **and** she passed the exam.

For short sentences or when connecting words rather than complete clauses, a comma is usually unnecessary.

> I bought apples **and** oranges.

> She is smart **and** kind.

---

# 27. Conjunctions in One Sentence

Consider this sentence:

> **Although** it was raining, Tom went to school **because** he had an important test, **and** he wanted to pass.

| Conjunction | Type | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Although** | Subordinating | Shows contrast |
| **because** | Subordinating | Shows reason |
| **and** | Coordinating | Adds information |

---

# 28. Conjunctions in Everyday English

| Situation | Conjunction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add information | **and** | I bought bread **and** milk. |
| Show contrast | **but** | I was tired, **but** I worked. |
| Give a choice | **or** | Tea **or** coffee? |
| Show a result | **so** | It was late, **so** I went home. |
| Give a reason | **because** | I left **because** I was tired. |
| Give a condition | **if** | **If** you study, you'll pass. |
| Show time | **when** | Call me **when** you arrive. |
| Show contrast | **although** | **Although** it was hard, I tried. |
| Show purpose | **so that** | I studied **so that** I could pass. |

# 30. Easy Way to Remember

| Type | Think About | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordinating** | Connects equal ideas | and, but, or, so |
| **Subordinating** | Shows relationship between main and dependent clauses | because, if, when, although |
| **Correlative** | Works in pairs | both...and, either...or |

### FANBOYS

> **F** = for → reason  
> **A** = and → addition  
> **N** = nor → negative alternative  
> **B** = but → contrast  
> **O** = or → choice  
> **Y** = yet → contrast  
> **S** = so → result

## Key Point

Conjunctions **connect ideas** and show the relationship between them.

The most important patterns are:

> **and** → addition  
> **but** → contrast  
> **or** → choice  
> **so** → result  
> **because** → reason  
> **if** → condition  
> **when** → time  
> **although** → contrast  
> **both...and** → two positive ideas  
> **either...or** → alternatives  
> **neither...nor** → two negative alternatives
